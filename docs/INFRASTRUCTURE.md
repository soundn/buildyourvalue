# Move the edge to Cloudflare, not the data

Build Your Value should run a mix, not either pure stack: Cloudflare Workers (via OpenNext) for the two Next.js apps and the hot API paths, Supabase Postgres and Supabase Auth in London reached through Hyperdrive, Amazon SES for the digest once volume passes roughly 100,000 users, and Cloudflare's own data and email products nowhere near the critical path. The all-Cloudflare stack fails on two layers that matter most for this product: Cloudflare sells no consumer identity (Access is $7 per workforce seat), so it needs Clerk at **$0.02 per user, about $299,000 a month at 15 million MAU**, and Cloudflare Email Service is still a beta with a 50-recipient cap, unpublished daily quotas and no webhooks. The Vercel + Supabase + Resend stack works fine to about 100,000 users at under $800 a month, but from one million users Vercel's per-request and per-gigabyte meters make it **5 to 19 times** the Workers bill and Resend's overage runs **3 to 6 times** SES. Before any of that matters, the founders should recalibrate the ambition: Jobberman has **2.5 to 2.6 million registered jobseekers after 16 years**, LinkedIn reaches about 13 million Nigerians, and no Nigerian product outside daily-use fintech wallets has ever passed 10 million; a two-year target of 1.5 to 2.5 million would already make this the country's largest job platform. The cost model therefore treats 10 million as the design ceiling the architecture must not preclude and 50 million as a planning band, and under its stated assumptions the recommended mix costs about **$50, $330, $2,600, $24,000 and $107,000 a month** at 1,000, 100,000, 1 million, 10 million and 50 million registered users, against $85, $765, $4,200 to 5,000, $33,000 to 45,000 and $148,000 to 224,000 for Vercel + Supabase + Resend. One product fact shrinks the hardest technical problem: matching only needs each active user's vector compared against roughly 200,000 live job vectors, an index under 2 GB at every tier, so the 50-million-vector pgvector problem exists only if employer-to-candidate reverse search is built. The decisions this changes are ADR-0008 (web hosting moves to Workers between 100,000 and 300,000 users; the API splits into Workers hot paths and a Node job runner) and ADR-0005 (SES takes the digest stream between 100,000 and 500,000 users); ADR-0007 is confirmed and should be amended to name the forward/reverse distinction.

## Two million in two years would be a record; 50 million has no precedent in Nigeria

The 10-to-50-million ambition is not supported by any comparable. Jobberman, the market leader, reported **329,432 new jobseekers in all of 2025** ([Jobberman 2025 Wrapped](https://www.jobberman.com/discover/2025-wrapped-our-winning-year-at-jobberman-nigeria)) on a base its boilerplate describes as "over 2.5 million job applicants and 60,000 employers" ([Citi Newsroom](https://www.citinewsroom.com/2025/09/jobberman-celebrates-13-years-of-building-businesses-and-shaping-careers-in-ghana/)); a 2026 third-party review puts it at 2.6 million registered with **about 606,000 active candidates**, a 23% active ratio ([Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/jobberman-review-2026-is-jobberman-legit-or-a-scam/)). Ngcareers took roughly ten years to reach one million before Jobberman acquired it ([Technext](https://technext.ng/2020/09/04/job-recruitment-platform-jobberman-acquires-top-competitor-ngcareers-to-expand-operation-in-nigeria/)), and Jobberman's CEO has said plainly that "it is difficult getting from zero to your first one million users" ([How We Made It In Africa](https://www.howwemadeitinafrica.com/p/the-ins-and-outs-of-running-an-online-jobs-platform-in-nigeria)). The ceiling is set by the addressable professional population, not by internet access: Nigeria has **109 million internet users** ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nigeria)) and a labour force of about **89 million** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/companies/article/here-are-major-trends-in-nigerias-unemployment/)), yet LinkedIn, with a global brand and two decades of operation, reaches only about **12.9 million** of them by ad-audience estimate ([NapoleonCat](https://stats.napoleoncat.com/linkedin-users-in-nigeria/2025/12/)). Fifty million registered users would be 56% of the entire labour force.

The only Nigerian consumer products above 10 million are payment wallets with agent networks and cash incentives: OPay claims **45 million users** after roughly seven years with 39 million monthly transacting ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/05/30/opay-hits-45-million-users-expands-merchant-network-to-over-1-million-nationwide/)), and PalmPay went from 100,000 users in 2019 to 35 million by 2025 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/09/29/nigerias-top-10-most-downloaded-fintech-apps-in-q3-2025/)). Those are daily-use products backed by hundreds of millions of dollars; job search is used once or twice a year, which is why practitioner guidance says job boards should measure "resurrection" rather than retention and that "your best users are supposed to leave" ([Cavuno](https://cavuno.com/blog/job-board-retention)). The fastest job-platform growth on record is Apna in India, which went from 1.2 million users in August 2020 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-08-31/jobs-app-for-poor-indian-workers-gets-8-million-from-investors)) to **more than 10 million by June 2021** with Tiger Global money ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/31/tiger-global-in-talks-to-make-apna-indias-fastest-unicorn)), in a market with roughly six times Nigeria's labour force. Scaling Apna's curve down by that market ratio gives 1.5 to 2.5 million Nigerian registrations in two years, which is the honest planning target. Companies that do operate job platforms at 10 million or more users carry four-digit headcounts: Apna about 1,600 to 2,100 ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/apna.co); [Revelio](https://www.reveliolabs.com/companies/apnatime-tech/employees)) and the Jobberman group about 1,600 ([Revelio](https://www.reveliolabs.com/companies/jobberman/employees)), and Indeed removes 100,000 to 500,000 fraudulent accounts per half-year with a dedicated trust-and-safety team ([Indeed H2 2025 transparency report](https://www.indeed.com/legal/transparency-report-hub/h22025-indeed-transparency-report)). Two founders will hit the review-and-support wall long before any database ceiling.

The cost model nevertheless runs all five tiers, because the question is which architecture must not be precluded, and it uses one consistent set of activity assumptions. These are modelling choices, not measurements; no job platform publishes them, and Jobberman's measured 23% active ratio suggests the 30% MAU figure is generous, so the costs lean high.

| Assumption | Value used in every table below |
|---|---|
| Monthly active users | 30% of registered (Jobberman measures about 23%; 20% is the low case and roughly scales every usage line by two-thirds) |
| Web traffic | 20 page views per active user per week; each page view is 1 Next.js render, 2 API calls, 5 static assets and 150 KB |
| Email | 13 digests a month to each active user plus 2 transactional emails per registered user: **5.9 emails per registered user per month**; digest-day peak is 30% of registered |
| Relational data | 35 KB per registered user in Postgres (profile, embedding, applications, events) |
| CV files | 200 KB per user in object storage |
| Job corpus | 300 listings a day retained two years, about **220,000 live job embeddings** at 1,024 dimensions; matching is the forward query (user vector against the job index), one run per active user per week |
| Compute sizing | Supabase instance chosen by its published recommended maximum database size; PITR add-on from 100,000 users; Team plan from 10 million; no replicas, standby, staging or observability spend at 10 million and above |
| Phone OTP | ₦7 per SMS, 0.3 to 1.0 OTP per active user per month, identical across stacks and shown separately |
| Excluded | Claude tokens, embedding tokens, job-data feeds, WhatsApp, staff, Enterprise discounts |

## Hosting: Workers is 5 to 19 times cheaper from one million users, and Lagos is the reason to move early

Cloudflare has served Nigeria from a Lagos facility since October 2018, colocated at MDXI Lekki and peering at IXPN and WAF-IX so that content is "delivered locally from Lagos" instead of from London or Lisbon ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/lagos)). Vercel has no Nigerian point of presence; its only African compute region is Cape Town (cpt1, AWS af-south-1), and its edge network lists no Lagos or Johannesburg PoP ([Vercel regions](https://vercel.com/docs/edge-network/regions)). Cape Town is also Vercel's most expensive region in this comparison: **$0.28 per GB** beyond 1 TB and **$2.80 per million** edge requests beyond 10 million ([Vercel cpt1 pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/pricing/regional-pricing/cpt1)) against $0.15 per GB and $2.60 per million in Frankfurt ([Vercel fra1 pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/pricing/regional-pricing/fra1)), and active CPU in cpt1 is $0.200 per hour against $0.128 in US regions ([Vercel Fluid compute pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/fluid-compute/pricing)). Because every request touches a London database, the sensible Vercel region is Europe, which means every Lagos request crosses an undersea cable twice before it reaches a function. Workers run in the Lagos PoP by default and Smart Placement can move the API Worker next to the database when that is measurably faster ([Smart Placement](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/smart-placement/)). No Lagos latency measurement for either platform was found; the architectural asymmetry is the finding.

The cost asymmetry is structural. Workers Paid is **$5 a month with 10 million requests and 30 million CPU-milliseconds included, then $0.30 per million requests and $0.02 per million CPU-ms**; static asset requests are free and unlimited, there is no bandwidth meter and subrequests are not billed ([Workers pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)). Vercel Pro is $20 per seat with $20 of credit, 1 TB of transfer and 10 million edge requests included, after which both the request and the gigabyte meters scale linearly with page views with no volume tiering below Enterprise ([Makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/md/saas/vercel-cost)). Under the traffic assumptions above, Vercel Pro in Frankfurt lands at roughly **$40, $85, $1,190, $13,300 and $67,100** a month across the five tiers if it serves everything, or about $40, $60, $470, $6,000 and $30,600 if Cloudflare's free proxy absorbs the static assets in front of it (that variant is the topology ADR-0008 already chose, but Vercel's billing treatment of a third-party proxy could not be verified). The all-Cloudflare Workers stack, including a KV cache for Next.js ISR, 10%-sampled Workers Logs, a Pro zone from one million users and Browser Rendering for PDF export, lands at roughly **$5, $8 to 33, $85 to 90, $700 and $3,600 to 3,700**. The ratio at one million users is 13x against Vercel alone or 5x against Vercel behind Cloudflare, and 8 to 19x at 10 and 50 million. Doubling page weight adds about $5,700 a month on Vercel at 10 million and nothing on Cloudflare; the single biggest Cloudflare swing is unsampled logging, which would add about $1,400 at 10 million, so sampling or Logpush is a day-one configuration. Independent models agree on direction: Makerkit's preset small-SaaS workload costs about $60 on Vercel Pro versus $5.04 on Workers ([Makerkit calculator](https://makerkit.dev/pricing-calculator/vercel-vs-cloudflare)), and Vercel's own bill-shock history includes Cara's roughly **$96,000 for one viral week** ([CostGoat](https://costgoat.com/bill-shock/stories/vercel-cara-96k-week-bandwidth)). Vercel's Spend Management can pause production at a cap, but its checks "are not continuous" and visitors then see a 503 ([Vercel Spend Management](https://vercel.com/docs/spend-management)), which for a Nigerian audience paying ₦431 to ₦800 per gigabyte of data ([Abokiforex](https://abokiforex.app/show-news/1724381-nigerians-spend-n66tn-internet-data-year-consumption-hits-record-high); [AndroidPols](https://www.androidpols.com.ng/2026/01/1gb-data-price-nigeria-2026-updated.html)) is a worse failure mode than a larger bill. Region-flat egress also matters more for Africa than for the US: metered CDNs charge Africa 30 to 230% more per gigabyte, with CloudFront at $0.14 per GB there, and "only Cloudflare's unmetered model is region-flat" ([egresscost.com](https://egresscost.com/cdn-pricing)).

Capability is where Vercel still earns its premium, and the gap has narrowed enough to make the move safe but not free. Next.js runs on Workers through `@opennextjs/cloudflare`, which "currently only supports the Node runtime of Next.js" ([OpenNext Cloudflare docs](https://opennext.js.org/cloudflare)); Cloudflare's current guide lists ISR, middleware (except Node.js middleware from Next.js 15.2), server actions, PPR and `use cache` as supported, with image optimisation "through Cloudflare Images" ([Cloudflare Next.js guide](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/framework-guides/web-apps/nextjs)). That support table changed three times in eighteen months, and as late as December 2025 the OpenNext docs listed Next.js 15 as the only officially supported version ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1pex20t/snapshots/2025-12-06T08%3A30%3A34.415989Z)), so the founders should deploy the real app to a preview Worker before relying on any of it. Cloudflare's own Vite-based reimplementation, vinext 1.0, claims "more than 99%" test compatibility excluding cache components ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/vinext-nextjs-on-vite/)), but independent analysis still calls OpenNext "the safer choice for many teams today" ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/vinext-cloudflares-vite-based-next-js-replacement/)). Next.js 16.2 stabilised the Build Adapters API that OpenNext and other non-Vercel hosts build on ([Makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/md/tutorials/nextjs-16)), which makes the exit from Vercel a plumbing change rather than a rewrite. Hard limits on Workers are **128 MB of memory per isolate** and a 1-second startup budget on every plan, 10 ms of CPU per request on Free and 30 seconds (configurable to 5 minutes) on Paid ([Workers limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)); the historical 3 MB/10 MB bundle cap that blocked Next.js on the free plan was reportedly replaced on 4 September 2026 by a single **64 MiB uncompressed limit** on all plans ([Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/cloudflare-workers-64mib-size-limit/)), which if confirmed makes the admin console a free-plan deployment. Use Workers with Static Assets rather than Pages: Cloudflare's own migration guide and Workers' "distinctly broader set of features" make Pages a maintenance target ([Cloudflare migrate from Pages](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/static-assets/migrate-from-pages/)).

The Hono API is portable both ways, since Hono exports a default handler on Workers and runs on Node through `@hono/node-server` ([node-server README](https://github.com/miyamonz/node-server)), but three API dependencies decide where it lives. With `nodejs_compat` Workers provide Buffer, streams and a virtual `node:fs`, but `node:child_process` "is a non-functional stub" ([Cloudflare Node.js compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/nodejs-compatibility/)), so stock Playwright cannot run; PDF rendering means Cloudflare Browser Rendering with the `@cloudflare/playwright` fork ([cloudflare/playwright](https://github.com/cloudflare/playwright)) at **$0.09 per browser-hour beyond 10 included hours and $2 per concurrent browser beyond 10** ([Browser Rendering pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/browser-rendering/pricing/)). That concurrency cap is the operational catch: a digest landing in 300,000 inboxes on the same morning will produce PDF bursts well above ten concurrent renders, so either a queue sits in front or PDFs render on a small Node service. The `docx` package and the Anthropic SDK's streaming should work on Workers with `nodejs_compat` since both are built on Web APIs and JSZip, but no source confirmed either; test both in `wrangler dev` before committing. The clean split is therefore Workers for request-response paths (the two Next.js apps, auth-gated reads, listing pages, the match endpoint) and a single Node process on Fly.io or Railway for the long-running work the product needs anyway: digest rendering and sending, ingestion, Playwright PDFs, `docx` export and the pgmq consumers from ADR-0006. The Fly.io line was not priced in this research, so the hosting rows below assume the API runs as Workers or Vercel functions; a small Fly machine adds a fixed tens-of-dollars line at every tier.

Operationally, both platforms give git previews and rollbacks, but Vercel's are turnkey for Next.js while Cloudflare's come through Wrangler, Workers Builds or GitHub Actions ([freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-deploy-a-full-stack-next-js-app-on-cloudflare-workers-with-github-actions-ci-cd/)). Security tooling favours Cloudflare: DDoS mitigation is free on both and Vercel does not bill blocked traffic ([Vercel DDoS mitigation](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-firewall/ddos-mitigation)), but Vercel's WAF custom rules "incur usage for every challenged or denied request" and its OWASP managed rules and rate limiting are Enterprise meters ([Vercel WAF pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-waf/usage-and-pricing)), whereas Cloudflare bundles WAF and rate limiting into its zone plans and Turnstile is free with "unlimited challenges" ([Turnstile plans](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)). In practice the Vercel option needs Cloudflare in front anyway for Lagos and for bot defence, which is why running two hosting vendors to get one PoP is the weaker position. Lock-in is symmetric but different in kind: Vercel locks Next.js runtime semantics (ISR, `use cache`, Image Optimization, Fluid), Cloudflare locks storage bindings (KV, Durable Objects, R2, Queues) and the Workers runtime; keeping bindings behind interfaces in `api/` keeps either exit to days of work for apps this size.

## Data and identity: stay on Postgres, because the forward match is tiny and Supabase Auth, not storage, is the bill

Supabase remains plain AWS-hosted Postgres with published linear pricing up to a **16XL instance of 64 vCPU and 256 GB RAM at about $3,730 a month**, with 500 direct connections and 12,000 pooler clients, and "Contact Us" above that ([Supabase compute and disk](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk)). Its Pro plan is $25 with 100,000 monthly active users included and then **$0.00325 per MAU**, 8 GB of disk then $0.125 per GB, 250 GB of egress then $0.09 per GB, and 100 GB of file storage then $0.0213 per GB ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)); PITR costs $100 per seven days of retention with a two-minute recovery point ([Supabase backups](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/backups)). Read replicas run at the primary's compute size plus 1.25x its disk, serve only REST GETs and direct SQL, and Supabase's own guidance is that they "become the right choice after you reach the largest compute size of 16XL or earlier if you need workload isolation" ([Supabase read replicas](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/read-replicas)). There is no managed sharding; Multigres, Supabase's Vitess-style layer, shipped a single-shard alpha in June 2026 with horizontal sharding "planned" ([Supabase blog](https://supabase.com/blog/multigres-vitess-for-postgres)). Under the 35 KB-per-user assumption, operational data is about 33 GB at one million users, 334 GB at 10 million and 1.7 TB at 50 million, which Supabase's own sizing table places on an XL, a 4XL and a 16XL respectively; the 50-million row is inside the published envelope but beyond anything Supabase has documented a customer running on one project. The published scaling pattern in the industry agrees on the order of failures: connection exhaustion, then read load, then the vector index outgrowing RAM, then single-writer limits, which is when Figma moved to "a dozen vertically partitioned databases" and then sharding ([Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-figmas-databases-team-lived-to-tell-the-scale/)). This model places that last step at the 10-to-50-million band, after the realistic horizon.

The pgvector sizing changes entirely once the matching direction is stated. Supabase's own benchmarks run to one million 1,536-dimension vectors per database, using 32 to 40 GB of RAM on a 2XL to 16XL at 0.99 recall ([Supabase compute add-on guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/ai/choosing-compute-addon)), and its scaling guidance is to split vector collections into separate "pod" projects because "vectors typically grow faster than operational data" ([Supabase engineering for scale](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/ai/engineering-for-scale)). A general guide puts HNSW capacity at roughly 2 to 3 million 1,536-dimension vectors in 16 GB and 16 to 25 million in 128 GB ([Tiger Data](https://dev.to/tigerdata_dev/the-postgres-developers-guide-to-vector-index-tradeoffs-o3h)), and sharding an HNSW graph across nodes degrades it badly, with cross-node traversals making up "over 80% of the total search steps" in a five-node split ([arXiv 2512.17264](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17264)). Applied to 50 million user profiles at 1,024 dimensions, that is about 205 GB of raw float32 or 102 GB in `halfvec`, and 1.2 to 2x that for the index, which exceeds a 16XL. But the product does not need that index. The digest is a forward query: each active user's embedding is compared against the live job corpus, which at 300 listings a day with two-year retention is **about 220,000 vectors, under 2 GB with its HNSW index**, and runs on a Medium or Large Supabase instance at every user tier. The 7-to-35-million-vector profile index is only required for reverse search (an employer or recruiter querying all candidates), which is not in the current product. ADR-0007's choice of `voyage-4-lite` at 1,024 dimensions in `halfvec` is therefore confirmed, its "corpus approaching five million vectors" revisit trigger will not fire on the job corpus at any tier, and the ADR should be amended to say so explicitly and to name reverse search as the event that triggers it (at which point the options are halfvec on an 8XL-plus primary, Supabase vector pods, or Vectorize). Supabase Postgres also keeps the pg_dump exit: Supabase documents the three-dump migration procedure itself, with friction concentrated in the `auth` and `storage` schemas, Vault keys and Storage objects ([Supabase backup and restore](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/migrating-within-supabase/backup-restore)), and Auth exports with bcrypt hashes and phone numbers that WorkOS and PropelAuth import directly ([WorkOS](https://workos.com/docs/migrate/supabase)).

Cloudflare's data products are cheap and egress-free but every one has a ceiling that forces application-level sharding at this scale, and none offers SQL vector search or consumer identity. D1 is SQLite capped at **10 GB per database and 1 TB per account** on Workers Paid ([D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/)), priced at $0.75 per GB-month beyond 5 GB with 25 billion rows read and 50 million written included ([D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/)); at 35 KB per user that is about 285,000 users per database, so 35 databases at 10 million and 175 at 50 million, with every cross-user query (admin analytics, employer search) written by hand in Workers. Durable Objects give one 10 GB SQLite store per object with unlimited objects ([Durable Objects limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/)) at $0.20 per GB-month ([Durable Objects pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)), a natural per-user profile store with no cross-user query capability at all. Vectorize caps indexes at **20 million vectors and 1,536 dimensions** with a topK of 100 ([Vectorize limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/vectorize/platform/limits/)), priced at $0.01 per million queried dimensions and $0.05 per 100 million stored ([Vectorize pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/vectorize/platform/pricing/)); it would serve the forward match against 220,000 jobs for a few dollars a month, and a reverse index of 50 million users would need three indexes merged client-side. R2 is $0.015 per GB-month with free egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)), which makes it the right home for CV files on any stack (9.5 TB at 50 million is about $143 a month). Hyperdrive, the piece that makes the mix work, is included in Workers Paid at no extra charge ([Hyperdrive pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/platform/pricing/)), supports about 100 origin connections per configuration ([Hyperdrive limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/platform/limits/)), and Cloudflare's own Supabase guide says to use the direct connection string with a Postgres driver rather than the pooled string or supabase-js ([Hyperdrive with Supabase](https://developers.cloudflare.com/hyperdrive/examples/connect-to-postgres/postgres-database-providers/supabase/)). Putting Workers in front of the existing Supabase project is additive, not a migration.

Identity is where the stacks diverge most, and it is the dominant cost line at scale on every route. Cloudflare Access is workforce Zero Trust, free to 50 users and then $7 per user per month ([Cloudflare Zero Trust plans](https://www.cloudflare.com/plans/zero-trust-services/)); Cloudflare sells nothing for consumer login. The all-Cloudflare stack therefore needs a third-party identity provider, and Clerk, the usual pairing, costs $25 a month plus **$0.02 per retained user above 50,000**, which Clerk's own article prices at about $17,225 for a million users ([Clerk pricing explained](https://clerk.com/articles/clerk-pricing-explained)): roughly $5,000 a month at one million registered users, $59,000 at 10 million and $299,000 at 50 million under the 30% active assumption. Supabase Auth at $0.00325 per MAU is about six times cheaper and is the only consumer-grade identity bundled into a database plan at list price ([Supabase MAU usage](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/monthly-active-users)), yet it still reaches about **$650 at one million, $9,400 at 10 million and $48,400 a month at 50 million registered users**, which is 85% of the Supabase bill at the top tier and squarely an Enterprise negotiation. Supabase bills users authenticated through Clerk, Auth0, Firebase or Cognito as Third-Party MAUs at the same $0.00325 ([Supabase third-party MAU](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/manage-your-usage/monthly-active-users-third-party)), so bolting Clerk on later would add its fee without removing Supabase's; the choice is Supabase Auth or a fully self-hosted library, not a mix. The one unpriced lever is Neon's Managed Better Auth, included with Neon Postgres up to one million MAU with London available and "request an increase" above that ([Neon plans](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/plans); [Neon regions](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/regions)), which would put the data-and-identity layer of a Workers + Hyperdrive + Neon route at about **$174 at one million and $2,057 plus an unknown auth line at 50 million**, against Supabase's $1,037 and $57,185. It is worth a conversation at the 10-million planning stage, not a change now, because its phone-OTP support and its price above one million MAU are undocumented. Phone OTP itself is a product cost that no stack changes: Supabase does not resell SMS and its Send SMS Hook exists to plug in "a regional SMS provider" ([Supabase Send SMS Hook](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/send-sms-hook)), and at ₦7 per message ([BulkSMSNigeria](https://www.bulksmsnigeria.com/resources/sms-pricing-calculator)) OTP delivery runs ₦630,000 to ₦2.1 million a month at one million users and **₦31.5 to 105 million a month at 50 million**, comparable to the entire Supabase bill. Long-lived sessions, passkeys and WhatsApp or email OTP fallbacks are worth more than any database decision at that tier.

## Email: Resend to 100,000 users, SES from 500,000, Cloudflare Email Service not yet viable for a digest

Cloudflare Email Service bundles the long-standing inbound Email Routing with Email Sending, announced as a private beta on 25 September 2025 for "transactional emails from Workers" ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/email-service/)), and the October 2026 docs still say it "is currently in beta" ([Cloudflare Email Service docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/index.md)). Workers Paid includes 3,000 emails a month and then charges **$0.35 per 1,000** ([Email Service pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/platform/pricing/)); each message may have 50 recipients, and "new accounts start with a conservative daily quota and scale up over time based on your sending behavior" with no published numbers ([Email Service limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/platform/limits/)), one community thread reporting a 200-a-day quota ([Cloudflare Developers Discord via AnswerOverflow](https://www.answeroverflow.com/m/1533088772433121313)). No dedicated-IP option, no bounce or complaint webhooks and no public path to a multi-million-per-day quota surfaced in any source. That makes it unusable for a digest that needs 300,000 sends on a Tuesday morning at one million users, and at 3.5 times SES's unit price there is no cost reason to wait for it. It is a reasonable future home for low-volume transactional mail sent from a Worker (OTP fallback, receipts) once it is generally available.

Resend is the right Phase 0 and Phase 1 provider, as ADR-0005 says, and the wrong one past roughly half a million users. Pro is $20 for 50,000 emails and Scale starts at $90 for 100,000 with no daily cap ([Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing)); dedicated IPs are a $30 add-on on Scale for senders above 3,000 a day with automatic warm-up; overage is about $0.90 per 1,000 at the low Scale tiers falling to about $0.46 at 2.5 million ([Automation Atlas](https://automationatlas.io/answers/resend-pricing-explained-2026/)); and Enterprise, quote-based, begins at **3 million emails a month** ([WP Mail SMTP](https://wpmailsmtp.com/resend-review/)), which under this model is about 510,000 registered users. The account-wide API limit is 10 requests per second with no burst, raiseable through support ([Resend quotas and limits](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/account-quotas-and-limits)), so batch sending and a raised limit are prerequisites before the 100,000-user tier. Resend's reliability record is adequate for a digest and marginal for OTPs: a 15 February 2026 incident delayed most mail by about two hours for 3.5 hours ([Resend incident report](https://www.plushcap.com/content/resend/blog/resend-incident-report-for-february-15-2026)), and a tracker counts 81 outages since February 2024 ([IsDown](https://isdown.app/status/resend/outage-history)), which argues for transactional mail on a separate stream and a second provider behind a circuit breaker from day one.

Amazon SES has no plan ceiling and gets cheaper per thousand as volume rises: **$0.10 per 1,000** outbound, standard dedicated IPs at $24.95 each with self-managed warm-up, Managed Dedicated IPs at $15 a month per account plus $0.08 per 1,000 falling to $0.02 above 50 million, and Virtual Deliverability Manager at $0.07 per 1,000 falling to $0.02 above 100 million ([AWS SES pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/pricing/)). SES has an endpoint in Cape Town (af-south-1) with quotas tracked per region ([AWS SES endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/ses.html)), automatic warm-up for dedicated IPs ([AWS Messaging blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/messaging-and-targeting/amazon-ses-can-now-automatic-warm-up-your-dedicated-ip-addresses/)) and `SendBulkTemplatedEmail`, which delivers up to 50 distinct personalised digests per API call from one template with per-recipient data ([SES API reference](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/goto/WebAPI/email/SendBulkTemplatedEmail)). Under the 5.9-emails-per-user model, SES with VDM and Managed Dedicated IPs costs about **$1, $115, $1,490, $11,800 and $46,000** a month across the five tiers, against Resend at $20, about $530, an Enterprise quote extrapolated at $2,700 to 3,500, $15,000 to 27,000 and $60,000 to 136,000. Postmark pauses accounts that send promotional mail on a transactional stream and requires its Broadcast stream for digests ([Postmark message streams](https://postmarkapp.com/blog/send-promotional-email-with-postmark-message-streams)), and at $1.20 to 1.80 per 1,000 it is five to seven times SES; SendGrid Pro and Mailgun Scale both top out at 2.5 million emails a month, about 424,000 users here ([Carly on SendGrid](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/sendgrid-pricing/); [Carly on Mailgun](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/mailgun-pricing/)). Managed Dedicated IPs are the right SES option for two founders despite costing about $2,600 a month more than eight self-managed IPs at 59 million emails, because they remove warm-up scheduling and pool sizing from a team that should not hand-roll either.

Deliverability rules are identical on every provider and set the operating constraints. Gmail and Yahoo treat senders of roughly 5,000 messages a day as bulk senders requiring SPF, DKIM, aligned DMARC and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe honoured within 48 hours on marketing-style mail, and since November 2025 Gmail rejects or rate-limits non-compliant traffic ([Red Sift](https://redsift.com/resources/blog/gmails-enforcement-ramps-up-what-bulk-senders-need-to-know)); Gmail's mitigations become unavailable when user-reported spam exceeds **0.3%** ([Google sender guidelines FAQ](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414)), and Postmaster Tools v2 now shows binary compliance checks and retires the reputation charts ([Blueshift](https://blueshift.com/blog/google-postmaster-tools-v2/)). The job digest is marketing-shaped under these rules even though users asked for it, which matches the repository's `email-template` skill. A well-warmed dedicated IP carries about 1 to 2 million emails a day, with one practitioner delivering 2.5 million a day from four IPs ([Suped](https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/technical/how-many-ips-are-needed-per-million-emails-sent-daily)), so the project needs one or two IPs warmed before one million users (300,000-recipient digest days), three to four at 10 million and nine to sixteen at 50 million, warmed over four to eight weeks. Nigerian recipients are an email audience: **98.1% of Nigerian internet users use email monthly**, the highest in the region ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1489276/email-service-usage-worldwide-by-region)). No evidence was found that Gmail or Yahoo treat Nigerian recipients differently, but scam-adjacent vocabulary ("earn", "no fee", "urgent hiring") overlaps the product's domain, so seed-testing digest copy matters more here than elsewhere. Two architectural rules follow regardless of provider: suppressions and unsubscribes live in Postgres, written from provider webhooks and the one-click endpoint, so a provider move does not drop them and spike the spam rate; and the digest runs through a provider interface in the Node job runner (pgmq batches of 50 users, one `SendBulkTemplatedEmail` or Resend batch call each), which is why the Node process in the hosting recommendation is needed whatever happens to Workers.

## Compliance and operations, not servers, are the two-founder ceiling

Every stack in this comparison keeps Nigerian users' CVs outside Nigeria, and that is a cross-border transfer under the NDPA whatever the region. Nigeria "has not adopted a single, comprehensive data localisation law" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/new-technology/1821620/data-localisation-in-nigeria-separating-myth-from-reality)); transfers are governed by NDPA sections 41 to 43 and GAID 2025, in force since 19 September 2025, which permits them under adequacy decisions, approved transfer instruments or the section 43 exceptions, with Schedule 5 setting the adequacy criteria ([Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/33614-data-protection-commissions-general-application-and-implementation-directive-including-cross-border-data-transfer-regulation-ndpcndp-act-gaid012025-enters-into-force); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/from-principles-to-practice-operationalizing-nigerias-data-protection-act-through-the-gaid)). The NDPC "has not yet published a formal adequacy list", the 2019 whitelist "has no continuing legal effect", and cross-border transfer instruments "require prior NDPC approval before any transfer is initiated" ([Mondaq compliance guide](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1793876/cross-border-data-transfers-under-nigerias-ndpa-a-compliance-guide)). Enforcement is real: MultiChoice Nigeria was fined **₦766 million** in part for unlawful cross-border transfers ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1798234/cross-border-data-transfers-and-international-compliance-legal-opportunities-in-global-tech-transactions)). At the volumes in this model the platform is a controller of major importance with annual compliance audit returns ([Mondaq on GAID](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1627428/key-regulatory-updates-under-the-2025-general-application-and-implementation-directive-gaid-issued-by-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-ndpc)). The direction of travel is toward more in-country requirements: NITDA's National Digital Cloud Policy, unveiled in October 2026, says "Level 3" data (financial, health, biometric and identity) "must be stored at rest in Nigeria" ([NITDA policy PDF](https://nitda.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/National-Cloud-Policy-2025-Oct2-2025.pdf); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/a-review-of-nigerias-national-digital-cloud-policy-new-direction-and-implication-for-businesses)), though commentators call it a policy instrument rather than primary legislation, and a reported CBN circular requires payment transaction data to be stored in Nigeria from 1 January 2027 ([Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/data-localisation-vs-crossborder-transfer-nigeria-2026/)), which the product sidesteps by keeping payment data inside Paystack. The practical mitigations are to avoid collecting NIN, BVN or biometric identifiers in CVs, and to treat the Cloud Policy as the reason to keep the data layer in portable Postgres.

The stacks differ in how defensible the Transfer Impact Assessment is. A single Supabase project in London (eu-west-2) is the most defensible footprint: data at rest in one jurisdiction with an independent regulator, Supabase's DPA as the processor instrument, and Supabase's own caveat that "backups, logs, data exported to external systems, Edge Function execution, and sub-processors can affect your data residency" ([Supabase GDPR compliance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/security/gdpr-compliance)). Supabase lists London and no African region ([Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)); Neon is the same ([Neon regions](https://neon.com/docs/introduction/regions)); only Aurora offers managed Postgres in Africa, in Cape Town with pgvector 0.8 ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/04/pgvector-0-8-0-aurora-postgresql)), at about $556 a month for an r5.xlarge rising to $7,921 for an r6g.16xlarge ([Bytebase dbcost](https://www.bytebase.com/dbcost/aurora/instance/db.r6g.16xlarge.md)), which is the fallback if a strict reading of the Cloud Policy ever bites. The all-Cloudflare footprint is materially weaker: D1, R2 and Durable Objects can be pinned only to "eu", "us" or "fedramp" jurisdictions with no African option ([R2 data location](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/reference/data-location/)), Vectorize supports no Data Localization Suite control at all ([Cloudflare DLS compatibility](https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/compatibility/)), and Workers run globally unless the Enterprise-tier Regional Services feature is used, which Cloudflare describes as "a compliance solution, not a performance optimization" ([Cloudflare Regional Services](https://developers.cloudflare.com/data-localization/regional-services/)). The mix inherits the London footprint for data at rest, with one addition the TIA must document: Hyperdrive caches query responses at Cloudflare edge locations worldwide, so caching should be disabled for queries that return personal data or the brief transit documented.

Operational burden for two founders is the tiebreaker the cost tables cannot show. Supabase is one console, one bill, compute resizes "with less than 2 minutes of downtime" ([Supabase compute and disk](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/compute-and-disk)) and a $100 PITR add-on; Cloudflare-native storage is many small databases and indexes with scale-to-zero billing but bespoke sharding, consistency and export code, and neither Vectorize nor Durable Objects has a bulk export path, while D1 exports as SQLite SQL in files of at most 5 GiB that need pgloader and type-affinity fixes to reach Postgres ([D1 import and export](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/import-export-data/)). SES adds an AWS account, IAM, sandbox exit and SNS bounce handling, which is exactly why ADR-0005 deferred it, but Managed Dedicated IPs and VDM remove the two tasks (warm-up and reputation monitoring) that actually consume a founder's week. Workers add Wrangler, a changing OpenNext support matrix and a Browser Rendering concurrency budget. None of this is where the team will actually break: the Indeed and Apna numbers above say listing review, scam triage, payment disputes and support grow faster than users, so the architecture should be judged on how much human review it avoids (automated screening quality, admin queue tooling) at least as much as on the hosting line. Managed services on any stack remove the ops roles; none removes the review roles.

## Recommendation per layer, the triggers that move it, and the ADRs it changes

The recommendation is a staged mix with explicit triggers rather than a one-time choice, because the current ADRs are correct for the first deploy and wrong by about the 300,000th user. Spend decisions should be made for the one-million tier, which the evidence says is reachable; architecture decisions should not preclude ten million; fifty million should be treated as a band in which Supabase Auth, SES IP pools and the single-primary Postgres are all Enterprise or sharding conversations that a funded company with hundreds of staff has, not two founders.

Three readings of the table matter more than the totals. First, below 100,000 users the three stacks are within a few hundred dollars of each other and the choice should be made on operations and Nigeria fit, which is why ADR-0005 and ADR-0008 stand for the first deploy. Second, from one million users the mix saves roughly 25 to 50% against Stack 1, and the saving comes almost entirely from hosting and email, because both stacks carry the same Supabase Auth line; at 50 million that line is 45% of the recommended stack's bill, so the identity negotiation (or a self-hosted library) is the single largest lever the founders hold, worth more than the hosting and email decisions combined. Third, the all-Cloudflare stack is cheapest at 1,000 users and most expensive at every tier from one million, purely because of Clerk; its storage layer (D1, R2, Vectorize at about $2,800 a month for 50 million users) is competitive, and a variant with self-hosted Better Auth and SES instead of Email Service would cost roughly $52,000 a month at 50 million, cheaper than the recommendation but at the price of 175 hand-sharded SQLite databases, no SQL vector search and no bulk export, which is not a trade two founders should make.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "Vercel or Cloudflare" to "which layers have a per-user meter and which do not". Hosting and email have usage meters that diverge by an order of magnitude between vendors, so they are the layers to move, and the triggers are concrete and early (a $150 hosting bill, 590,000 emails a month). Postgres is a commodity on every host and moves with pg_dump, so staying on Supabase costs nothing in optionality. Identity is the layer with the steepest per-user meter on every route and the fewest alternatives, which inverts the usual intuition that the database is the scaling cost: at 50 million users the recommended stack spends more on login than on compute, storage and email combined, and SMS OTP in naira rivals the entire cloud bill. The product decisions that shrink those two lines (long-lived sessions, passkeys, WhatsApp or email fallbacks, and above all a growth target the market can support) are worth more than any infrastructure switch.

The forward-match insight has a second-order effect worth acting on now: because the job index stays under 2 GB regardless of user count, the matching pipeline can be designed as a batch over active users against a small, hot index rather than as a fan-out over a growing one, and the only thing that would ever force a dedicated vector store is a feature (recruiter search) that should be priced as a product decision when an employer asks for it. The ADR amendments that follow from this report are small and should be made before the first deploy, while the decisions are fresh: ADR-0005 gains a provider interface, a Postgres suppression table and a dated hand-off to SES; ADR-0008 gains the Workers pilot for `admin`, the two-deployable API and the Hyperdrive connection rule; ADR-0007 gains one sentence on forward versus reverse search. Everything else in the current stack survives.

## What could not be verified

The research sandbox's proxy refused direct fetches of nearly every vendor page, so most prices and limits come from search-result excerpts of the named pages rather than the pages themselves; the exceptions are the Supabase, Cloudflare, Neon, PlanetScale and pgvector documentation, which were read from the vendors' public documentation source repositories on GitHub. The founders should open these before signing anything: the Workers limits and pricing pages (to confirm the 64 MiB uncompressed bundle limit reported for 4 September 2026, the 128 MB memory cap and the $5 / $0.30 per million / $0.02 per million CPU-ms rates), the Vercel Fluid and regional pricing pages for fra1 and cpt1, Cloudflare's current Next.js support table for Next.js 16, Cloudflare's plan page for Pro and Business prices (sources disagree between $20/$200 and $25/$250), the Resend pricing page for Scale tiers above 100,000 and the batch endpoint's limits, and the SES pricing page for the Managed Dedicated IP band above 100 million emails.

Several load-bearing facts have no primary source. Vercel's billing treatment of a third-party proxy in front of it was not retrievable, so the Cloudflare-fronted Vercel figures are an assumption and the Vercel-only figures are the safe upper bound. Whether `docx`, the Anthropic SDK's streaming and the Hyperdrive-to-Supabase connection work in production Workers is inferred from the Node-compatibility documentation, not tested; ADR-0008 already lists this as its open check. No Lagos-to-London or Lagos-to-Workers latency measurement exists in any source, and no measured cold-start figure for OpenNext on Workers. Fly.io and Railway were not priced, so the Node job runner's cost is absent from every row. Neon Managed Better Auth's price above one million MAU and its phone-OTP support are undocumented. Supabase publishes no pricing above 16XL, no Enterprise MAU rate and no read-replica count limit, and neither Supabase nor anyone else has published a pgvector benchmark at 10 to 50 million vectors on managed Postgres; the halfvec extrapolations are from a 1,536-dimension float32 guide. Cloudflare Email Service's daily quota rests on one forum post, and no source addressed its webhooks, dedicated IPs or acceptable-use wording on digest mail. Resend's Enterprise rate, and every SendGrid, Mailgun, Brevo and Loops figure above 2.5 million emails a month, are extrapolations because none is published.

On the Nigerian side, the primary GAID 2025 text on ndpc.gov.ng was unreachable, so the Schedule 5 article numbering, the exact consent fallback (sources disagree on whether consent alone suffices) and whether any cross-border transfer instrument has yet been approved in 2026 are taken from law-firm summaries; the CBN directive on payment-data localisation from 1 January 2027 was seen only through secondary commentary and should be checked against the CBN circular; and the National Digital Cloud Policy's Level 3 rule is a policy document whose binding force on private companies no court has tested. No Nigerian job platform publishes MAU, session counts, per-user data footprints or digest open rates, so every activity assumption in the cost model is a modelling choice bracketed by Jobberman's one disclosed active ratio and LinkedIn's third-party estimates; the registered-user figures for Jobberman, Apna, OPay and PalmPay are company claims, with Apna's current total contested between 26 and 60 million. Finally, no engineering account of any job board's digest or matching pipeline at 10 million or more users exists in public, and no African or Indian consumer app at that scale publishes a Vercel, Workers, Supabase or Resend case study; the comparable companies found (Paystack, Jumia, Meesho) run Kubernetes on AWS or GCP, which says more about their age and payments compliance than about these platforms' limits.
