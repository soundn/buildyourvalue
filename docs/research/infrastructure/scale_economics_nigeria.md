# Scale economics for a Nigerian job-matching platform at 10–50M users (as of 2026-10-24)

Method note: outbound page fetches were blocked at the sandbox proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), so every figure below comes from search-result snippets and is tagged **[snippet]**. No page was read in full. Vendor marketing is tagged **[vendor claim]**; company self-reported numbers are tagged **[company claim]**. Figures that are my own arithmetic are in the Inferences sections and the sanity model, not in Cited Findings.

## Q1. Reference points: how big are comparable platforms, and how long did they take?

### Takeaway
No Nigerian job platform has ever approached 10M registered users: Jobberman, the market leader, reports ~2.5–2.6M registered jobseekers after 16 years and ~330k new sign-ups in 2025. The only Nigerian consumer apps that reached 25–45M did so as cash-wallet fintechs with agent networks and cash incentives over 4–8 years; Apna (India, a market ~6× larger) reached 10M in roughly two years and ~35M+ later. The plausible ceiling for a Nigerian job platform is bounded by LinkedIn's ~13M Nigerian members, not by the 109M internet users.

### Inferences
- Time-to-milestone summary (from the cited points): Ngcareers ~10 years to 1M; Jobberman ~16 years to 2.5M; Fuzu ~9 years to 1.5M; Kormo ~4 years to 4.5M across three countries with Google's distribution; Apna ~2 years (2019 launch → June 2021) to 10M in a market with ~6× Nigeria's labour force and ~8× its internet users; PalmPay ~3.5 years to 25M and ~5.5 years to 35M; OPay ~7 years to 45M registered / 39M monthly transacting; Kuda ~5 years to 7M; PiggyVest ~9 years to 5.8M.
- Every Nigerian product above 10M is a payments wallet where the product is used daily, acquisition is subsidised (agents, cashback) and the company raised hundreds of millions of dollars. No employment, education or e-commerce product in Nigeria is near 10M registered users (Jumia's whole 8-country footprint is 6M annual actives).
- Ceiling arithmetic: 109M internet users × 75% smartphone share ≈ 80M smartphone-internet users. LinkedIn, with global brand and 20+ years, reaches ~13M Nigerians (≈12% of internet users, ≈15% of the labour force). India's state-run NCS has 57.9M registered jobseekers out of a labour force of roughly 600M (≈10%). A 10% share of Nigeria's 89M labour force is ≈9M; a LinkedIn-sized 15% share is ≈13M. 50M would be ~56% of the entire labour force and ~46% of all internet users, above any job platform's penetration in any market found here.
- Realistic two-year targets by analogy: Apna-speed growth, scaled down by the ~6× market-size difference, is ≈1.5–2.5M registered in two years and would already make the product Nigeria's largest job platform. 10M in two years would require growth 4× faster than Jobberman achieved in 16 years and a fintech-scale acquisition budget; 50M in two years has no precedent in Nigeria in any category, including payments.

### Gaps
- No official Jobberman registered-user total for 2025/2026 (only the 2.5M boilerplate and 2025 sign-ups); no date for when Jobberman itself crossed 1M.
- No MAU figures published by Jobberman, MyJobMag, Indeed Nigeria or LinkedIn for Nigeria; only ad-reach and SEO-tool estimates.

- uLesson, Moniepoint consumer-app and Kormo-Nigeria user numbers not found.
- No NBS 2025 labour-force release surfaced; 2023 is the latest NBS figure found.

## Q2. Activity assumptions: MAU/registered, sessions, digest open rates, post-hire churn

### Takeaway
Job search is episodic (once or twice a year per person), so published practitioner guidance treats job boards as "resurrection" rather than retention products; the only Nigerian ratio found (Jobberman ~606k active of ~2.6M, ≈23%) and LinkedIn's estimated 24–48% MAU/member ratio both support planning on 15–25% MAU/registered. Open-rate benchmarks are vendor-sourced and conflict (19–42%); there is no job-alert-specific benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Jobberman Nigeria: "over 2.6 million on the platform", "over 606,000 active candidates" (June 2026, third-party) — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/jobberman-review-2026-is-jobberman-legit-or-a-scam/) [snippet]
- LinkedIn: ~1.2–1.3B registered members; LinkedIn does not publish MAU; third-party MAU estimates range from ~310M to ~582M; DataReportal warns its LinkedIn figures are ad-reach based on registered members, "not a proxy for MAU" — [DataReportal LinkedIn stats](https://datareportal.com/essential-linkedin-stats?lang=en); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/library/linkedin-statistics); [Waalaxy](https://blog.waalaxy.com/en/linkedin-statistics/) [snippet]

### Inferences
- Planning ratios for the sanity model: MAU = 20% of registered (range 10–30%); weekly-digest audience = ~40% of registered (anyone active in the last 90 days who has not unsubscribed), i.e. about 2× MAU; digest open rate 20–35% (Apple Mail privacy inflates opens, so treat click-through as the real signal); unsubscribe <0.3%/send and spam complaints <0.1% as hard operating limits, since the recruitment-sector benchmarks put 0.3% as the danger line.
- Apna's ratio of 1.5–1.8 interviews per registered user per month implies very high activity for a blue-collar market with many short tenures; a white-collar Nigerian product should assume far fewer actions per user.
- Post-hire churn means the active base is set by inflow × average search duration (typically 3–6 months), not by cumulative sign-ups. Reaching 10M MAU would therefore require 30–50M registered on a 20–30% ratio, which collides with the ceiling in Q1.

### Gaps
- No published MAU/registered ratio from any job platform (LinkedIn, Indeed, Apna, Jobberman all withhold MAU).
- No job-alert-specific open-rate benchmark; all figures are vendor marketing data.
- No measured post-hire churn rate; only qualitative guidance.
- No Nigerian-specific weekly session counts for job seekers.

## Q3. Architecture by stage: what breaks at 100k, 1M, 10M, 50M and the standard fixes

### Takeaway
Published scaling accounts (Figma, Notion, Supabase docs, pgvector guides) agree on the order of failures: connection exhaustion first (fixed by pooling), then read load (replicas + cache), then the vector index outgrowing RAM (halfvec/quantisation, then a separate vector store), then single-writer limits (vertical partitioning, then sharding). Supabase's own ceiling is a single 64-core/256GB primary plus read-only replicas with no managed sharding (Multigres is in preview), which is adequate to roughly the 10M-registered tier of this model and strained beyond it.

### Inferences
- Stage map for a Postgres + Next.js + email-digest system under this model's assumptions:
  - 1k–100k registered: nothing structural breaks. Risks are operational: exhausting the Supabase pooler's client connections from serverless functions (fix: transaction-mode pooler, short-lived connections), unbounded egress from CV uploads (fix: direct-to-storage uploads behind a CDN), and domain reputation during the first weeks of digest sends (fix: ramp sends, enforce one-click unsubscribe, suppress bounces).
  - 100k–1M: the digest becomes the biggest batch job (≈400k emails/week at 1M). Resend's $0.90/1,000 overage pricing stops making sense versus SES at $0.10–0.16/1,000 unless an enterprise rate is negotiated; a dedicated IP becomes justified by Postmark's 300k/month rule. Reads from the dashboard and job pages need a cache or CDN in front of the API. Auth MAU pricing starts to show (≈$325/month at 200k MAU on the $0.00325 figure). Compute likely at Large/XL ($100s/month).
  - 1M–10M: the profile-embedding table (7M vectors) and its HNSW index approach the 64 GB RAM band in the pgvector guidance, forcing halfvec/quantisation or a move of ANN search out of the primary. Read replicas become mandatory; matching fan-out (every new listing scored against millions of profiles, or every digest recipient retrieving top-k) must move to a queue with batch workers. Compute at the 8XL–16XL band ($1.9k–$3.7k/month primary) plus replicas; this is where Supabase's single-primary ceiling is first visible. Admin review of flagged listings exceeds what two founders can read; Indeed's trajectory shows removals growing faster than users, so automated screening must handle >95% of volume.
  - 10M–50M: the 16XL primary (256 GB) cannot hold a 35M-vector index plus the working set; you need either sharded Postgres (Multigres, Citus, application-level by region/user bucket as Notion did) or a separate vector store, plus multi-tenant email streams and multiple sending domains. This is the band in which Figma and Notion each adopted sharding; nothing in Supabase's managed offering covers it today.
- Trigger pattern in the documented exits: Vercel departures were driven by unpredictable usage meters (bots, function invocations, Marketplace databases), typically at bills in the $1k–2k/month range; none cited compliance. Supabase departures for scale reasons were not found, only arrivals. So the realistic trigger for a managed-to-cloud move is bill shape and the single-primary ceiling, which this model places around the 10M-registered tier.

### Inferences
- Hosting 50M Nigerians' CVs in London or the US is a cross-border transfer under NDPA s.41 with no adequacy decision to lean on today. The compliant path is a documented Schedule 5-style assessment of the destination plus an NDPC-approved transfer instrument (and a DPIA under s.28 given the volume and the sensitivity of employment data); at this scale the platform would almost certainly be an Ultra-High or Extra-High "controller of major importance" with annual CAR filings. None of this is a localisation mandate, but it is paperwork and approval latency, and the direction of travel (CBN 2027 payment-data rule, NITDA sovereign cloud) is toward more in-country requirements. Keeping payment data entirely inside Paystack (which must itself comply with the CBN rule) avoids the one hard localisation rule that exists.
- Data cost shapes product more than hosting does: at ≈₦431–₦800/GB, a 1 MB page costs the user roughly ₦0.4–₦0.8 and a 5 MB CV preview ₦2–₦4; the digest email and WhatsApp are cheaper for the user than opening the app. WhatsApp utility messages at ≈₦14 each would cost ≈₦280M (≈$190k) per weekly send to a 20M-recipient digest list, versus ≈$2k on SES, so WhatsApp is for transactional/high-value nudges only.
- Paystack's 600 req/min standard limit and 1,200/min charge limit are per-integration ceilings: at 50M registered with a few percent paying, renewals cluster on billing days and must be spread across the month or driven by Paystack's own subscription/webhook model rather than client-initiated charges.

## Q7. Sanity model (reusable table)

### Takeaway
Under stated assumptions (20% MAU/registered, weekly digest to ~40% of registered, 1024-dim halfvec embeddings, ~20 KB of relational data per user, 100 API requests per MAU per month), the system stays inside a single managed Postgres and a shared email pool through 1M registered, needs replicas, queues, a dedicated sending setup and vector quantisation at 10M, and exceeds a single-primary Supabase deployment at 50M. ### Inferences

Assumptions (state these wherever the table is reused)
- A1 MAU = 20% of registered (job search is episodic; range 10–30%).
- A2 Weekly digest audience = 40% of registered (active in last 90 days, not unsubscribed) → monthly sends = 40% × registered × 4.33.
- A3 Profile embeddings = 70% of registered (users who completed a CV), one 1024-dim halfvec vector each (2 KB), HNSW index overhead ≈ +1.0–1.5× vector bytes.
- A4 Live job listings scale sub-linearly with users (supply is the Nigerian formal market, not the user base): 20k at ≤100k users, 100k at 1M, 300k at 10M, 500k at 50M (pan-African expansion assumed at the top tier); 90-day retention of listing embeddings ≈ 2× live count.
- A5 Relational footprint ≈ 20 KB per registered user (profile, applications, events, digest logs); CV files (~300 KB average) live in object storage, not Postgres.
- A6 API traffic ≈ 100 requests per MAU per month (≈4 sessions × 25 requests); peak = 5× average.
- A7 Matching work: each new listing is scored against the profile index once (batch), and each digest recipient runs one top-k ANN query per week.

Reading the table against the cited limits
- 100k: digest volume (≈173k/month) is below Postmark's 300k/month dedicated-IP threshold; shared IPs plus domain reputation suffice. DB fits any small managed instance. Monthly email ≈$17 on SES, ≈$35–$90 on Resend tiers.
- 1M: ≈1.7M emails/month ≈$170–$280 on SES vs ≈$1.5k on Resend overage pricing; dedicated IP justified. Vector index ~2–3 GB fits a 16 GB instance. ≈40 req/s peak is trivial for one API node behind a CDN. This tier is inside a single Supabase Large/XL plus pooler.
- 10M: ≈17M emails/month (≈$1.7k–$2.8k SES, ≈$15k Resend list price) at ≈4M per weekly send, i.e. ~600k/day if spread over a week or several million on one day, which crosses Postmark's 100k/day-per-IP guidance → multiple IPs/streams. The 35–45 GB index sits inside the 64 GB RAM band where the pgvector guide says HNSW still works if cached; halfvec/binary quantisation and a dedicated 8XL–16XL primary plus read replicas are needed. 4M ANN queries/week is ~7/s average but bursts to hundreds/s during digest builds unless batched.
- 50M: ≈87M emails/month (≈$8.7k–$14k SES, ≈$78k Resend list price); a 150–180 GB vector index plus a 1 TB relational set no longer fit comfortably in a single 256 GB primary; sharding or an external vector store is required, which is beyond Supabase's current managed offering (Multigres in preview). ≈1,900 req/s peak is modest for a CDN-fronted API but the write path (applications, events) needs partitioning. This is the band where Figma and Notion sharded.
- Egress check: with CDN-cached HTML/JS and CV previews averaging, say, 3 MB per session, 10M MAU × 4 sessions ≈ 120 TB/month ≈ $10k–$17k/month on a metered CDN at $0.085–$0.14/GB versus a flat Cloudflare plan; at 1M registered the same figure is ≈2.4 TB/month (≈$200–$340), i.e. egress is not decision-relevant below the 10M tier.

### Gaps
- All per-user storage and request assumptions are modelling choices, not measured from a comparable product; no job platform publishes them.
- pgvector capacity bands are for 1536-dim fp32 and are extrapolated here to 1024-dim halfvec.
- No published figure for how many live listings Nigeria's formal market produces per month (Jobberman's 60k employers and Apna's 1.4M postings/year are the only anchors).
