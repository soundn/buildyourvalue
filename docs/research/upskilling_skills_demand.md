# Upskilling and Skills-Demand Landscape for Nigerian Job Seekers (2025–2026)

**Method note for the report writer.** Research date: 2026-10-20. Findings below come from web-search results (titles, URLs and the search engine's extracted passages). Every attempt to open a page directly (WebFetch and curl via the session proxy) was refused for every host tried (weforum.org, 3mtt.nitda.gov.ng, blog.google, coursera.org, jobscan.co, resumeworded.com, alxafrica.com, techcabal.com, businessday.ng, nairametrics.com, guardian.ng and others), and the shared search budget was exhausted before Andela/Turing/Toptal, affiliate programmes, Microsoft/AWS/Cisco/HubSpot free certifications, HBS "Hidden Workers", LinkedIn Career Explorer and nudge-RCT evidence could be searched. Figures are therefore as reported in search-indexed passages, not verified against the full primary documents; where a figure is secondary or vendor-sourced this is said. Nigeria-specific vs global data is labelled throughout.

## Key Question 1: Which roles and skills are most in demand in Nigeria right now?

### Takeaway
The only Nigeria-specific, employer-survey-based demand data found is the WEF Future of Jobs 2025 Nigeria figures (as relayed by LSETF and Nigerian press): cybersecurity is the standout (87% of Nigerian employers call network/cybersecurity skills critical vs 70% globally), with AI/ML specialists, data analysts and sustainability experts the fastest-growing roles. Beyond tech, the best-evidenced shortages are in accounting (ICAN/ACCA), skilled construction trades (welders, electricians, plumbers, carpenters), and BPO/customer support (the FG is explicitly steering 3MTT fellows into BPO jobs). No Nigeria-specific LinkedIn "Jobs on the Rise 2025" list or Jobberman 2025 demand report could be located.

### Cited Findings

**Nigeria — WEF Future of Jobs 2025 (secondary coverage; primary PDF could not be opened)**
- 87% of Nigerian employers identify network and cybersecurity skills as critical, "well above the global average of 70 percent" — [LSETF (relaying WEF Future of Jobs 2025 Nigeria data)](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678); same figure also in [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1028029)
- Fastest-growing roles for Nigeria: AI and machine learning specialists (+82% net), data analysts (+41%), sustainability experts (+33%) — [LSETF](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678)
- 65% of Nigerian employers cite skill gaps as a major barrier to business transformation; about half report difficulty attracting talent, resistance to organisational change and regulatory inflexibility — [LSETF](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678)
- 73% of firms operating in Nigeria see the need for increased public-sector funding for reskilling/upskilling — [LSETF](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678)
- Caveat: these percentages appear in several outlets that seem to relay the same LSETF material; one CNBC Africa item carries a disclaimer that its content may be inaccurate — [CNBC Africa](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/media/6366857075112/wef-future-of-jobs-2025-re-shaping-africas-industries)
- Global context (not Nigeria-specific): WEF projects 170m jobs created and 92m displaced by 2030; survey of 1,000+ large employers (500+ staff) across 55 economies — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/01/09/top-10-fastest-declining-jobs-in-2025-report/); [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=506159)
- Africa-wide WEF-attributed estimate: 39% of workers' core skills will change by 2030 — [CNBC Africa](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/media/6366857075112/wef-future-of-jobs-2025-re-shaping-africas-industries)
- IFC-attributed figure (via a consultancy blog, not the WEF report): 28 million jobs in Nigeria will require digital skills by 2030 — [Phillips Consulting](https://phillipsconsulting.net/?p=11261)

**Nigeria — Jobberman / employer sentiment**
- Jobberman Nigeria's 2025 student job-market piece says employers want reskilling, cross-functional skills and a growth mindset; it also flags sustainability skills (environmental management, sustainable design, renewable energy) in manufacturing, energy and construction — [Jobberman Nigeria](https://jobberman.com/discover/student-job-market-trends-what-employers-are-looking-for-2025)
- Jobberman Nigeria's Head of Operations (Samantha Ifezulike): employers "are placing less emphasis on qualifications alone and paying closer attention to practical skills, adaptability and a candidate's ability to learn quickly" — [BusinessDay interview](https://businessday.ng/?p=1028029)
- A BusinessDay piece names "top six tech skills Nigerians need to stay relevant in 2026" (content not retrievable; headline only) — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/top-six-tech-skills-nigerians-need-to-stay-relevant-in-2026/)
- No standalone Jobberman Nigeria 2025 in-demand-skills report was found; the "10 Most In-Demand Job Skills in 2025" list surfaced is Jobberman **Ghana** (data science/AI-ML, Python, R, data visualisation) — [Jobberman Ghana](https://jobberman.com.gh/discover/10-most-in-demand-job-skills-in2025-jobberman-g)

**Nigeria — tech salaries (indicative, mixed-quality sources)**
- Senior tech roles pay ₦2m+ monthly (~$1,333 at ~₦1,500/$); data engineers ₦1.8m–₦2.5m monthly mid-to-senior — [TechCabal, "highest-paying tech jobs in Nigeria (2025)"](https://techcabal.com/?p=167925)
- Recruiter estimate: software engineers mid-level ₦600k–₦2m/month, senior ₦2m–₦4m; data analysts ₦300k–₦800k; graphic designers ₦100k–₦350k — [Employsome](https://employsome.com/?p=6898)
- Self-reported data: middle 80% of software engineer respondents earn ₦366k–₦2.7m gross monthly — [Paylab Nigeria](https://nigeria.paylab.com/salaryinfo/information-technology/software-engineer)
- Naira moved from ~₦800 to ~₦1,600 per USD since early 2024, which inflates naira salary figures across sources — [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/software-engineer-salary-nigeria/)

**Nigeria — finance/accounting (ICAN/ACCA)**
- "Chartered accountants remain among the most consistently in-demand professionals across Nigeria's economy... employers actively compete for ICAN- and ACCA-qualified candidates"; Big Four Nigeria firms "explicitly prefer or require ICAN or ACCA credentials for audit, tax, and advisory" — [Profolio.ng](https://www.profolio.ng/accountant/best-certifications)
- Statutory audit / public-company sign-off roles require ICAN or ANAN membership; ACCA favoured by multinationals and international banks, ICAN stronger in public sector and local corporates; mutual recognition lets ACCA members fast-track ICAN and vice versa — [Learnsignal (training-provider blog)](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/ican-vs-acca-nigeria-2026/)
- Salary ranges conflict: entry-level qualified accountants ₦250k–₦400k/month (one estimate) vs newly-qualified ACCA ₦700k–₦1.2m/month (another); Big 4 advisory ₦15m–₦70m/year; finance managers ₦6m–₦25m/year — [Learnsignal](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/acca-salary-nigeria/); [Profolio.ng](https://www.profolio.ng/finance-manager/best-certifications)

**Nigeria — skilled trades**
- Construction sector "bleeding" from a shrinking supply of skilled workers; contractors say projects stall for lack of competent hands — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=541633); [BusinessDay, "Nigeria has thousands of unfilled jobs even as many young Nigerians cry for unemployment"](https://businessday.ng/pro/article/nigeria-has-thousands-of-unfilled-jobs-even-as-many-young-nigerians-cry-for-unemployment/)
- Up to 80% of artisans working in Nigeria are foreigners (mostly from Benin, Togo, Ghana); naira depreciation has pushed many to leave; welders, electricians, plumbers and fabricators named as chronically scarce; skilled carpenters reported earning ₦1m–₦2m monthly (anecdotal) — [TechEconomy, "How Nigeria can reverse the artisan shortage"](https://techeconomy.ng/how-nigeria-can-reverse-the-artisan-shortage)

**Nigeria — healthcare**
- Only indirect evidence found: GMC data cited showing a sharp rise in Nigerian nurses registering in the UK 2018–2022 (emigration-driven domestic shortage) — [Kola King Substack, "10 in-demand jobs as Nigerian firms seen hiring more"](https://kolaking.substack.com/p/10-in-demand-jobs-as-nigerian-firms)

**Nigeria — BPO / customer support**
- Federal Government partnered with BPO companies to create jobs for 3MTT fellows — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/technology/fg-bpo-companies-partner-to-create-jobs-for-3mtt-fellows/)
- Nigeria's remote market has separate naira rates for local employers and dollar rates for foreign clients; a foreign employer hiring directly should budget ₦150k–₦300k/month for an experienced English-speaking support agent or VA in Lagos — [HiveDesk Nigeria salary guide](https://www.hivedesk.com/salary-guides/nigeria)

**Nigeria — 3MTT programme tracks**
- 3MTT focus areas: Artificial Intelligence, Cybersecurity, Software Development, Data Science "and more" — [SchoolNGR](https://www.schoolngr.com/news/federal-government-3mtt-programme)
- Specialised 2025 tracks: Airtel–3MTT NextGen Fellowship — [ScholarDigger](https://www.scholardigger.com/post/airtel-3mtt-nextgen-fellowship-2025); DeepTech_Ready Upskilling Programme for 3MTT fellows via 3mtt.nitda.gov.ng — [Voice of Nigeria](https://von.gov.ng/?p=386833)
- Cohort sizes: Phase 1 30,000 fellows — [Technology Times](https://technologytimes.ng/3mtt-30000-fellows-ready-for-job-market/); Phase 2 270,000 fellows selected — [NAN](https://nannews.ng/ministry-commences-phase-2-of-3mtt-initiative-selects-270000-fellows/); Cohort 3 90,000 fellows — [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/?p=37449); Cohort 3 exit assessment concluded, Cohort 4 pending — [3MTT Lagos Community Substack](https://3mttlagoscommunity.substack.com/p/3mtt-lagos-community-update)

**Nigeria — labour market baseline (NBS)**
- NBS unemployment 5.3% in Q1 2024 (up from 5.0% Q3 2023); youth (15–24) 8.4% — [NAN](https://nannews.ng/?p=266943); [Arise TV](https://www.arise.tv/nigerias-unemployment-rate-rises-to-5-3-in-q1-2024-nbs-report-reveals/)
- Q2 2024 reported at 4.3% (secondary) — [Nairametrics tag page](https://nairametrics.com/tag/nigeria-labour-force-survey/)
- NBS changed methodology to ILO definitions in 2023; the fall from 33.3% (Q4 2020) is methodological, so old and new series are not comparable — [Gazette NGR](https://gazettengr.com/nbs-nigerias-unemployment-rate-dropped-to-4-1-says-statiscian-general/)
- No NBS 2025 Labour Force Survey release surfaced; a World Bank modelled series gives 3.1% for 2025 — [Statistics of the World](https://statisticsoftheworld.com/country/nigeria/unemployment)

**LinkedIn**
- LinkedIn Jobs on the Rise 2025 methodology: jobs started by members 1 Jan 2022–31 Jul 2024, three-year lookback, excludes internships/interim/student roles — [LinkedIn Work Change Report](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/work-change-report); UK edition for reference — [Edge Foundation](https://www.edge.co.uk/research/skills-shortages/skills-shortages-publication-library/linkedin-jobs-on-the-rise-2025-the-25-fastest-growing-roles-in-the-uk/). No Nigeria edition found.

### Inferences
- For a "curated path" the defensible demand-ranked shortlist for Nigeria is: (1) cybersecurity, (2) data analysis/AI-ML, (3) software development, (4) accounting with ICAN/ACCA, (5) customer support/BPO (dollar-earning potential, FG placement channel), (6) skilled trades (high pay, almost no formal upskilling supply online). UI/UX, product and digital marketing have no Nigeria-specific demand data in what was found — they should be shown as "common" rather than "fastest-growing" unless the team pulls job-board counts itself.
- Employer rhetoric (Jobberman, WEF Nigeria) consistently emphasises adaptability and practical skills over credentials, which supports a path design that ends in a portfolio artefact or project rather than only a certificate.
- Salary data is fragmented and FX-distorted; the product should present salary ranges with a date and FX rate stamp, or in USD for remote roles.

### Gaps
- No Nigeria-specific LinkedIn "Jobs on the Rise 2025", no Jobberman Nigeria 2025 skills report, no Stears or PwC Nigeria demand report surfaced; job-board category counts (Jobberman/MyJobMag) were not retrievable.
- WEF Nigeria economy-profile percentages could not be verified against the WEF PDF (host blocked).
- No quantitative demand data for UI/UX, product management, digital marketing, healthcare or sales roles in Nigeria.
- 3MTT's official full track list for Cohort 3/4 was not obtained (site blocked).

## Key Question 2: What is the documented skills gap (employer complaints, "unemployable graduates")?

### Takeaway
The headline "85% of Nigerian graduates lack digital skills" comes from a ~100-person GetBundi survey of NYSC members (2023), and the "<20% immediately employable" figure is an unverified AfDB attribution; the rigorous evidence is small, sector-specific studies showing gaps in logical thinking, negotiation, responsibility, communication and marketing. The most credible system-level number is WEF's 65% of Nigerian employers naming skills gaps as a major barrier.

### Cited Findings
- GetBundi survey (2023): "more than 85% of Nigerian graduates lack digital skills", based on about 100 NYSC members; CEO links the gap directly to graduate unemployment — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2199459); [NAN](https://nannews.ng/85-of-nigerian-graduates-have-no-digital-skill-report/); one explainer attributes the same 85% figure to NITDA instead, which is unverified — [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/nigerian-graduates-lack-digital-skills/)
- GetBundi recommendations: update curricula with coding, data analysis, cybersecurity; run digital-literacy programmes for the existing workforce — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2199459)
- 65% of Nigerian employers cite skill gaps as a major barrier to transformation (WEF FoJ 2025 Nigeria) — [LSETF](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678)
- Oyo State study of 200 employers (2017): employers value social and communication skills highly but also rated graduates' levels of these skills as high — findings point both ways — [University of Ibadan repository](https://repository.ui.edu.ng/items/031448d6-366f-4c14-9e3f-2b27ec9f64ec)
- Real-estate graduates, Lagos and Abuja (2021; 172 employer + 119 graduate responses): employers prize responsibility, administrative, listening and communication skills; significant gaps in logical thinking, business negotiation, responsibility and marketing — [Emerald, Property Management](https://www.emerald.com/pm/article/39/5/581/322389/Factors-influencing-real-estate-graduates-soft)
- Estate-management graduates, Ikeja (40 employers): most graduates lacked basic soft and hard skills (small sample) — [IATED](https://library.iated.org/view/AYEDUN2017ANA)
- Claim that fewer than 20% of Nigerian graduates are immediately employable, attributed to AfDB, appears in a Brunel-hosted paper; the original AfDB source could not be verified — [Brunel University repository](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/33025/1/FullText.pdf)
- BusinessDay columnist argues "unemployable" is a misleading label; the core issue is curriculum–labour-market mismatch, and soft skills such as communication and adaptability "are rarely cultivated with intention" — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/columnist/article/why-nigerian-employers-struggle-to-find-employable-talent/); [SOL Nigeria repost](https://www.solnigeria.com/blog/why-nigerian-employers-struggle-to-find-employable-talent)
- "Widening skills gap keeps millions of Nigerians jobless" (headline-level; content not retrieved) — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/article/widening-skills-gap-keeps-millions-of-nigerians-jobless/)
- Veriv Africa insight on "Nigeria's graduate employability crisis" (headline-level; content not retrieved) — [Veriv Africa](https://verivafrica.com/insights/nigerias-graduate-employability-crisis-bridging-the-skill-gap-for-career-success)

### Inferences
- The product can credibly say "employers report gaps in digital literacy, communication, logical/analytical thinking and negotiation" but should avoid repeating "85%" or "<20% employable" as hard facts without the sample-size caveat.
- Digital literacy (including spreadsheet/Excel competence) is implied by the GetBundi findings but no source explicitly measured Excel skills; treat Excel as an inferred need, not a documented one.

### Gaps
- No large national employer survey on graduate skills was found; no PwC Nigeria graduate-skills report surfaced.
- No source specifically quantified Excel/spreadsheet or written-English deficits among Nigerian graduates.

## Key Question 3: Remote and global work from Nigeria — roles, pay, platforms, barriers

### Takeaway
About 17% of Nigerian jobs are remote (MyJobMag 2025) vs 28% globally, and Nigeria accounts for a large share of ~17.5m online gig workers in its region (World Bank). Dollar-priced support/VA work is realistic (indicative $300–$2,000/month for VAs, $10–$25/hour support), but the hard constraints are payments (PayPal effectively unusable, Payoneer stacked fees), FX volatility, and the 2025 tax reform taxing residents' worldwide income. Most pay data comes from vendors or modelled salary databases and conflicts widely.

### Cited Findings
- MyJobMag 2025 Remote Work Statistics Report: ~17% of jobs in Nigeria are remote vs global average 28% — [AllAfrica (2026)](https://allafrica.com/stories/202607240385.html)
- World Bank estimate of ~17.5 million online gig workers across Nigeria plus two other countries (sources name Kenya/South Africa or Ghana/South Africa) — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1038451); [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202607240385.html)
- Bookipi/Upwork analysis (vendor): Nigerian freelancers average $163 per job in some categories, 23rd globally for rates — [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/?p=53015)
- Indicative remote pay (2026 guide, directional): virtual assistants $300–$2,000/month; customer support $10–$25/hour — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/remote-jobs-in-nigeria-top-platforms-that-actually-pay-2026-guide/)
- Modelled salary database: median remote customer support representative in Nigeria ~$30,703/yr; customer service representative ~$10,599/yr (3x discrepancy for similar roles) — [Plane](https://plane.com/salaries/customer-support-representative/nigeria); [Plane](https://plane.com/salaries/customer-service-representative/nigeria)
- Foreign employer budget for an experienced Lagos support agent/VA hired directly: ₦150k–₦300k/month — [HiveDesk](https://www.hivedesk.com/salary-guides/nigeria)
- A Jobberman remote VA listing is commission-only, illustrating weak local pay structures — [Jobberman](https://www.jobberman.com/jobs/customer-service-support/remote/contract)
- Payment barriers: freelancers face "payment barriers, currency volatility, and high transfer fees"; fintechs (Cleva, Grey etc.) positioning to bridge — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1038451); [Cleva (vendor)](https://getcleva.com/blog/how-to-receive-payments-from-upwork-in-nigeria-in-2025)
- PayPal is "effectively unusable" for Nigerian freelancers' earnings; personal Nigerian accounts cannot receive payments; deposit fees and conversion markups are the main obstacles — [TechEconomy (2026)](https://techeconomy.ng/best-platform-to-withdraw-from-upwork-in-nigeria-2026)
- Payoneer stack: 1% receiving fee + withdrawal fee + currency conversion markup up to 4.5% — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/?p=1943)
- 2025 tax reform: Nigerian tax residents taxed on worldwide income; freelancers with foreign clients must report it — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/what-you-need-to-know-as-tech-freelancers-to-brace-for-2025-tax-reform/)
- Global platform trend (not Nigeria-specific): Oyster 2025 Global Hiring and Impact Report shows a 46% increase in contractor engagements and a tenfold rise in consulting hires YoY — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/contractor-consulting-roles-surge-as-global-hiring-shifts); Remote.com's 2025 contractor/freelance report exists but country breakdown not retrieved — [Remote.com](https://remote.com/en-ph/resources/research/contractor-management-report)
- FG–BPO partnership is channelling 3MTT fellows into BPO (customer-support-type) jobs — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/technology/fg-bpo-companies-partner-to-create-jobs-for-3mtt-fellows/)
- One self-reported Nigerian profile: customer support specialist/VA at $24,000/yr ($20/hr), 3 years' experience — [web3.career](https://web3.career/@oscarjr)

### Inferences
- The realistic entry ladder for a weak-CV Nigerian user is BPO/customer support or VA work (local naira or dollar), then specialised remote roles (support engineering, data entry/annotation, content, design) once a portfolio exists. The product should pair any remote-work path with a "getting paid" module (Payoneer/Grey/Cleva, invoicing, tax residency) since payments are a documented, recurring barrier.
- Pay figures should be shown as ranges with source labels; the 3x discrepancy in modelled databases means a single "average" would be misleading.

### Gaps
- No data obtained on Andela, Turing, Toptal acceptance rates or on Deel/Oyster/Remote.com country rankings for Nigeria (search budget exhausted).
- No primary Upwork/Payoneer country report for Nigeria found; no data on time-zone or trust barriers beyond anecdote.

## Key Question 4: Course and credential sources — free/low-cost options, recognition, outcomes

### Takeaway
Three sources have the strongest combination of (near-)free access and published outcome data: Google Career Certificates on Coursera (1m graduates; ~70–75% self-reported positive outcome; financial aid waives fees; Nigeria-localised Coursera Plus at $24/month or $160/year), ALX Africa (free; self-reports 347,100 graduates and 63% employed within six months, but an unofficial 70–80% dropout estimate), and 3MTT (free, 390,000+ fellows selected across three cohorts, but the minister concedes placements — ~15,000 — are "small"). Bootcamp fees (AltSchool, Utiva, Decagon) are poorly documented; Udemy/LinkedIn Learning naira pricing was not found.

### Cited Findings

**Google Career Certificates / Coursera**
- Over 1 million graduates across nine programs; 350,000+ in the US; ~70% of US graduates report a positive career outcome (new job, promotion or raise); 40% were unemployed or underemployed at start; 150+ companies in the Grow with Google Employer Consortium — [Google Keyword blog, May 2025 impact report](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/grow-with-google/google-career-certificates-impact-report-may-2025/); PDF — [Google impact report](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/new_google_career_certificates_impact_report.pdf)
- Coursera's listing cites 75% of graduates reporting a positive outcome within six months (different measure from Google's 70%) — [Coursera Google Career Certificates collection](https://www.coursera.org/collections/google-career-certificates); [Coursera blog, 1 million graduates](https://blog.coursera.org/1-million-graduates-the-real-world-impact-of-google-career-certificates)
- Coursera financial aid: fee waiver covering course content and certificate for learners who cannot afford to pay; requires a brief application describing financial circumstances and motivation — [Coursera blog](https://blog.coursera.org/courseras-financial-aid-what-it-is-and-who-is); Nigerian guides say approval typically within ~15 days (aggregator claim, not Coursera documentation) — [ICANStudent](https://www.icanstudent.com/unlock-your-future-free-google-career-certificates-for-nigerian-students/amp/); [NaijaSabi](https://naijasabi.com.ng/free-online-courses-certificates-nigerians-2026/)
- Claimed 100,000 free Google Career Certificate spots for Africans aged 18–35 in 2026 (unverified against Google/Coursera) — [Opportunity Inside](https://opportunityinside.com/blog/grow-with-google-scholarship-africa-2026.html)
- NITDA/Google learning scholarship for Nigerian citizens (2024): six-month Coursera licence — [Scholarships.plus](https://scholarships.plus/scholarship/nitda-google-learning-scholarships-for-nigerian-students-2024/)
- Coursera localised Nigeria pricing: Coursera Plus monthly $24 (described as 60% cheaper), annual $160; individual course $31 — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1019597); compare US-market Coursera Plus $59/month or $399/year — [ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/coursera/pricing)
- Coursera Career Academy (institution-licensed): entry-level Professional Certificates from Google, IBM, Meta mapped to roles — [Coursera blog, Introducing Career Academy](https://blog.coursera.org/introducing-career-academy)

**ALX Africa**
- ALX self-reports 347,100 graduates, 257,900 "moving into work"; 63% of graduates secure employment within six months; 154,300 in wage employment, 43,400 freelancing or building businesses — [ALX news-press](https://www.alxafrica.com/news-press/africas-talent-was-never-the-problem-alx-expands-access-across-the-continent/)
- Earlier ALX material claimed ~85% employed within six months on a base of ~85,000 graduates since 2021 (not comparable to the 63%) — [TechInAfrica](https://www.techinafrica.com/alx-tech-hub-revolutionizing-sas-tech-education-landscape/); [CNBC Africa](https://cnbcafrica.com/media/6329967198112/this-initiative-is-upskilling-sas-youth-for-the-global-job-market)
- ALX does not publish completion rates; a third-party review says "community data and alumni surveys consistently point to a 70–80% dropout rate" (unofficial, source unclear) — [Mctaba review](https://www.mctaba.com/learn/africa/alx-africa-review)
- Mastercard Foundation-backed launch of ALX's all-tech programmes for 32,000+ learners across Africa — [Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/saving-lives-and-livelihoods/alx-kick-starts-inaugural-all-tech-training-programs-for-over-32000-learners-across-africa)
- ALX Nigeria programme page and testimonials (AWS Cloud Practitioner, Salesforce associate passes; self-reported, no hiring outcome) — [ALX Nigeria](https://nigeria.alxafrica.com); [ALX programmes](https://alxafrica.com/?p=11436); independent reviews — [Trustpilot](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/www.alxafrica.com)

**3MTT (free, FG-funded)**
- Phase 1: 30,000 fellows — [Technology Times](https://technologytimes.ng/3mtt-30000-fellows-ready-for-job-market/); Phase 2: 270,000 fellows — [NAN](https://nannews.ng/ministry-commences-phase-2-of-3mtt-initiative-selects-270000-fellows/); Cohort 3: 90,000 — [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/?p=37449)
- Minister Bosun Tijani (Dec 2025): programme has supported nearly 3,000 interns and helped place over 15,000 trainees; "We know the number is small" — [Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/12/12/how-3mtt-will-translate-to-jobs-bosun-tijani/)

- Industry view: 3MTT "has undeniably succeeded in creating a massive top-of-funnel pipeline" but not the shortage of experienced professionals — [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/beyond-entry-level-talent-why-nigeria-must-turn-digital-skills-into-economic-growth); analysis — [TechCabal (Oct 2024)](https://techcabal.com/2024/10/04/3mtt-nigeria-tech-talent-powerhouse/)

## Key Question 5: Design lessons from existing career-path products; what makes paths stick

### Takeaway
Existing products converge on the same skeleton: pick a target role → show its skill requirements → sequence 3–8 courses into a 3–8-month, 3–10 hr/week path → anchor each unit on a hands-on project with fast human feedback. Self-paced open courses complete at single-digit rates (median 6.5%), Udacity's project-plus-mentor Nanodegrees report 34% (and >60% with personalised mentorship), and sponsored or cohort programmes report 50–90% — but most cohort figures are vendor-published and confounded by selection.

### Cited Findings
- Coursera Career Academy: learners "review each role's skill requirements, and enroll in the entry-level Professional Certificates and Guided Projects mapped to each role"; certificates from Google, IBM, Meta; Guided Projects build skills in under two hours — [Coursera blog](https://blog.coursera.org/introducing-career-academy); [GoCertify](https://www.gocertify.com/articles/coursera-launches-certificate-and-career-academy-program)
- Career Academy certificate duration: 6–8 months at 3–10 hours/week for learners without prior experience — [UTPB Career Academy page](https://careers.utpb.edu/resources/career-academy/); a partner FAQ estimates 3–4 months — [Texas Wesleyan FAQ](https://txwes.edu/continuing-education/coursera-career-academy/faqs)
- Udacity Nanodegrees: every course contains a hands-on project with clearly defined objectives; programs run 12–24 weeks; projects are reviewed by a mentor with feedback — [DevOpsCube review](https://devopscube.com/udacity-review-nanodegree/)
- Udacity project reviews: 61,000 projects evaluated in 12 months, 50% within 2.5 hours and 88% within 24 hours of submission — [Georgia Tech Lucy Labs case study](https://lucylabs.gatech.edu/expert-evaluation-of-300-projects-per-day)
- Udacity: "our Nanodegree programs have a 34% graduation rate"; with personalised mentorship "cohorts have commonly exceeded 60% graduation rates" (undated blog) — [Udacity blog](https://www.udacity.com/blog/?p=5498); [MentorCruise on Udacity's mentorship model](https://mentorcruise.com/blog/the-mentorship-model-at-udacity-cb11a6b1e894/)
- Bertelsmann-sponsored Udacity scholarship cohorts: 59% completion (Deep Learning; Data Science) — [Bertelsmann University](https://bertelsmann-university.com/news/bertelsmann-scholarship-initiative-high-first-round-completion-rate.html); [Bertelsmann University](https://www.bertelsmann-university.com/news/udacity-data-science-scholarship-program-successfully-completed.html)
- Udacity graduate-outcomes post (content not retrieved) — [Udacity blog](https://www.udacity.com/blog/?p=20523)
- Fuzu (Kenya/Nigeria): "fully personalised user experience based on a 360° assessment of skills, personality and interest"; AI matching plus end-to-end application guidance; 100+ courses; instant feedback on every application; access to certified career coaches; 3m+ registered users, 2,000+ employers; revenue from premium subscriptions, employer postings, ads — [TechCabal (2022)](https://techcabal.com/2022/02/12/fuzu-uses-ai-to-disrupt-job-search-and-recruitment-in-nigeria/); [Fuzu forum](https://www.fuzu.com/forum/article/find-grow-and-thrive-with-fuzu-africa-s-1-career-platform); [HapaKenya (2015)](https://hapakenya.com/2015/09/21/fuzu-ltd-launches-a-career-coaching-and-employment-platform-for-kenyan-job-seekers)
- Fuzu's AI Powered Job Connections Program pairs AI job matching, personalised coaching, targeted skilling and light-touch employer engagement for disadvantaged youth — [Nairobi Garage](https://nairobigarage.com/?p=15985)
- Lightcast Open Skills Taxonomy: 34,000+ (blog) / 35,000+ (API page) standardised skills from hundreds of millions of postings, profiles and resumes, updated every two weeks; 3-tier hierarchy (category → subcategory → skill); Career Pathways API offered; free for nonprofits; free tier reportedly capped at 50 skill-extraction calls/month — [Lightcast blog](https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/open-skills-taxonomy); [Lightcast API access](https://lightcast.io/open-skills/access); [Lightcast docs](https://docs.lightcast.io/lightcast-api/docs/general-taxonomies-skills); [JobsPipe write-up](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/lightcast-api.md)
- MOOC completion baseline: median 6.5% of enrollees (9.8% of active learners) across 39 courses, range 0.9–36.1% (Jordan, IRRODL 2014); HarvardX/MITx first year ~5.1% of registrations earned certificates; Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente (Science 2019) found no improvement over six years — summarised in [Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/cohort-vs-self-paced-completion-study) and [Guroo Academy](https://guroo.academy/post/cohort-based-vs-self-paced)
- Vendor cohort claims: Disco cites 85–90% completion for cohort-based learning vs 3–15% for MOOCs — [Disco](https://www.disco.co/blog/cohort-based-learning-vs-mooc); Ruzuku's own data: 64.2% cohort vs 48.2% self-paced, and (2026, 27,156 courses) 53.4% scheduled cohorts vs 41.9% self-paced — [Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/cohort-vs-self-paced-completion-study); an 85% figure for one Harvard Business School Online course is anecdotal — [Learning Revolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/cohort-based-courses/)
- ALX employment outcomes and 3MTT placement figures (see Q4) indicate that large free programmes achieve scale but not reliably placement.

### Inferences
- Design pattern to borrow: role-first entry (not course-first), explicit time budget (e.g., "4 months at 5 hrs/week"), one project per milestone, and fast feedback on submissions — Udacity's turnaround data suggests same-day feedback is a measurable commitment.
- Expect single-digit completion for purely self-paced paths; adding scheduled cohort dates, a mentor/reviewer touchpoint, or sponsorship (a "someone paid for this" effect, as in Bertelsmann cohorts) plausibly moves completion into the 35–60% range — but the evidence is vendor-heavy and selection-confounded, so pilot and measure.
- Lightcast's taxonomy (free for nonprofits; limited free tier) is a practical backbone for mapping CV skills to target-role skills; a Nigeria-specific layer (ICAN/ACCA, NYSC, local tools) would have to be added manually.

### Gaps
- No material found on LinkedIn Learning Career Explorer or Grow with Google path structure specifics; no peer-reviewed RCT evidence on reminders, planning prompts or accountability nudges for course completion (search budget exhausted before these queries).
- No independent, controlled comparison of cohort vs self-paced completion was found.

## Key Question 6: How should CV "weakness" be defined measurably?

### Takeaway
Two complementary industry rubrics exist: Jobscan's job-specific keyword match rate (hard skills > education > job title > soft skills > other keywords; target 75–80%) and Resume Worded's job-agnostic quality score (impact/quantification, growth/leadership, brevity, action verbs, buzzwords, section completeness, ATS compatibility; 85+ "good", 90+ "passes all checks"). Harvard OCS supplies the editorial rules (action verbs, results over duties, skimmability). A measurable Nigerian rubric can combine these three.

### Cited Findings
- Jobscan match rate is a 0–100% score against one selected job description; factors in order of importance: hard skills, education (only when the posting asks for an advanced degree), job title, soft skills, then other keywords; word count and quantified results do not count toward it; recommended minimum 75% — [Jobscan tutorial](https://Jobscan.co/jobscan-tutorial)
- Jobscan later "generally recommend[s] a match rate score of 80%" while 75% "can still work well" — [Jobscan blog](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/what-jobscan-match-rate-should-i-aim-for/)
- Third-party description of Jobscan scoring: keyword overlap, keyword frequency, required credentials, formatting/parsing signals; scores vary between scanners and runs and are not a true ATS pass/fail — [JobShinobi](https://jobshinobi.com/blog/jobscan-resume-scanner-how-it-works); [JobShinobi](https://jobshinobi.com/blog/what-is-a-good-jobscan-match-rate)
- Resume Worded score bands: 85+ is good, ideally 90+; 90+ "passes all checks"; 80–84 still has issues a recruiter would notice — [Resume Worded score guide](https://resumeworded.com/score-guide)
- Resume Worded check categories: "impact and quantified achievements, growth and leadership signals, brevity, action verbs, buzzwords and filler, section completeness, and ATS compatibility" (20+ checks); another page says 30–40 checks depending on career level; flags weak openers such as "Assisted" or "Helped"; scoring adapts to seniority — [Resume Worded LLM info page](https://resumeworded.com/llm-info); [Resume Worded free review](https://resumeworded.com/free-resume-review); [Resume Worded Score My Resume](https://www.resumeworded.com/score.php)
- Resume Worded criticises Jobscan for treating soft skills like hard skills; argues soft skills should be evidenced through accomplishments, not listed — [Resume Worded compare page](https://resumeworded.com/compare)
- Harvard OCS top resume mistakes: "using passive language instead of 'action' words", "not well organized or easy to skim", "not demonstrating results"; guidance to be fact-based and "quantify and qualify"; categorised action-verb lists (Leadership: Accomplished, Achieved, Led, Increased, Spearheaded; Communication, etc.) — [Harvard OCS undergraduate guide (PDF)](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu:443/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2022/04/undergrad_resumes_and_cover_letters-1.pdf); [Harvard OCS 2023 guide (PDF)](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu:443/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2023/08/College-resume-and-cover-letter-4.pdf); [Harvard Extension guide (PDF)](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu:443/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2022/03/hes-resume-cover-letter-guide.pdf); [Harvard OCS HES resource page](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/hes-create-impactful-resumes-and-cover-letters/)
- Nigerian employer context relevant to "missing certifications" checks: ICAN/ACCA explicitly preferred/required by Big Four Nigeria for audit/tax/advisory — [Profolio.ng](https://www.profolio.ng/accountant/best-certifications); cybersecurity skills deemed critical by 87% of Nigerian employers — [LSETF](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678)

### Inferences
- A measurable "CV weakness" score for the product can be the sum of: (a) target-role keyword coverage (Jobscan-style, weighted hard skills > title > soft skills; computed against a Lightcast-style skill list for the chosen role); (b) achievement quality (share of bullets with a number; share starting with a strong action verb; penalise "Assisted/Helped/Responsible for"); (c) completeness (contact, summary, skills, education, experience, certifications sections present; NYSC status for Nigerian graduates); (d) gaps/tenure flags; (e) parse-ability (single column, standard headings, no tables/images, .docx/.pdf); (f) role-specific missing credentials (e.g., no ICAN/ACCA for accounting target; no Google/CompTIA/Cisco-type cert for cybersecurity target). Each component can trigger a specific learning-path module, which is what makes "your CV is weak, here is a path" actionable rather than merely diagnostic.
- Resume Worded's seniority-adaptive thresholds are worth copying: a fresh NYSC graduate should not be penalised for lacking leadership signals the way a 10-year professional would.

### Gaps
- HBS/Accenture "Hidden Workers" ATS research and Jobscan's claimed ATS-adoption statistics were not retrieved (search budget exhausted); no Nigeria-specific research on ATS adoption by Nigerian employers was found.
- Exact weights and thresholds (page length, bullet counts, % quantified) used by Jobscan and Resume Worded are not published in the passages found.
