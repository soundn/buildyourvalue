# Monetizing a Consumer Subscription Product in Nigeria (2025–2026): Pricing, Payments, Trust, Compliance, Unit Economics

Research date: 20 October 2026. Scope: an AI career platform (CV builder + emailed job matches + course recommendations) for Nigerian job seekers, many unemployed or low-income.

Method note: all findings below came from web search result snippets; direct page fetches (WebFetch and curl) were blocked by the sandbox proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), so I could not open primary pages such as paystack.com/pricing or the NDPC Guidance Notice itself. Where a figure comes from a secondary/aggregator source I say so. Several queries (Nigeria Tax Act 2025 small-company CIT threshold, NBS unemployment data, Alison/ALX affiliate terms, Canva Pro and LinkedIn Premium naira prices, B2B2C precedent deals) could not be run because the per-turn search budget was exhausted; these are listed under Gaps.

Exchange-rate anchor used for USD conversions: the naira averaged about ₦1,556/$ at the official (NFEM) market in H1 2025 — [BusinessDay (clone mirror)](https://clone.businessday.ng/?p=1067036); it opened 2025 at about ₦1,541/$ and closed 2025 at about ₦1,436/$ per CBN data — [BusinessDay](https://clone.businessday.ng/?p=1067036), [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/?p=1848937) (Daily Trust gives ₦1,436.31; AllAfrica gives ~₦1,445 — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601020184.html)). I use ≈₦1,500/$ as a 2025 rule of thumb; conversions are approximate.

## Key Question 1: Pricing benchmarks — what Nigerians pay for digital subscriptions; income sanity checks; CV-writing prices

### Takeaway
Mass-market Nigerian consumer subscriptions in 2025 cluster at ₦1,300–₦2,500/month (≈$0.85–$1.65): Spotify ₦1,300 (possibly ₦1,600 by 2026), Apple Music ₦1,300, YouTube Premium ₦1,700, Netflix Mobile ₦2,500, Showmax mobile ₦1,600. Anything above ₦5,000/month is "premium" territory (Netflix Standard ₦6,500, Google AI Pro ₦28,500, ChatGPT Plus ₦31,500) against a ₦70,000 minimum wage. One-off professional CV writing is priced ₦10,000–₦30,000, which is the most directly comparable benchmark for a CV product.

### Inferences
- A "Spotify-tier" price point of ₦1,300–₦2,000/month (≈$0.90–$1.35) is what global consumer brands have converged on for Nigeria after repeated 2024–2025 hikes; a job-seeker product should not expect to clear that by much for a monthly plan. A ₦1,500–₦2,000/month core plan is roughly 2–3% of minimum wage and equivalent to one 1.8GB data bundle.
- Because one-off CV writing is already priced at ₦10,000–₦30,000 by Nigerian providers, an AI CV builder can plausibly charge a one-off ₦2,000–₦5,000 "finished CV" fee (far below the human alternative) in addition to, or instead of, a subscription — this also matches how unemployed users think (pay for an outcome, not a month).
- Price hikes in 2025 (data +50%, YouTube +54%, Spotify +44%, Netflix +21%) show consumer wallets are squeezed; Spotify's 2026 stance ("don't raise prices, grow payers via local pricing and telco partnerships") is a signal that volume, not ARPU, is the lever in Nigeria.

## Key Question 2: Payment infrastructure — Paystack/Flutterwave recurring billing, fees, settlement, alternatives, app-store cuts, and which model suits low-income users

### Takeaway
Paystack charges 1.5% + ₦100 on local cards, capped at ₦2,000, and waives the ₦100 on transactions under ₦2,500 — so a sub-₦2,500 charge costs only 1.5% (₦15 on ₦1,000) while a ₦2,500–₦5,000 charge costs 3.5–5.5% effective. Recurring billing works via tokenised card authorisations (first charge needs 2FA) or NIBSS direct debit mandates; mobile wallets have no native recurring mandate. Vendor estimates put card renewal failure at roughly 8–14% per cycle in Nigeria, mostly from insufficient balance. Flutterwave raised local fees in April 2025 (sources conflict: 1.4%+₦100 vs 2.0%). App-store billing takes 15% (Google subscriptions; Apple Small Business Program).

### Cited Findings

**Paystack fees (Nigeria)**
- Local cards/transactions: 1.5% + ₦100, capped at ₦2,000; the ₦100 flat fee is waived for transactions under ₦2,500 — [Paystack support: "What are Paystack's transaction charges"](https://support2.paystack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009881920-What-are-Paystack-s-transaction-charges); also summarised at [learnwithhasan](https://learnwithhasan.com/payment-gateways/paystack/) and [Kolonell 2026 comparison](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-vs-flutterwave-merchant-fees-lagos-2026).
- International cards: 3.9% + ₦100 (Visa/Mastercard/Verve); 4.5% for American Express — same Paystack support page.
- Transfers (payouts): ₦10 for transfers ≤₦5,000; tiered ₦10/₦25/₦50 by size — same Paystack support page.
- Chargeback fee ₦2,500 (appears in support docs, not main pricing page) — [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-vs-flutterwave-checkout-fees-tiers-lagos-2026).
- Schools get a discounted 0.7% capped at ₦1,500 — [Paystack support](https://support2.paystack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009881920-What-are-Paystack-s-transaction-charges) (relevant if the platform ever structures as an education provider; unlikely to apply).

**Paystack recurring billing mechanics**
- Subscriptions API: create a plan, create a subscription, listen for events; supports Card and Direct Debit (Nigeria) only — [Paystack docs: Subscriptions](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/).
- A subscription requires a prior successful charge because it bills a saved card authorisation; local regulation requires 2FA on the initial card transaction before subsequent charges; if the customer has several authorisations you can pass a specific `authorization_code` — [Paystack docs: Recurring Charges](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/recurring-charges).
- Direct debit skips the initial charge: initiate via the Initialize Authorization endpoint and save the authorisation from webhooks once the customer approves — [Paystack docs: Recurring Charges](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/recurring-charges).
- Only the email used to create an authorisation can charge it; if the user changes email on your system, the authorisation can no longer be charged — [Paystack docs](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/recurring-charges).
- Paystack Direct Debit is built with NIBSS, Nigeria-only, supported by 20+ banks; mandate is created by the customer making a small bank transfer to a NIBSS-provided account — [Paystack blog: Direct Debit waitlist](https://paystack.com/blog/product/direct-debit-waitlist); [Paystack support: Direct Debit overview](https://support.paystack.com/hc/en-us/articles/12542843851420-Direct-Debit); [Condia](https://thecondia.com/paystack-direct-debit-nibss-nigeria/).
- A third-party NIBSS eMandate integration guide (Lendsqr) describes a ₦50 activation transfer and ₦100 debit attempt and says "due to the minimum transaction fees involved... transactions below ₦5,000 are generally not cost-effective" — [Lendsqr docs](https://docs.lendsqr.com/receiving-payments-with-nibss-direct-debit). (Third-party; Paystack's own direct-debit fee was not found.)
- Common silent-failure causes for Paystack subscriptions: cancelled/disabled subscription, non-reusable authorisation, expired card, plan amount changed after creation, arrears after retries stopped — [mctaba](https://mctaba.com/learn/paystack/paystack-subscription-not-charging); reusable vs non-reusable authorisations explained at [mctaba](https://mctaba.com/learn/paystack/reusable-vs-non-reusable-paystack-authorizations).

**Flutterwave fees (Nigeria) — sources conflict**
- One 2026 comparison says Flutterwave raised local card fees from 1.4% to a combined 2.0% (1.4% transaction + 0.6% platform fee) in April 2025 — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/interswitch-vs-paystack-vs-flutterwave-how-nigerias-top-payment-gateways-really-compare-in-2026/); [Kolonell fee breakdown](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-vs-flutterwave-fees-breakdown-nigeria-2026).
- Other 2026 guides list local NGN cards at 1.4% + ₦100 capped at ₦2,000 and note a local NGN fee increase effective 11 April 2025 affecting cards, bank transfer, USSD, OPay and NQR — [Kolonell pricing](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-vs-flutterwave-pricing-nigeria-2026); [Awajis](https://awajis.com/online-payment-gateways-in-nigeria/).
- International cards quoted at 4.8% in one source and 3.8% + ₦100 (no cap) in another — same sources.
- Settlement: Paystack and Flutterwave both settle in roughly one business day; new accounts may start on longer (up to 48h) cycles — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/best-payment-systems-for-online-business-in-nigeria-2026-guide/).

**Other rails (all secondary sources)**
- Monnify (verified June 2026 per the guide): 1.5% capped ₦2,000 for bank transfers; 1.5% capped ₦1,500 for cards; virtual-account fees capped ₦1,000; no setup/monthly fee — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/best-payment-systems-for-online-business-in-nigeria-2026-guide/).
- OPay Business: 1.5% capped ₦2,000 on local cards, no monthly fee — [Condia](https://thecondia.com/best-payment-gateway-nigeria/).
- Interswitch: pricing negotiated, not public; local cards historically ~1.5–2%; integration free on current developer platform; WebPay being folded into Quickteller Business/IPG — [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/interswitch-vs-paystack-vs-flutterwave-how-nigerias-top-payment-gateways-really-compare-in-2026/).

**Recurring failure and churn (vendor estimates, methodology undisclosed)**
- Card penetration in African markets below 10%; subscription infrastructure built for credit cards breaks down — [Spotflow](https://www.spotflow.one/blog/how-subscription-payments-in-africa-work).
- Mobile money has no native auto-debit mandate; each cycle needs fresh authorisation, manufacturing involuntary churn — [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/recurring-mandate-mobile-money-subscription-billing-lagos-2026).
- Card renewal failure estimates: 8–14% involuntary renewal failure; tokenised cards ~8%; mobile money ~22%; manual monthly retry 20–35%; top failure cause is insufficient balance (55% of cases) — [Kolonell dunning](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/dunning-failed-recurring-mobile-money-payments-nigeria-2026); [Kolonell recurring](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/recurring-subscription-billing-momo-nigeria-2026). Retry/dunning recovers 20–38% of failed payments; reminders + tokenised cards could bring involuntary churn to ~4% vs 12–18% without — same sources. Treat all as directional.
- Anecdotal: Nigerian banks cap FX spending on naira cards so international (USD) SaaS charges frequently fail and users churn silently — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/its-not-your-nigerian-customers-ghosting-you-it-s-your-checkout-4aaae911c8).

**App-store billing**
- Google Play charges 15% on subscriptions from day one (since Jan 2022); in 2026 the US/UK/EEA split it into 10% service + 5% billing fee — [PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/google-play-subscription-fees-2026-real-math); [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2021/10/21/google-play-store-drops-fees-on-subscriptions-and-content/).
- Apple Small Business Program: 15% for developers earning under $1M/year; standard rate 30% first subscription year, 15% thereafter — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/small-business-program.md); [Glassfy](https://docs.glassfy.io/docs/reduced-platform-commission-for-new-and-small-publishers).

### Inferences
- Fee cliff: Paystack's ₦100 flat fee is waived under ₦2,500, so a ₦1,000 charge costs ₦15 (1.5%), ₦2,000 costs ₦30 (1.5%), but ₦2,500 costs ₦137.50 (5.5%) and ₦5,000 costs ₦175 (3.5%). Price points of ₦999/₦1,499/₦1,999/₦2,499 or ≥₦5,000 (quarterly/annual) are fee-efficient; ₦2,500–₦4,000 is the worst zone.
- Web checkout via Paystack (1.5–2%) is far cheaper than in-app billing (15%); the platform should sell on web/PWA and keep the mobile app for consumption, or price the in-app plan higher.
- For unemployed users with volatile balances, "insufficient funds" is the dominant failure mode, so: (a) charge on predictable dates (e.g., let user pick a day), (b) implement dunning with 3–5 retries over 7–10 days plus WhatsApp/SMS reminders, (c) offer pay-as-you-go top-ups via bank transfer/USSD as a fallback, and (d) prefer prepaid passes (weekly/one-off) over auto-renewal for the lowest tier.
- NIBSS direct debit is poorly suited to sub-₦5,000 tickets (per Lendsqr's guidance) — use it only for annual/quarterly plans.

### Gaps
- Could not open paystack.com/pricing to confirm bank-transfer, USSD and direct-debit fee lines or official settlement SLA; figures above come from Paystack's support page via search snippets and third-party summaries.
- Flutterwave's current local card rate (1.4%+₦100 vs 2.0%) is unresolved; check flutterwave.com/ng/pricing.
- No independent (non-vendor) data on failed-recurring-charge rates for Nigerian cards; Paystack/Flutterwave do not appear to publish this.
- Apple/Google do not publish Nigeria-specific storefront pricing/tax handling in sources found.

## Key Question 3: Alternative revenue — employer fees, course affiliates, B2B2C, sponsorship; freemium conversion and churn benchmarks

### Takeaway
Employer-side pricing in Nigeria is anchored by Jobberman (free basic listing, ₦100,000 incl. VAT for a standard listing), leaving room for a cheaper "featured candidates/recruiter access" tier. Course affiliates pay roughly 10–45% (Coursera) and 10–15% (Udemy). Freemium free-to-paid conversion benchmarks are 2–8% depending on study (median 8% in a 200-product survey; 3.7% average per First Page Sage; ~2% implied for ChatGPT), with time-limited trials converting far better (14–25%). No emerging-market-specific churn benchmarks were found.

### Cited Findings
- Jobberman Nigeria 2025: Basic Job free; Standard Job ₦100,000 incl. VAT; Executive Recruitment per position — [Jobberman brochure 2025](https://static.jobberman.com/Jobberman_Nigeria_Product_Brochure_2025.pdf); [2024 rate card](https://static.jobberman.com/jobberman-nigeria-product-rate-card-2024.pdf). MyJobMag describes itself as a marketplace that does not place candidates or screen employers; employer pricing not published — [Brands.ng MyJobMag review](https://brands.ng/myjobmag-review-2026-is-it-legit-for-job-seekers-in-nigeria/).
- Coursera affiliate: 10–45% depending on product — standalone courses ~20%, Specializations/Professional Certificates 45%, Coursera Plus 10%; 30-day cookie; commissions also earned on existing members buying a different course — [Learning Revolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/e-learning-affiliate-programs/); [UpPromote Coursera](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/coursera).
- Udemy affiliate: base 10%, rising to ~15% for high performers, plus affiliate-only promos; an older (2022) article cited 20% and a 7-day cookie — [UpPromote Udemy](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/udemy/); [Affiliate Marketer Training, 2022](https://www.affiliatemarketertraining.com/15-best-education-affiliate-programs-2/).
- Freemium conversion: 200-product survey (ChartMogul/Growth Unhinged/ProductLed) median 8%, ~25% of products below 2.5%, ~25% at 10–15% — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium). First Page Sage (80+ SaaS clients, 2021–2025): average 3.7%, range 2.6% (EdTech) to 5.8% (RegTech) — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-saas/). Lenny Rachitsky (1,000+ products): self-serve freemium 6–8%, sales-assisted 10–15% — [Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-to-premium). Mewayz (138k users, Mar 2026): freemium 2.1–4.5% vs time-limited trials 14–25% — [Mewayz](https://mewayz.com/cs/blog/free-tier-conversion-rates-across-saas-what-the-data-actually-shows). ChatGPT: ~12–15M paid vs ~800M weekly users ≈ 2% — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/freemium-is-back-the-ai-edition-but-youll-still-probably-need-50-million-active-users-for-freemium-to-actually-work-as-a-business-model/).
- Involuntary churn (payment-failure) estimates for Nigeria: 8–14% per cycle for cards, higher for wallets (vendor estimates; see Q2) — [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/dunning-failed-recurring-mobile-money-payments-nigeria-2026).

### Inferences
- With 2–5% free-to-paid conversion and a ₦1,500–₦2,000 ARPU, consumer subscription revenue alone is thin: 100,000 free users → 2,000–5,000 payers → ₦3–10M/month gross (≈$2,000–$6,700). Employer-side and affiliate revenue are therefore structurally important, not optional.
- Jobberman's ₦100,000 standard listing implies SMEs will pay ₦10,000–₦30,000 for a cheaper featured listing or shortlist of pre-scored candidates; "pay per qualified applicant" is also plausible.
- EdTech's lower freemium conversion (2.6% per First Page Sage) is the closest analogue to a career platform; time-boxed trials (e.g., 7-day full access after CV upload) are likely to outperform a perpetual free tier for conversion, but a free tier is still needed for trust (see Q4).
- Affiliate income per user is small in absolute terms (Udemy courses often sell at ~$10–15 in promos → ~$1–2 commission) but Coursera Professional Certificates at 45% are attractive; the higher-value play is B2B2C bulk licensing.

## Key Question 4: Trust and fraud — perception of paying upfront for job help; trust signals

### Takeaway
"Pay to apply" is the single most recognised job-scam pattern in Nigeria; the EFCC, Nigeria Immigration Service, Dangote Cement and others have publicly warned that legitimate employers never charge candidates, and reported fraudulent fees range from ₦5,000 to ₦500,000. Any product that asks job seekers for money must visibly separate itself from "recruitment fees" — charge for tools (CV, matching, coaching), never for access to a specific job or application. No survey quantifying Nigerian job-seekers' trust was found.

### Cited Findings
- Legitimate employers do not charge candidates application, recruitment or processing fees; government jobs are not exempt — [BusinessDay opinion](https://businessday.ng/opinion/article/the-rise-of-recruitment-fraud-in-nigeria-how-job-seekers-can-stay-safe/).
- Upfront fees demanded by scammers for "processing, training, onboarding or medical tests" range ₦5,000–₦500,000 — [News Verifier Africa, 2024](https://newsverifierafrica.com/rise-of-recruitment-scams-nigeria-2024/).
- EFCC: scammers asked applicants for a processing fee then accommodation/transport fees totalling ₦97,000; EFCC "does not and will never ask for money from prospective candidates" — [TheCable](https://thecable.ng/dont-pay-n97000-to-scammers-offering-efcc-jobs); [EFCC release](https://www.efcc.gov.ng/news-and-information/news-release/2036-efcc-recruitment-beware-of-scammers).
- Dangote Cement warned that fake listings were circulating and it "never request[s] candidates to pay a fee before they are considered" — [Global Cement](https://globalcement.com/news/12704-dangote-cement-warns-public-against-recruitment-scam).
- Scam ads spread via social media, WhatsApp groups and unregulated job websites, often using Gmail/Yahoo addresses or impersonating known HR staff; some are fronts for trafficking — [News Verifier Africa](https://newsverifierafrica.com/rise-of-recruitment-scams-nigeria-2024/); [ITWeb Africa](https://itweb.africa/content/KzQenqj8Qy27Zd2r).
- Jobberman and MyJobMag both let employers post basic jobs free and (MyJobMag) do not screen employers — [Jobberman brochure](https://static.jobberman.com/Jobberman_Nigeria_Product_Brochure_2025.pdf); [Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/myjobmag-review-2026-is-it-legit-for-job-seekers-in-nigeria/).

### Inferences
- Framing matters more than price: position paid tiers as "CV/career tools" and "faster, better-targeted matches", and state prominently "we never charge you to apply, and no employer on our platform may charge you". Any job that requires payment should be filtered out and reported.
- Trust signals that address the specific scam tells above: a verified corporate email domain (not Gmail), a CAC registration number (RC/BN) in the footer, a physical address, a named founding team, NDPC compliance statement, Paystack/Flutterwave checkout (recognised brands), a free tier first, and a money-back/no-questions refund within 7 days. A verified-employer badge and a public "scam watch" feature would differentiate from Jobberman/MyJobMag.
- Since large employers (Dangote, EFCC) are routinely impersonated, aggregated listings should link to the employer's own careers page/domain and show source provenance.

- No survey or quantitative data on Nigerian job seekers' willingness to pay for career tools or trust levels (NAPTIP/NBS/fraud-platform data not found).

## Key Question 5: Legal and compliance — NDPA 2023 / GAID 2025 / NDPC registration; consent & email marketing; CAC; Startup Act; VAT/tax; AI and recruitment-agency rules; scraping

### Takeaway
A CV platform is a data controller under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the GAID 2025 (effective replacement of the NDPR from 19 Sept 2025). If it processes data of more than ~200 data subjects in six months plus other factors, it is a "Data Controller of Major Importance" and must register with the NDPC — most startups will fall in the Ordinary High Level tier (₦10,000 fee, annual renewal); higher tiers (₦100,000 / ₦250,000) file an annual Compliance Audit Return. Direct marketing requires explicit consent (GAID Art. 18(1)(a)) with an absolute right to object. CAC business-name registration is ~₦10,000 plus ₦1,000 name reservation (revised schedule effective 1 Oct 2025). The Nigeria Startup Act label (via NITDA portal) unlocks a 3+2-year tax holiday (pioneer status) and 35% PIT relief for employees. VAT is 7.5% with small-business exemption at ≤₦100M turnover under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 (effective 1 Jan 2026). A pure matching/aggregation platform that does not place workers is not obviously a licensed "private employment agency", but the Labour Act licence regime exists and should be checked with counsel.

### Cited Findings

**NDPA 2023, GAID 2025 and NDPC registration**
- The NDPC issued the General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) on 20 March 2025; it replaces the NDPR 2019 and applies to controllers/processors handling personal data of Nigerian residents — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1606106/the-nigeria-data-protection-commission-issues-the-general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025-gaid); [Templars client alert, Apr 2025 (PDF)](https://templars-law.com/app/uploads/2025/04/NDPC-Issues-the-Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-General-Application-and-Implementation-Directive-2025.pdf); [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/06/nigeria-ndpc-issues-gaid-key-compliance-insights/). NDPR replacement effective 19 Sept 2025 — [LawPavilion](https://blog.lawpavilion.com/?p=9109).
- GAID Art. 9 requires all controllers/processors designated "of major importance" to register with the NDPC; UHL and EHL entities register once and file an annual Compliance Audit Return (CAR) by 31 March; OHL entities renew registration annually and are exempt from CAR; notify NDPC within 60 days of significant changes — [KPMG review of GAID 2025 (PDF)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/2025/05/Review%20of%20the%20NDPA%20General%20Application%20and%20Implementation%20Directive%20GAID%202025.pdf); [Legalbytes](https://legalbytes.substack.com/p/gaid-2025-what-nigerian-businesses); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/key-updates-from-the-nigeria-data-protection-act---general-application-and-implementation-directive-2025).
- Registration fees per the NDPC Guidance Notice (Feb 2024): MDP-UHL ₦250,000 (banks, telcos, insurers, multinationals, payment gateways); MDP-EHL ₦100,000 (government agencies, microfinance banks, higher-education institutions, hospitals, mortgage banks); MDP-OHL ₦10,000 (SMEs, primary/secondary schools, primary health centres) — [BusinessDay legal](https://businessday.ng/news/legal-business/article/key-highlights-of-the-guidance-notice-on-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1434270/key-highlights-of-the-guidance-notice-on-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance); [Afriwise guide](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/a-guide-on-the-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance-with-the-nigeria-data-protection-commission); [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/nigeria-data-protection-act-ndpa-controller-and-processor-registration/77465).
- Thresholds (sources differ): one summary says UHL = personal data of >5,000 data subjects in 6 months and EHL = >1,000 in 6 months; another gives a base criterion of >200 data subjects in six months with tier placement depending on how many additional factors apply (five for UHL/EHL, four for OHL) — [Mondaq explainer](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1456402/ndpc-guidance-notice-registration-of-data-controllers-and-processors-in-nigeria-explained); [ITEdgeNews](https://itedgenews.africa/ndpc-releases-guidelines-for-registering-key-data-controllers-and-processors). Verify against NDPC text.
- Original registration deadline 30 June 2024, extended to 30 Sept 2024 and then 31 Oct 2024 — [Templars deadline extension (PDF)](https://templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/07/NDPC-DCMI-DPMI_Registration_Deadline_Extension-.pdf); [Templars Oct 2024 alert (PDF)](https://templars-law.com/app/uploads/2024/10/Client-Alert-Nigerian-Data-Protection-Commission-.pdf). ### Inferences
- Compliance checklist for launch: (1) CAC incorporate as Ltd (not business name) if pursuing Startup Act label or investors — ~₦10,000 statutory fee at ≤₦1M share capital plus ₦1,000 name reservation and filing costs; (2) register with NDPC as a Data Controller of Major Importance once >200 data subjects' CVs are processed — likely OHL ₦10,000/year initially, re-tiered as volume grows; appoint a DPO (GAID requires DPOs for controllers of major importance per the "GAID Unpacked" series, details not retrieved); (3) separate, unticked consent checkboxes for (a) CV processing/AI matching, (b) job-alert emails (service), (c) promotional/partner emails (direct marketing), with one-click unsubscribe honoured immediately; (4) a privacy notice that discloses AI/automated profiling and allows human review of match decisions; (5) apply for the Startup Act label via the NITDA portal; (6) stay under ₦100M turnover for VAT exemption initially but plan for 7.5% VAT-inclusive pricing (Jobberman already quotes "incl. VAT").
- Recruiter licence: the Labour Act definition of an "employment agency" (matching job offers with applicants) is broad enough that a matching platform could be argued to fall within it, although Jobberman/MyJobMag operate as marketplaces; the ₦5,000 state-level licence is cheap relative to the risk, so budget for counsel's view and possibly obtaining it.
- Aggregation strategy: prefer official feeds/APIs and employer-authorised postings, respect robots.txt and ToS, link out to the original posting (Jobberman's own "Linkout" product shows this is an accepted pattern), store only job metadata (not recruiter personal data), and avoid scraping LinkedIn given its litigation record.

## Key Question 6: Unit-economics inputs — LLM cost for CV generation + scoring 200 jobs/user/week; email cost per 1,000; Paystack fee on small charges; per-user monthly cost

### Takeaway
Using a budget model (Gemini 2.5 Flash at $0.30/$2.50 per M tokens, GPT-5 mini at $0.25/$2.00, or GPT-4.1 mini at $0.40/$1.60), generating a CV costs under $0.01 and naively scoring 200 jobs/week costs roughly $0.55–$0.70 per user per month; with batching, caching and an embedding pre-filter this falls to ~$0.10–$0.30. Email is negligible on SES ($0.10/1,000 → ~$0.003/user/month for a daily digest) and ~$0.04–$0.05 on Postmark. Paystack takes ₦15 on a ₦1,000 charge and ₦30 on ₦2,000 (1.5%, flat fee waived). All-in variable cost per active user is therefore about ₦300–₦1,100 (≈$0.20–$0.75)/month, which means a ₦1,500–₦2,000 subscriber is gross-margin positive but a free user who gets full matching is not.

### Cited Findings
- Gemini 2.5 Flash: $0.30 per 1M input tokens, $2.50 per 1M output, cached input $0.03 — [kunalganglani LLM prices](https://www.kunalganglani.com/llm-prices/gemini-2.5-flash); older 2025 guide listed $0.15 input / $0.60 non-thinking output / $3.50 thinking output — [LaoZhang blog](https://blog.laozhang.ai/api-services/gemini-25-flash-pricing-2025-laozhang-ai-guide/) (likely outdated).

### Inferences (worked estimate — assumptions stated)
Assumptions: CV generation = ~3,000 input + ~2,000 output tokens; per-job scoring = ~1,500 input (CV summary + job text) + ~100 output tokens; 200 jobs/week ≈ 870 jobs/month; one digest email per day (30/month) plus ~5 transactional emails.
- CV generation (one-off, Gemini 2.5 Flash): 3k × $0.30/M + 2k × $2.50/M ≈ $0.0009 + $0.005 ≈ $0.006 per CV (≈₦9). Even 3 regenerations/month ≈ $0.02.
- Naive per-job LLM scoring, 870 jobs/month: Gemini 2.5 Flash ≈ 870 × ($0.00045 + $0.00025) ≈ $0.61; GPT-5 mini ≈ 870 × ($0.000375 + $0.0002) ≈ $0.50; GPT-4.1 mini ≈ 870 × ($0.0006 + $0.00016) ≈ $0.66; Claude Haiku 4.5 ≈ 870 × ($0.0015 + $0.0005) ≈ $1.74. So ≈$0.50–$0.70/user/month (₦750–₦1,050) on budget models.
- Optimised: embedding/keyword pre-filter to ~40 candidate jobs/month, batch 10–20 jobs per call with the CV in a cached prefix ($0.03/M cached on Gemini; $0.10/M on GPT-4.1 mini) → roughly $0.05–$0.15/user/month (₦75–₦225). Scoring each job once against many users (job-centric batching) is cheaper still since job text dominates input tokens.
- Email: SES 35 emails × $0.10/1,000 ≈ $0.0035/user/month; Postmark at $1.20–$1.80/1,000 ≈ $0.04–$0.06; Resend Pro at 50k/$20 ≈ $0.014. Email is immaterial versus LLM cost; SES is the cheapest at scale.
- Payment fee: ₦15 on ₦1,000; ₦22.50 on ₦1,500; ₦30 on ₦2,000; ₦137.50 on ₦2,500 (fee cliff); ₦175 on ₦5,000; ₦2,000 cap reached at ₦126,667+.
- Per-user monthly variable cost (excluding hosting/support): naive ≈ $0.55–$0.75 (₦800–₦1,100) ; optimised ≈ $0.10–$0.25 (₦150–₦400). Add ~1.5–2% payment fee on paid users. At ₦1,500/month ARPU and 3% conversion, each payer must subsidise ~33 free users; this is only viable if free users get the optimised (throttled) pipeline — e.g., weekly rather than daily digest and 20–50 scored jobs/week — while paid users get the 200-jobs/week service.
- Hosting/vector DB/storage not estimated; assume $50–$200/month fixed at early scale.
