# Go-to-market, growth channels and naming for a Nigerian consumer career platform (working name "Build Your Value"), 2025-2026

Research date: 2026-10-20. Method: ~30 web searches (TechCabal, Techpoint, Nairametrics, BusinessDay, company help centres, SEO tools, DataReportal reposts). Direct page fetches (WebFetch and curl) were blocked by the sandbox proxy for every external host tried (thecondia.com, grey.co, techcabal.com, allbusiness.africa, businessday.ng, kiboinsider.substack.com, launchbaseafrica.com, rdap.verisign.com, data.iana.org, search.cac.gov.ng, x.com, instagram.com all returned proxy 403 / DNS failure). All findings therefore rest on search-result excerpts, not full articles, and the web-search budget was exhausted before every sub-question could be covered. Items that could not be checked are listed under Gaps for each question rather than guessed.

## Key Question 1: Which growth channels have worked for Nigerian consumer startups, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
The best-documented Nigerian consumer growth engines are (a) cash referral programmes at roughly ₦1,000 per side, (b) student/campus ambassador programmes with scholarship-style incentives, (c) community/town-hall events and a Twitter/X-native brand voice, and (d) very cheap Meta reach (CPM roughly $1.50 / ₦2,100). TikTok (about 37m Nigerian users) now rivals Facebook; X is small (about 7.5m) but is where Nigeria's tech/professional conversation happens; "jobs in [city]" SEO is a proven six-figure-monthly-traffic channel for incumbents.

### Cited Findings

- PiggyVest reported ₦1.3 trillion in payouts and over 6 million users in 2025 — [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/piggyvest-reports-%e2%82%a61-3-trillion-in-payouts-over-6-million-users-in-2025/)
- Kuda pays a flat ₦1,000 to the referrer and ₦1,000 to the referred friend; the friend must confirm BVN, add a valid ID and buy at least ₦500 of airtime before the reward unlocks (24-hour delay) — [Kuda Help: Claim your referral rewards](https://help.kuda.com/en/articles/8954699-claim-your-referral-rewards); [Kuda Help: Share your referral code](https://help.kuda.com/en/articles/8954708-share-your-referral-code)

### Inferences
- A ₦1,000-per-side cash referral is the de facto Nigerian consumer standard (PiggyVest, Kuda). For a career product, a non-cash reward tied to the core value (e.g., one month of premium, or an extra AI CV rewrite) copies the Cowrywise fee-waiver model and avoids the fraud/airtime-farming problem that forced Kuda to gate rewards behind BVN + ID + ₦500 airtime.
- Campus programmes work best when framed as a scholarship/credential (₦200k award, certificate, internship path) rather than "sell our app"; Cowrywise's 80+ campuses via scholars is the template. A career platform has a natural hook: final-year students and NYSC-bound graduates need CVs anyway.
- With CPM around ₦2,100 and CPC ₦30–₦300, paid Meta/TikTok acquisition can plausibly land installs well under ₦1,000 if creative is strong; the hard constraint is conversion to paid, not reach.
- X is where Nigerian tech/HR professionals and journalists are, so it is the launch/credibility channel; TikTok and WhatsApp are the mass-reach channels for the 18–30 job-seeker base.
- Incumbents' SEO shows "jobs in [city]" queries have six-figure monthly volume; a new entrant should expect 12+ months to rank and instead publish structured, city-tagged job pages plus "how to write a CV for X" content to compound.

### Gaps
- No verifiable PiggyVest/OPay/Moniepoint referral terms from official sources; PiggyVest's ₦1,000 figure is a blogger's claim.
- No evidence found on Nairaland's job-board traffic or on named startups growing through Nairaland.
- No documented case of a startup activating at an NYSC orientation camp (search budget exhausted before a "NYSC camp sponsorship" query could be run).
- No audited influencer rate card; all figures are agency/vendor estimates that disagree by 3–5x.
- Nigerian-specific CPA / cost-per-install figures for Meta or Google were not found; only CPM/CPC ranges.
- Newsletter benchmarks (open rates, subscriber counts for Nigerian career newsletters) not found beyond the fact that Jobberman runs a biweekly one.
- Full articles (Condia PiggyVest interview, Grey ads guide) could not be opened; details are from search excerpts.

## Key Question 2: Distribution partnerships (universities, NYSC, state agencies, faith groups, cooperatives, telcos, banks, employers): what precedents exist?

### Takeaway
The clearest precedents are state employability agencies (LSETF has run placement partnerships with Jobberman, UNDP, USADF and King's Trust, with 44–50% placement targets), telco zero-rating of education content (Airtel x Roducate bundle, MTN's NCC-approved 500MB/day Edves bundle, and a federal 100MB/day zero-rated education programme starting 1 Oct 2026), and funder-backed employer networks (Jobberman x Mastercard Foundation). No precedent was found for churches, mosques or cooperatives as formal distribution partners for a career app.

### Cited Findings
- LSETF x King's Trust International "Get Hired Programme 2026": digital learning + in-person training + direct recruitment; eligibility 18–35, Lagos resident, must own a smartphone; venue Egbeda; recruitment fair at NECA House with 22 employer partners offering interviews — [MSME Africa: call for applications](https://msmeafricaonline.com/call-for-applications-lsetf-x-kings-trust-international-get-hired-programme-2026/); [MSME Africa: employability skills](https://msmeafricaonline.com/lsetf-partners-equip-lagos-youths-with-employability-skills-job-opportunities/)
- LSETF x USADF renewed MoU: earlier programme trained 10,500+ young Lagosians; target to place at least half in internships or jobs — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=352753)
- LSETF x UNDP Lagos employability project built a verified employer-facing database making trained candidates available for placement within 48 hours — [UNDP](https://undp.org/node/251061)
- LSETF x Jobberman x ATC Makoko pilot (2017–18) reported 44% job placement — [Nairametrics (2018)](https://nairametrics.com/2018/05/28/lsetf-partners-jobberman-others-to-empower-unemployed-youths-in-communities/)
- Jobberman x Mastercard Foundation (2020): train 5m job seekers and place 3m in "dignified employment" over five years — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202007200457.html)
- Jobberman's Kaduna (with Kaduna State Govt) and Edo events show state-government co-hosting of career fairs in 2025 — [ThisDay](https://thisdaylive.com/index.php/2025/03/27/jobberman-to-host-kaduna-career-fair-second-time-in-2025)
- Telco zero-rating: ALTON-member telcos endorsed the Federal Government's Zero-Rated Access to Educational Platforms and Content Programme (initial 100MB/day for approved educational platforms); public-school students to receive it from 1 October 2026 — [Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/economy/1730940-mtn-airtel-telcos-endorse-fgs-free-daily-data-nigerian-students/); [Innovation Village](https://innovation-village.com/fg-to-give-nigerian-students-100mb-free-data-daily-from-october-1/)
- MTN Nigeria obtained NCC approval to zero-rate Edves via a free 500MB daily bundle for students, teachers and parents (date unclear) — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=77532)
- Airtel Nigeria x Roducate "Exam Success Bundle" (Feb 2024) gives data-bundled access to exam-prep content; Airtel x UNICEF agreed to zero-rate Learning Passport sites — [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/?p=124658); [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=415385)
- Bank partnership precedent: PiggyVest's CMO credits a 2017 partnership with a commercial bank for scaling users — [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/zero-to-scale-joshua-chibueze-cmo-and-co-founder-piggyvest/)
- NYSC's institutional partners are financial/SME bodies (CBN, BOI, SMEDAN, Access Bank); a 2024 federal scheme promised ₦10m grants to 5,000 corps members — [VON](https://von.gov.ng/nysc-partners-with-stakeholders-to-strengthen-orientation-exercises/); [Gazette NGR](https://gazettengr.com/?p=130468)
- Campus distribution precedent: Cowrywise's scholar-ambassador network across 80+ campuses — [cowrywise.com/ambassadors](https://cowrywise.com/ambassadors)

### Inferences
- The LSETF/UNDP "verified employer database with 48-hour placement" is effectively the B2G version of what the platform's job-matching engine does; pitching the platform as the digital layer for state employability programmes (LSETF, and equivalents in Kaduna/Edo where Jobberman is active) is a credible wedge.
- Telco zero-rating has gone to school-facing edtech (Edves, Roducate) and now a federal programme; a career platform would need NCC approval for a zero-rated bundle, which MTN obtained for Edves, so the path exists but is slow and content-defined ("educational").
- Faith groups and cooperatives are unproven as formal partners; they are better treated as community channels (youth fellowships, alumni associations) reached via ambassadors than as contracts.

### Gaps
- No evidence found on university career-centre partnerships with startups (query not completed before search budget ran out).
- No precedent found for churches, mosques or cooperatives distributing a career/edtech product.
- No current (2025–26) MTN consumer-app bundle deal was found; the Edves approval date is unclear.
- Bank youth-product partnerships (e.g., with GTBank/Access youth accounts) were not found for career products.

## Key Question 3: How do Nigerian startups overcome scam skepticism, and what do low-data onboarding flows look like?

### Takeaway
Scam exposure is near-universal (84% of surveyed Nigerian adults encountered a scam in the past year per GASA 2026), so Nigerian founders frame trust as a day-one design layer: visible identity verification, founder-forward storytelling, and local relevance. Onboarding norms from fintech set expectations (phone number + OTP, with WhatsApp fallback because SMS to Nigerian numbers is unreliable), and programmes targeting job seekers assume smartphone ownership.

### Cited Findings
- 84% of surveyed Nigerian adults encountered a scam in the past year (Global Anti-Scam Alliance 2026 Nigeria report, cited secondhand) — [TechEconomy interview with Profiled Nigeria](https://techeconomy.ng/interview-how-profiled-nigeria-emmanuel-ajao-is-building-trust-infrastructur)
- 63% of African internet users are wary of sharing personal information online (Statista 2022, cited secondhand) — [Consonance Club: Building Credibility](https://consonanceclub.substack.com/p/building-credibility-in-the-african)
- Profiled Nigeria founder Emmanuel Ajao: Nigerian caution "reflects accumulated experience of fraud rather than Nigerians being overly suspicious"; identity verification should be a neutral layer; "If trust isn't part of the foundation from day one, you're constantly patching holes" — [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/interview-how-profiled-nigeria-emmanuel-ajao-is-building-trust-infrastructur)
- Lagos consultancy guidance: humanise the brand with founder stories and transparent "About Us" pages because Nigerian consumers gravitate to businesses they can humanise — [Discover Lagos](https://www.discoverlagos.ng/post/building-customer-trust-online-in-lagos-a-strategic-guide-for-nigerian-entrepreneurs)
- uLesson earned confidence by integrating local curricula and culturally relatable video examples — [Consonance Club](https://consonanceclub.substack.com/p/building-credibility-in-the-african)
- Fear of fraud and of receiving something different from what was ordered drives hesitation in Nigerian social commerce — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=180694)
- Microsoft framed Nigeria's digital growth as hinging on trust — [Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2891693)
- OTP delivery: a driver-app team found OTPs to Nigerian numbers failed by SMS ("unable to deliver") and only arrived via WhatsApp (single anecdote) — [Onde community](https://community.onde.app/t/driver-app-not-sending-otp/13389)
- Termii (Nigerian provider) documents an SMS OTP API with a 5-minute expiry sample — [Termii developers](https://termii.com/developers)
- Vendors sell SMS-with-WhatsApp-fallback OTP for Nigeria ("no paperwork, no approvals"), a marketing claim — [Message Central Nigeria](https://www.messagecentral.com/product/verify-now/overview/nigeria)
- Some carriers block international SMS; voice-call OTP fallback and rate limits on failed attempts are recommended — [MyOperator help](https://support.myoperator.com/portal/en/kb/articles/how-do-i-connect-a-phone-number-to-my-whatsapp-account)
- Fintech KYC norms: Kuda referral rewards require the new user to confirm BVN, add a valid ID and buy ₦500 airtime — [Kuda Help](https://help.kuda.com/en/articles/8954699-claim-your-referral-rewards)
- LSETF's Get Hired requires applicants to own a smartphone, indicating smartphone ownership is the baseline assumption for Lagos employability programmes — [MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/call-for-applications-lsetf-x-kings-trust-international-get-hired-programme-2026/)
- Job seekers' scam context: Nigerian youth unemployment at 53% with warnings that joblessness pushes youth toward irregular migration and cybercrime (Plan International/ActionAid 2025) — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509050060.html)

### Inferences
- For a job platform the specific scam fear is "pay-to-get-a-job" recruiters; the trust cues that follow from the sources are: never charge for applying, show CAC registration number and a named, photographed founding team, publish employer-verification criteria, and show real testimonials with names/schools.
- Onboarding should be phone-number-first with OTP via SMS and automatic WhatsApp (and voice) fallback, Google sign-in as a second path, and the first CV produced before any payment is requested ("free value first" mirrors ALX's $5/month-after-free-access and uLesson's price cuts).
- Keep the first session under a few MB: text-first CV builder, deferred image/video, and job alerts delivered by email/WhatsApp rather than requiring app re-opens.

### Gaps
- No source directly documents CAC-registration display or "verified badge" as a measured trust lever; this is practitioner inference.
- No published Nigerian onboarding-funnel data (drop-off by step, OTP delivery rates by carrier) was found.
- No research found specifically on job-seeker scam experiences in Nigeria (query not run before budget exhaustion).

## Key Question 4: What happened to comparable African job-tech/edtech startups, and what are the lessons on monetization, retention and unit economics?

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 record is a string of consumer-model retreats: Kibo School closed (2024, no sustainable student-financing model), Edukoya shut down and returned capital (Feb 2025), Decagon pivoted away from training (Mar 2025, loan defaults), uLesson halved prices (2024), Eden Life paused consumer subscriptions (decision Oct 2025) after a unit-economics audit, and AltSchool dropped its income-share model and shortened its diploma. Jobberman survives as a funder-backed (Mastercard Foundation) employer-side marketplace inside The African Talent Company. The consistent lesson: in Nigeria, consumer subscriptions survive only when the payer is the beneficiary, the price is a few dollars, and the product saves the user money or time they would otherwise spend informally (e.g., on WhatsApp).

### Cited Findings

**Jobberman (Nigeria)**
- One Africa Media (OAM) acquired 100% of Jobberman in 2015, before OAM merged with Ringier Africa to form ROAM Africa; in Sept 2020 Jobberman acquired rival NGcareers (founded 2009, 1m+ users by 2019), with plans to fold it into the Jobberman brand — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/fr/2020/10/27/jobberman-acquisition-ng-careers-roam-africa/); [Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/2020/09/03/jobberman-acquires-top-competitor-ngcareers-undisclosed-deal); [Ringier](https://www.ringier.com/jobberman-nigeria-acquires-ngcareers-com-one-of-nigerias-top-job-search-recruitment-and-career-development-platforms/). (CB Insights lists "acquired by ROAM Africa, October 2020", which conflicts with the 2015 OAM account — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/jobbermancom).)
- Parent is now The African Talent Company (TATC); CEO Hilda Kabushenga named a 2025 WEF Young Global Leader; 2025 metrics: 329,432 new jobseekers, 2.83m content views, 1.4m social followers — [Jobberman 2025 Wrapped](https://www.jobberman.com/discover/2025-wrapped-our-winning-year-at-jobberman-nigeria)
- Jobberman 2022 research: 1 in 5 unemployed youths had been jobless 5+ years; "over half" of Nigerian youths described as unemployed and unemployable; hustling, family support and partner stipends are survival modes — [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2022/12/13/in-new-report-jobberman-gives-fresh-insights-into-nigerias-youth-unemployment/)

**Kibo School (closed 2024)**

**uLesson**
- Cut subscription prices by half in 2024 to retain users; post-COVID return to in-person schooling removed much pandemic demand — [AllBusiness Africa](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/state-of-african-edtech-2026)
- Payer-beneficiary mismatch thesis: K-12 consumer models at African income levels do not produce sustainable unit economics; adult-learning models where the payer is the beneficiary do; Zeraki succeeded by selling to school administrators (claims 50%+ of Kenyan high schools) — [AllBusiness Africa: adult learning pivot](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/african-edtech-adult-learning-pivot); [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/african-edtech-pivot-to-adult-learning/)

- Paused cleaning, laundry and individual meal subscriptions to focus on corporate clients; decision taken October 2025 after "a rigorous internal audit of its unit economics"; TechCabal attributes the squeeze to inflation, FX volatility and logistics costs, and argues the problem is structural because a cleaner or cook can be hired directly over WhatsApp for less, making take rates fragile; "managed home subscriptions are brutally hard to scale in Nigeria's current economy" — [TechCabal Daily: Locked out of Eden](https://techcabal.com/2026/02/16/techcabal-daily-locked-out-of-eden/); [TechCabal (ES)](https://techcabal.com/es/2026/02/14/Eden-Life-pausa-su-negocio-de-consumo-para-centrarse-en-los-clientes-corporativos/)

**Fuzu (Kenya, operates in Nigeria)**
- Claims 20m+ cumulative users since 2015 (company figure; a government listing says 1.5m+), 85 employees across Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda; launched AI-driven job search in Nigeria in 2022; a Dec 2025 item reports a $1.75m raise from Finnfund and others — [WeAreTech](https://www.wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/solutions/kenyan-startup-fuzu-builds-integrated-career-platform-for-african-job-seekers); [Business Finland case](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/Advice-and-market-information/data-bank/cases/2022/a-finnish-start-up-is-setting-up-a-responsible-employment-service-in-africa/); [TechCabal 2022](https://techcabal.com/2022/02/12/fuzu-uses-ai-to-disrupt-job-search-and-recruitment-in-nigeria/); [Africa Global Funds](https://www.africaglobalfunds.com/news/investors/finnishkenyan-recruitment-platform-raises-175m-from-finnfund-and-others)

**Andela**
- Sept 2025 AI coding training partnership with GitHub; acquired assessment platform Qualified (with Codewars); about $379m raised; an older restructuring affected junior engineers in Nigeria, Uganda, Kenya — [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/andela); [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/andela-acquires-qualified-assessment-platform/); [Techbuild](https://techbuild.africa/?p=62198)

**AltSchool Africa**
- Dropped its $500 income-share model for the next batch; ₦10,000 application fee; launched a Nano-Diploma (Sept 2025) because the 20 hr/week one-year diploma was too demanding for working learners — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1026599); [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/?p=168419)

**ALX**
- $5/month "All Access" model payable in local currency — [ALX Africa](https://alxafrica.com/?p=16273)

### Inferences
- Monetization: the survivors price low and recurring ($5/month ALX) or take money from institutions (Jobberman from employers and Mastercard Foundation; Zeraki from schools; Eden Life now from corporates). A ₦2,000–₦5,000/month consumer tier is in the range the market has accepted; a four-figure-dollar ISA (Decagon, AltSchool) has failed repeatedly.
- Retention: consumer demand collapses when the external trigger ends (COVID for uLesson). A career platform's natural trigger (job search) is episodic, so retention must come from recurring value (weekly matched-job emails, learning-path progress) and from annual/exam-cycle re-activation rather than from daily app opens.
- Unit economics: Eden Life's lesson is that any service a Nigerian can arrange over WhatsApp for free will cap your take rate. AI CV building competes with free ChatGPT and ₦5k–₦20k freelance CV writers (price not verified); the defensible value is the matching and distribution to verified employers, not the document.
- A B2B2C or B2G revenue line (state agencies, universities, NGOs paying per placed/trained user) is the pattern that kept Jobberman alive and should be planned from day one.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 status found for Gebeya, Shortlist, TalentQL, Tuteria, Edves (beyond the MTN zero-rating item), Jobzilla, Jobsity, Workpay or Kazi; the search budget ran out before per-company queries.
- No founder-written post-mortem found for Eden Life or Edukoya; Kibo's lessons exist on its Substack but the pages could not be opened.
- No uLesson 2025 subscriber count or price point found.
- The Jobberman/ROAM acquisition date conflicts between sources (2015 vs 2020) and could not be resolved by opening primary pages.

## Key Question 5: Positioning and messaging: how do Nigerian job seekers talk about their goals, what tone works, and what about gender inclusion?

### Takeaway
"Japa" (emigration) is the dominant career frame among young Nigerians and is driven by economic despair, not wanderlust; tech is seen as a "ticket out". Pidgin has documented appeal in advertising, but marketing commentators warn that forced youth slang reads as fake and that Nigerian Gen Z trusts creators over celebrities. Women are structurally under-represented in Nigerian tech (6 of 93 surveyed tech firms had a woman in top management; no state scored top marks on women's participation in tech/AI), so inclusion is a positioning opportunity, not just an ethics line.

### Cited Findings
- Young Nigerians are "pivoting to tech" as a "ticket out of the country"; destination countries run programmes "to lure tech bros over" (e.g., UK Tech Nation Visa) — [Ventures Africa Substack](https://venturesafrica.substack.com/p/japa-syndrome-1262813)
- A 27-year-old developer: "a developer will reach a certain level of his career and the thought of leaving the country will creep in", tied to limited opportunity outside Lagos and Abuja — [ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/?p=237907)
- Economic despair and lack of opportunity are the primary japa driver; example of someone earning ₦70,000/month and spending ₦60,000 on transport — [Discover Lagos: Japa or stay](https://www.discoverlagos.ng/post/japa-or-stay-navigating-the-aspirations-and-realities-of-lagos-s-youth)
- Youth leader: creating indigenous tech opportunities would curb japa — [NAN](https://nannews.ng/opportunities-in-tech-can-curb-japa-syndrome-youth-leader/)
- Frustration and the bad economy fuel the japa wave inside the tech industry — [Kola King Substack](https://kolaking.substack.com/p/how-frustration-bad-economy-fuel)
- A startup literally named "Japa Global" positions as "LinkedIn for Noncode Tech in Africa" — [TotalEnergies Startupper listing](https://startupper.totalenergies.com/fr/juries/jPLv_c9cYDD4m1XX-SIicg/participations/4833/vote)
- Youth unemployment 53%, about 80m youths (Plan International/ActionAid 2025) vs NBS official 4.3% (Q2 2024) with 93% informal employment — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509050060.html); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=758096)
- Pidgin advertising "commands enormous public appeal because of its spread, flexibility and the creative possibilities it allows" — [Equinox: Linguistic creativity in Nigerian Pidgin advertising](https://api.equinoxpub.com/articles/15920)
- Marketing Edge: forced youth tone can "come off as fake, artificial, or out of touch"; brands copy youth style while ignoring buying power and digital habits; Nigerian Gen Z are mobile-first and influenced by trusted creators rather than celebrities — [Marketing Edge: Gen Zs are not distracted](https://marketingedge.com.ng/gen-zs-are-not-distracted-your-brand-is-just-irrelevant/); [Marketing Edge: Gen Z brief is broken](https://marketingedge.com.ng/the-gen-z-brief-is-broken/)
- Employer-side vocabulary: Fuzu listings tell candidates "the employer will review your Fuzu Profile and contact shortlisted candidates" — [Fuzu](https://www.fuzu.com/kenya/job/computers-software?page=2)
- Gender: BudgIT state assessment found no state earned a top rating for women's participation in tech/AI; only Lagos, Kaduna and Cross River rated green; only four states fund women-specific STEM programmes; Nigeria loses $11bn a year to the digital skills gap — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/nigeria-risks-gendered-recession-as-women-shut-out-of-fast-growing-tech-ai-sectors/)
- ONE Campaign/CGD survey of 93 Nigerian tech firms: only six had a woman in a top management position — [ONE.org](https://www.one.org/africa/stories/nigerias-tech-sector-is-rising-but-its-leaving-women-behind/)
- TechCabal/UK-Nigeria Tech Hub Nigerian Women in Tech Report: 59% of respondents faced challenges during their course of study — [TechCabal report](https://techcabal.com/reports/nigerian-women-in-tech-report/)
- "Naija Girls in Tech" (launched Dec 2025) targets training/mentoring/job pathways for 100,000 girls and young women by 2030 — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512230063.html)

### Inferences
- Message around outcomes job seekers already name: "get shortlisted", "fix my CV", "upskill", and (carefully) "japa-ready". A platform can serve both camps by positioning as "build the value that gets you hired here or abroad" rather than taking a side on emigration.
- Tone: standard Nigerian English for product and trust surfaces (CV, employer pages); Pidgin and slang only in creator-led social content, written by Nigerian creators rather than by the brand account. Avoid "tech bro" framing in brand copy because it is gendered and already parodied.
- Gender inclusion: a visible women-focused track (female mentors, partnership with Naija Girls in Tech-type initiatives, women-in-tech learning paths) addresses a documented gap and differentiates from Jobberman's generic positioning.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence on the phrases "soft life", "my CV" or "shortlist" as used by job seekers (search budget exhausted before these queries ran); the "tech bro" and "japa" usage is sourced above.
- No study found measuring Pidgin vs English conversion for digital products; the Pidgin evidence is about advertising appeal generally.
- Dates of the BudgIT, ONE/CGD and TechCabal reports were not visible in search excerpts; the ONE/CGD survey appears older.

## Key Question 6: Naming: is "Build Your Value" a strong brand, and what are better alternatives? Domain, handle and trademark feasibility.

### Takeaway
"Build Your Value" is clear but generic: it is a three-word descriptive phrase (hard to protect as a trademark, long for a handle, and "value" skews toward finance/valuation in Nigerian search results). No Nigerian company or trademark with this exact name surfaced in web search, but neither the CAC public search, WHOIS/RDAP, nor X/Instagram could be reached from this environment, so availability is entirely unverified. The strongest alternatives below are short, Nigerian-rooted but Africa-portable, and avoid known startup names.

### Cited Findings
- A web search for "Build Your Value" + Nigeria returned only business-valuation firms and construction companies (e.g., Aviaan Accounting valuation pages, AG Vision Construction), i.e., the phrase currently has a finance/valuation connotation in Nigerian search results and no existing brand owner surfaced — [Aviaan Accounting](https://aviaanaccounting.com/business-valuation-companies-in-nigeria/); [ConnectCiti listing](https://connectciti.com/listing/ag-vision-construction-nig-ltd-2)
- A search for "Build Your Value" with trademark/brand/coaching terms returned only generic coaching-brand articles and no registered mark or company of that name — [ICF](https://coachingfederation.org/blog/3-things-to-build-your-brand); [Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/blog/coach-branding-guide)
- Conflict precedents in the category that a new name must steer clear of: Jobberman (Nigeria), Fuzu (Kenya/Nigeria), Japa Global, ALX, AltSchool, Risevest, Cowrywise, PiggyVest, Kuda — see sources in Questions 1 and 4, e.g. [Jobberman](https://www.jobberman.com/discover/2025-wrapped-our-winning-year-at-jobberman-nigeria), [Fuzu](https://www.fuzu.com/forum/article/find-grow-and-thrive-with-fuzu-africa-s-1-career-platform), [Japa Global](https://startupper.totalenergies.com/fr/juries/jPLv_c9cYDD4m1XX-SIicg/participations/4833/vote)

### Inferences (naming analysis; the author's assessment, not sourced facts)

**Evaluation of "Build Your Value"**
- Clarity: high (says what it does). Distinctiveness: low (a common English phrase; descriptive marks are weak at any trademark registry). Memorability: medium (three words, no hook). Pronounceability across Nigerian languages: fine. Handle: "buildyourvalue" is 14 characters, within X's 15-char limit but long; likely to collide with motivational/coaching accounts globally. Semantic drift: "value" reads as fintech/valuation (see search finding above). Acronym "BYV" is unmemorable. Verdict: acceptable as a campaign line ("Build your value") under a shorter brand, weak as the brand itself.

**Alternative names (Nigeria-first, Africa-scalable). Known-conflict flags are from the author's general knowledge and are unverified.**
1. CareerPadi — "padi" is Pidgin for close friend; warm, trustworthy ("your career padi"). Pidgin is understood across West Africa; less so in East Africa. Check: "Padi" is used by several Nigerian apps (e.g., payments); confirm no career-category conflict.
2. Ladda — phonetic "ladder"; climb/progress; two syllables, works in any African market; plain-English meaning survives. Check: short .com almost certainly taken; .ng/.africa plausible.
3. Afara — Yoruba for "bridge" (bridge to work); soft, feminine-friendly sound, scalable. Check: existing Nigerian small brands may use it.
4. Worthi / MyWorth — plays on "know your worth", a phrase Nigerians use about salary and self-respect; keeps the "value" idea from the working name. Check: "Worth" fintechs exist globally.
5. Valuely — direct derivative of Build Your Value; keeps brand equity; modern suffix. Risk: still finance-sounding.
6. Sabi Careers / SabiWork — "sabi" = to know/be skilled (Pidgin). RED FLAG: "Sabi" is an existing well-funded Nigerian B2B commerce startup; avoid as a lead name.
7. Goke — Yoruba "climb up/rise"; also a common given name (friendly, human). Check: personal-name collisions on handles.
8. Dara Careers — Yoruba "dara" = good/fine; also a name across Nigeria; easy to say continent-wide. Check: heavy global use of "Dara".
9. Hustle-free names: Steady / Stedi — "steady" is a Nigerian compliment ("you dey steady"); conveys stable income. Check: Stedi is a US software company (conflict if expanding).
10. Upskilla — descriptive of the learning-path feature; Africa-neutral; weaker on jobs.
11. Shortlistd — speaks the exact outcome job seekers want ("I got shortlisted"); risk: hard to spell, similar to Shortlist (the East-African hiring startup named in the brief).
12. Nextrung — "next rung" of the ladder; English, portable; slightly corporate.
13. Jara Careers — Pidgin "jara" = extra/bonus. RED FLAG: "Jara" has been used by Nigerian rewards/loyalty apps; check CAC.
14. Opor — Yoruba for abundance/plenty; short, distinctive. Check: unfamiliar outside the south-west; pronunciation varies.
15. Workdey — Pidgin "work dey" (there is work); upbeat, directly on-message ("Work dey, come find am"). Check: Pidgin-only resonance.

**Recommended shortlist of 5 to check first:** CareerPadi, Ladda, Afara, Worthi, Valuely (keep "Build your value" as the tagline).

## Key Question 7: What activation, retention and conversion benchmarks should a Nigerian consumer subscription target in its first 6–12 months?

### Takeaway
No Nigeria-specific published benchmarks exist in the sources found; the only hard local anchors are prices and behaviours (ALX $5/month; uLesson had to halve prices; Kuda gates ₦1,000 referrals behind KYC; Jobberman adds about 330k jobseekers a year; LSETF pilots place 44–50%). Global consumer-subscription references (download-to-paid under 2%, top apps about 4%; consumer monthly churn 5–10%, with 20–40% of churn from failed payments) should be treated as ceilings, not targets, because Nigerian purchasing power and payment-failure rates are worse.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps: most apps convert under 2% of downloads to paid; top apps near 4% — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/app-engagement-metrics-subscription-apps.md)
- Many consumer subscription brands lose 5–10% of subscribers per month; roughly 20–40% of churn can be failed payments — [MEAN CEO 2026 churn stats](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=10240)
- 1% monthly churn retains about 90% of users after 12 months; 8% monthly churn retains under 40% — [Parsa Substack](https://parsa.substack.com/p/consumer-subscription-kpi-benchmarks-retention-engagement-and-conversion-rates-9ac13b57c3d3)
- The widely cited VC Cafe table (B2C mobile conversion about 2%, B2C subscription churn 6–8%) was, per its author, generated with ChatGPT and US-centric; do not rely on it — [VC Cafe](https://www.vccafe.com/conversion-retention-and-churn-benchmarks/)
- Nigerian price anchors: ALX $5/month — [ALX](https://alxafrica.com/?p=16273); uLesson halved subscription prices in 2024 to retain users — [AllBusiness Africa](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/state-of-african-edtech-2026); AltSchool ₦10,000 application fee and abandoned $500 ISA — [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1026599)
- Acquisition anchors: Kuda ₦1,000 per side referral — [Kuda Help](https://help.kuda.com/en/articles/8954699-claim-your-referral-rewards); Meta CPM about ₦2,100, CPC ₦30–₦300 — [Grey](https://grey.co/blog/facebook-ads-cost-nigeria)
- Scale anchors: Jobberman 329,432 new jobseekers in 2025 — [Jobberman](https://www.jobberman.com/discover/2025-wrapped-our-winning-year-at-jobberman-nigeria); LSETF/Jobberman Makoko pilot 44% placement; LSETF/USADF target 50% placement — [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2018/05/28/lsetf-partners-jobberman-others-to-empower-unemployed-youths-in-communities/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=352753)
- Demand-side context: 53% youth unemployment; 1 in 5 unemployed youths jobless 5+ years — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509050060.html); [ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2022/12/13/in-new-report-jobberman-gives-fresh-insights-into-nigerias-youth-unemployment/)

### Inferences (proposed targets; derived, not sourced)
- Activation (first session): 60%+ of sign-ups complete a CV draft and set at least one job-alert preference; the OTP step should lose under 15% (use SMS + WhatsApp + voice fallback).
- Engagement: weekly email/WhatsApp job digest open rate 35–50% (WhatsApp vendor claims of 95% are not credible planning numbers); 30-day return rate 25–35% given episodic job-search intent.
- Conversion: free-to-paid 2–3% in months 1–6, aiming for 4–5% by month 12 with a ₦1,500–₦3,000/month tier (below ALX's $5 and in line with uLesson's post-cut pricing); expect card/wallet payment failures to drive a quarter of churn, so support bank transfer, USSD and wallet (OPay/PalmPay) rails and offer quarterly prepay.
- Retention: monthly churn under 10% in months 1–6 and under 7% by month 12 would be strong for Nigeria; measure "re-subscription within 90 days" because job seekers naturally lapse after landing a role.
- Acquisition cost: blended CAC target of ₦700–₦1,500 per activated free user (below the ₦1,000 referral norm plus paid-media mix), and CAC payback within 3 subscription months for paid users.
- Placement proof: publish a "shortlist rate" (share of active users who get at least one interview per quarter); the LSETF pilots' 44–50% placement for trained cohorts is the external bar funders will compare against.

### Gaps
- No Nigerian or African consumer-app cohort data (D1/D7/D30 retention, free-to-paid, churn) was found in any source; everything above is inferred from global data plus Nigerian pricing anchors.
- No data on failed-payment rates specifically on Nigerian cards/wallets.
- No uLesson, ALX or AltSchool conversion or retention figures were disclosed in the sources found.
