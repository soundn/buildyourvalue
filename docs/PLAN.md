# Own Nigeria's Trusted Job-Match Gap at ₦1,999

*A SoundTechWorks solution. Working product name: Build Your Value.*

No Nigerian or global competitor bundles AI CV improvement, a personalised emailed job digest and a CV-gap-driven learning path under one transparent naira subscription, and the research says the winning version of "Build Your Value" sells **trust and relevance, not the CV document**. Nigeria's headline unemployment of 4.3% hides a market where **93% of the employed are informal**, roughly **600,000 graduates a year** chase **418,000–450,000 NYSC slots**, a single reputable vacancy draws **200–2,400 applications**, and **84% of adults met a scam last year**, so the jobs-to-be-done are "get shortlisted", "know why I failed" and "never pay a fake recruiter". Jobberman already sells an AI CV tool at **₦12,000/month**, Nigerian AI startups sell credit packs at ₦5,500–12,500, global tools cost $20–50 in USD with trial traps, and mass-market consumer subscriptions (Spotify, YouTube, Netflix Mobile) have settled at **₦1,300–2,500/month**; the plan therefore prices a core tier at **₦1,999/month** (just under Paystack's ₦2,500 fee cliff, costing ₦30 in fees), a Pro tier at ₦4,999, a one-off 7-day "Finished CV" pass, and employer and course-affiliate revenue from day one because 2–5% free-to-paid conversion alone will not carry the business. The MVP is a mobile PWA (Android is ~82% of Nigerian mobile traffic) on Next.js + Supabase/pgvector, fed by Google-for-Jobs aggregators and Remotive rather than scraping, with a two-stage embedding-plus-cheap-LLM matcher whose variable cost is about **$0.10–0.30 per registered user per month** on email, but roughly double that if weekly WhatsApp marketing messages are bundled. "Build Your Value" is clear but generic and skews toward finance/valuation in Nigerian search results; keep it as the tagline and brand as **Ladda** (runner-up Afara, Nigeria-only option CareerPadi), subject to domain, CAC, trademark and handle checks the research could not run. Launch gates are confirming that SoundTechWorks, which builds and owns the product with the brand as its trading name ([ADR-0002](adr/0002-soundtechworks-builds-and-owns-the-product.md)), is a CAC limited company whose registered objects cover this business, NDPC registration before the 200th data subject, GAID-compliant consent for marketing email, and a documented decision on the Labour Act recruiter licence; the biggest unverified assumption is that unemployed Nigerians will pay anything at all, which the founder must test with a paid pilot before building Phase 2.

## Nigeria's job market hides 10–15 million formal-job seekers behind a 4.3% headline

The official numbers understate the opportunity and mis-describe the customer. The NBS Labour Force Survey for Q2 2024 put unemployment at **4.3%** (down from 5.3% in Q1), time-related underemployment at 9.2% and the combined LU2 measure at 13.0% ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/news/unemployment-rate-drops-to-4-3-in-q2-2024-nbs/)), but the same release showed **93.0% of employed Nigerians in informal work** and **85.6% self-employed** ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2024/11/26/nbs-says-85-6-of-working-class-nigerians-are-self-employed/)). No later NBS release surfaced in the research, so the product should treat "employed" users as people hustling informally while seeking a first or better formal job. Advocacy figures of 53% youth unemployment or "80 million unemployed youths" ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202509050060.html)) use incompatible definitions; the defensible addressable pool is the formal-job-seeking segment, which the research sizes at roughly 10–15 million by combining Nigeria's **~14 million LinkedIn users** ([NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/linkedin-users-in-nigeria/)) with annual graduate cohorts and the NYSC backlog.

The graduate pipeline is the most reachable, dated cohort in the market. The NYSC Director-General says over 400 institutions produce **around 600,000 graduates a year** ([TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/?p=1297801)), while the 2026 mobilisation target is only 418,000 after an extra 50,000 slots were approved "in response to long waits" ([NAN](https://nannews.ng/tinubu-approves-additional-50000-for-nysc-mobilisation-in-2026/)), later cited as 450,000 ([VON](https://von.gov.ng/nysc-to-mobilise-450000-graduates-in-2026/)). Commentary puts the backlog awaiting mobilisation above 500,000 ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602030633.html)), and for 2025 Batch C only about 40% of registered prospective corps members could join orientation because camps were full ([ABS Tech Connect](https://www.abstechconnect.com/blogs/important-update-for-2025-batch-c-prospective-corps-members-db10041b)). Batch dates are public (Batch C1 registration opened 4 November 2025 with camp 19 November to 9 December, per [Leadership](https://leadership.ng/2025-nysc-batch-c1-how-to-get-your-call-up-number-all-you-need-to-know/)), which means the platform can time acquisition pushes to the moment 100,000+ graduates simultaneously need a CV. Age ceilings of around 25 at some large employers and "2 years' experience" demands on entry-level roles ([NaijaTechGuide](https://www.naijatechguide.com/why-your-dream-graduate-job-remains.html)) make speed-to-first-offer a genuine anxiety for this cohort.

What these seekers need is not more listings but filtering, feedback and safety. The market is "distributed across dozens of sources", with Jobberman the largest board and WhatsApp groups, Telegram channels, X threads and Instagram recruiter pages carrying a large share of postings informally ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/business-services/how-nigerian-job-seekers-are-using-aggregation-platforms-to-navigate-a-fragmented-market/)). A posting from a reputable company draws **200–500 applications, and 2,000+ for banks, multinationals or oil and gas** ([Techpoint Brand Press](https://techpoint.africa/brandpress/cvsense-launches-ai-recruitment-platform-to-help-companies-reduce-costly-hiring-mistakes/)); ROAM Africa says the average African listing attracts over 2,400 applicants ([ROAM Africa](https://www.roam.africa/an-average-job-listing-in-africa-attracts-over-2400-applicants/)), and a 2025 paramilitary drive drew 1.9 million applicants for 30,000 posts ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202508190095.html)). "No response" is the most common complaint about Jobberman across forums and app reviews ([Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/jobberman-review-2026-is-jobberman-legit-or-a-scam/)), which is structural at those ratios; a product that scores fit before the user applies and explains the gap per job supplies the feedback employers never give. Recruitment fraud is the most heavily documented pain point: fraudsters demand **₦5,000 to ₦500,000** for "processing, training, onboarding or medical tests" ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202508190095.html)), Dangote Group warned in August 2025 of scammers impersonating its HR on LinkedIn ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=508829)), and the Global Anti-Scam Alliance's 2026 Nigeria report found **84% of surveyed adults encountered a scam in the past year** ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/interview-how-profiled-nigeria-emmanuel-ajao-is-building-trust-infrastructur)). The fact that desperate seekers pay scammers ₦5,000+ for a "slot" proves willingness to pay for perceived access, and simultaneously proves that trust is the precondition for charging anything.

Device and data constraints shape the product. Nigeria had **109 million internet users (45.5% penetration)** at the end of 2025, essentially flat on a year earlier ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nigeria)); Android holds about **82% of mobile OS share** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile-operating-system/nigeria)); WhatsApp is used by over 95% of internet users aged 16+ ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1176101/leading-social-media-platforms-nigeria/)), roughly nine times LinkedIn's reach. After the NCC approved a 50% tariff rise in January 2025 the average 1GB bundle rose 65% to **₦475** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=928934)) and MTN's 1.8GB monthly plan became ₦1,500 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/02/11/mtn-nigeria-raises-internet-prices/)), so every megabyte the app consumes competes directly with the subscription price. Remote work is a complement, not the core: about 17% of Nigerian jobs are remote versus 28% globally, fewer than 10% of remote workers earn above $350/month, and **82% would prefer structured local employment** ([Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/07/remote-work-reshapes-nigerias-tech-workforce-widens-global-opportunities/amp/)). On the demand side, the WEF Future of Jobs 2025 Nigeria data relayed by LSETF shows **87% of Nigerian employers calling cybersecurity skills critical** (vs 70% globally), AI/ML specialists (+82%), data analysts (+41%) and sustainability experts (+33%) as the fastest-growing roles, and **65% of employers naming skill gaps a major barrier** ([LSETF](https://lsetf.ng/node/5678)).

What Nigerians already pay anchors the price. Human CV rewrites run ₦10,000 (0–2 years), ₦20,000 (3–9 years) and ₦30,000 (10+ years) at RightMyCV ([RightMyCV](https://rightmycv.onrender.com)) and ₦30,000 / ₦50,000 / ₦80,000 by seniority at MyJobMag ([MyJobMag](https://www.myjobmag.com/cv-rewrite)). Mass-market subscriptions have converged after 2024–25 hikes at Spotify ₦1,300 ([Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/2024/10/07/spotify-increases-price-for-its-nigerian-premium-subscribers/)), YouTube Premium ₦1,700 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/04/05/youtube-increases-youtube-premium-service-price-by-54-in-nigeria/)) and Netflix Mobile ₦2,500 ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/04/netflix-hikes-subscription-prices-in-nigeria-again-premium-plan-now-n8500/)), while ChatGPT Plus jumped to ₦31,500 when OpenAI aligned Nigeria to the global $20 rate ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/chatgpt-plus-price-in-nigeria-jumps-over-200/)). The national minimum wage is **₦70,000/month** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/nigeria/minimum-wages)), ALX charges learners $5/month ([ALX Africa](https://alxafrica.com/?p=16273)), and uLesson had to halve its prices in 2024 citing hardship ([Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/2024/02/20/ulesson-halves-educational-subscription-fees-nigeria/)). Inflation has eased to 15.91% (June 2026) with the naira near ₦1,380/$ ([Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/pwc-nigerias-inflation-halves-naira-steadies-as-reforms-take-hold)), which still means naira prices need review at least annually. The only direct spend evidence on upskilling is a 12-person sample reporting ₦462,500 a year including data ([Condia](https://thecondia.com/gen-z-cost-online-upskilling-nigeria/)), far too small to generalise, so **no representative willingness-to-pay survey exists and the founder must run one**.

## No incumbent closes the CV → match → learn loop, and Jobberman anchors the price at ₦12,000

The competitive map has a hole exactly where this product sits. Jobberman, owned by ROAM Africa's The African Talent Company, claims 2.65 million users and a seeker audience of 606,000 ([Jobberman](https://www.jobberman.com/about)), ranks #212 in Nigeria on Similarweb ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/jobberman.com/competitors/)), added 329,432 new job seekers in 2025 ([Jobberman 2025 Wrapped](https://www.jobberman.com/discover/2025-wrapped-our-winning-year-at-jobberman-nigeria)), and is the only incumbent selling a naira-priced AI career tool to seekers: **₦5,200 for a week, ₦12,000 for a month, ₦35,000 for three months**, entered through a free CV review ([Jobberman AI Career Tool](https://www.jobberman.com/account/cv-services)). Its revenue is still overwhelmingly employer-side, with a free basic listing and a ₦100,000 Standard listing ([Jobberman Employer](https://www.jobberman.com/employer)). MyJobMag (Nigeria rank #287) sells human CV rewrites and ₦7,500 featured posts ([MyJobMag Employers](https://www.myjobmag.com/employers/)); its most common complaint is roles that appear open but were already filled ([Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/myjobmag-review-2026-is-it-legit-for-job-seekers-in-nigeria/)). Reviewers note that scam listings using convincing company names "slip through verification" even at Jobberman ([Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/jobberman-review-2026-is-jobberman-legit-or-a-scam/)).

A cluster of small Nigerian AI tools has appeared since 2024, none of which combines all four pieces. Abeni AI scores a CV against a posting in under 30 seconds and sells credit packs at **₦5,500 (15 analyses), ₦8,500 (30) and ₦12,500 (60)** ([Abeni AI](https://myabeni.com/)). HirePadi is the closest analogue, offering AI match scores, CV tailoring and a "Daily Job Digest" built from Adzuna, MyJobMag, Jooble, JSearch and other feeds, but it is free with no paid tier found ([HirePadi](https://www.hirepadi.com/)). The only venture-backed Nigerian-founded player, Sorce (YC F25, swipe-to-apply), targets the US market ([Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/feature/ai-swipe-for-jobs-viral/)), a signal that investors doubt Nigerian consumer willingness to pay. Free Telegram channels are the de facto alert layer, with @jobnetworkng showing about **54,300 subscribers** ([Telegram](https://t.me/jobnetworkng)), all unpersonalised and unverified. Globally, AI CV builders cluster at $19–50/month with heavy annual discounting, and Resume.io's $2.95 trial rolling into $29.95 every four weeks is "the root cause of most negative reviews" ([AI Tools Police](https://aitoolspolice.com/reviews/resume-io/)); at the plan's ₦1,400/$ anchor (see Pricing) those prices translate to ₦28,000–70,000/month, and Nigerian banks' FX caps on naira cards make USD subscriptions fail silently ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/its-not-your-nigerian-customers-ghosting-you-it-s-your-checkout-4aaae911c8)). On the learning side, Coursera Career Academy organises courses by job role ([Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/career-academy)) and the federal 3MTT programme launched a placement portal in October 2025 ([FMCIDE](https://fmcide.gov.ng/ministry-announces-3mtt-njfp-placement-initiative-to-connect-skilled-digital-talent-with-employers-nationwide/)), but no source shows any platform deriving a learning path from a user's own CV gaps and then re-matching them.

The gap to own is therefore a combined loop (CV → fit-scored matches → learning path when the CV is weak → re-match) wrapped in a trust layer (verified employers, listing freshness, a "no fee to candidates" rule and a public scam watch) and sold at a transparent naira price with cancel-anytime billing. Jobberman's ₦12,000/month and Abeni's packs define a market-tested ceiling around ₦10,000–15,000/month, while HirePadi at ₦0 defines the floor for an aggregated digest; the defensible position sits well below Jobberman on price and well above HirePadi on relevance and trust. The cautionary record matters as much as the gap: Edukoya shut down in February 2025 despite a $3.5 million pre-seed, citing market readiness and low household purchasing power ([Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/2025/02/26/edukoya-shuts-down/)); Decagon pivoted away from training in March 2025 after loan defaults and uLesson halved prices ([AllBusiness Africa](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/state-of-african-edtech-2026)); Kibo School closed in 2024 "due to challenges in finding a sustainable student financing model" ([Kibo Insider](https://kiboinsider.substack.com/p/navigating-student-financing-lessons)); and Eden Life paused consumer subscriptions after a unit-economics audit because a cleaner or cook can be hired over WhatsApp for less ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/02/16/techcabal-daily-locked-out-of-eden/)). The lesson is consistent: in Nigeria a consumer subscription survives only when the payer is the beneficiary, the price is a few dollars, and the product delivers something the user cannot arrange for free on WhatsApp. An AI-written CV competes with free ChatGPT; verified, fit-scored distribution to real employers does not.

## Keep "Build your value" as the tagline and brand as Ladda

"Build Your Value" is clear about what the product does and weak as a brand. A web search for the phrase plus Nigeria returned only business-valuation and construction firms ([Aviaan Accounting](https://aviaanaccounting.com/business-valuation-companies-in-nigeria/)), meaning no existing owner surfaced but also that "value" reads as finance in Nigerian search results. It is a three-word descriptive phrase, which trademark registries treat as weak; "buildyourvalue" is 14 characters, within X's limit but long and likely to collide with motivational-coaching accounts worldwide; the acronym BYV is unmemorable. It works well as the campaign line under a shorter brand, which is how this plan treats it.

Of the fifteen alternatives the research generated, five survive the obvious conflict screens: **Ladda** (phonetic "ladder", two syllables, meaning survives in any African market), **Afara** (Yoruba for "bridge", soft and feminine-friendly), **CareerPadi** ("padi" is Pidgin for close friend; warm and trustworthy but Pidgin-bound and "Padi" is already used by Nigerian payment apps), **Worthi** (plays on "know your worth", a phrase Nigerians use about salary) and **Valuely** (keeps the working name's equity but still sounds like fintech). Names that must be avoided include anything built on "Sabi" (an existing well-funded Nigerian B2B startup), "Jara" (used by Nigerian loyalty apps), "Shortlistd" (too close to Shortlist, the East African hiring startup) and "Stedi" (a US software company). The recommendation is **Ladda as the primary brand**, with "Build your value" as the tagline, because it is the only shortlisted name that is short, portable across Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya, pronounceable in every Nigerian language, free of fintech connotation and semantically tied to the product's progression metaphor (rungs, climbing, "next rung" paths); Afara is the runner-up if the founder wants a Nigerian-rooted name with the same portability, and CareerPadi is the choice only if the business will stay Nigeria-only, since its warmth is its strength and Pidgin is its ceiling. Every one of these verdicts rests on the author's assessment, not sourced facts, and every availability claim is unverified: the research environment could not reach Verisign RDAP, the IANA bootstrap for .ng and .africa, the CAC public search, or X and Instagram, so the founder must personally run five checks before committing: (1) .com and .africa availability at a registrar such as Namecheap, (2) .ng and .com.ng at whois.nic.net.ng or a NiRA-accredited registrar such as Whogohost or QServers, (3) company and business-name conflicts at search.cac.gov.ng, (4) Classes 35, 41 and 42 at the Nigerian Trademarks Registry (iponigeria.com), and (5) direct checks of x.com/NAME, instagram.com/NAME and tiktok.com/@NAME. Register the .ng, .com.ng and .com together, or pick the next name. Because the platform ships as a SoundTechWorks solution (decided: [ADR-0002](adr/0002-soundtechworks-builds-and-owns-the-product.md)), the product sits under SoundTechWorks, which must be a limited company for Startup Act labelling and investment (a business name cannot take either), with the product brand registered as a trading name of that company, and the parent name should appear in the footer, privacy notice and Paystack merchant descriptor so the charge on a user's bank statement matches the brand they trust.

## Price at ₦1,999 under Paystack's fee cliff and sell on the web, not in the app store

Payment mechanics, not just affordability, dictate the price points. Paystack charges **1.5% + ₦100 on local cards, capped at ₦2,000, and waives the ₦100 on transactions under ₦2,500** ([Paystack](https://support2.paystack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360009881920-What-are-Paystack-s-transaction-charges)), so a ₦1,999 charge costs ₦30 (1.5%) while ₦2,500 costs ₦137.50 (5.5%) and ₦5,000 costs ₦175 (3.5%); the ₦2,500–4,000 band is the worst zone and should be avoided. Flutterwave's local rate is disputed between 1.4% + ₦100 and a combined 2.0% after an April 2025 increase ([Brands.ng](https://brands.ng/blog/interswitch-vs-paystack-vs-flutterwave-how-nigerias-top-payment-gateways-really-compare-in-2026/); [Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-vs-flutterwave-pricing-nigeria-2026)). Google Play takes 15% on subscriptions ([PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/google-play-subscription-fees-2026-real-math)) and Apple's Small Business Program 15% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/small-business-program.md)), ten times Paystack's cut, which settles the channel question: sell through web checkout in the PWA and never route Nigerian subscriptions through app-store billing. Paystack subscriptions bill a saved card authorisation after an initial 2FA charge and support cards and NIBSS direct debit only ([Paystack docs](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/recurring-charges)); direct debit mandates are created by a small bank transfer and work with 20+ banks ([Paystack](https://paystack.com/blog/product/direct-debit-waitlist)), but a third-party NIBSS integration guide says transactions below ₦5,000 are "generally not cost-effective" ([Lendsqr](https://docs.lendsqr.com/receiving-payments-with-nibss-direct-debit)), so reserve direct debit for quarterly and annual plans. Mobile wallets have no native recurring mandate, and vendor estimates put involuntary renewal failure at 8–14% per cycle for cards with insufficient balance the top cause ([Kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/dunning-failed-recurring-mobile-money-payments-nigeria-2026)); these are directional, but for unemployed users with volatile balances the design response is clear: let users pick their charge date, run 3–5 dunning retries over 7–10 days with WhatsApp or SMS reminders, accept bank transfer, USSD and OPay/PalmPay top-ups as a fallback, and prefer prepaid passes over auto-renewal at the bottom tier.

The revenue arithmetic shows why consumer subscriptions cannot be the only line. Freemium conversion averages 3.7% across SaaS and only 2.6% in edtech ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/freemium-saas/)); most consumer apps convert under 2% of downloads to paid and top performers about 4% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/app-engagement-metrics-subscription-apps.md)). At 100,000 free users, 3% conversion and a blended ARPU of ₦2,500, monthly gross revenue is about ₦7.5 million (≈$5,400 at the FX anchor of **₦1,400 per US dollar (October 2026)**, the single dated rate used for every dollar conversion in this plan and in BRD §14), and 5–10% monthly churn with 20–40% of it from failed payments ([MEAN CEO](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=10240)) must be refilled continuously. Three supplements are therefore structural. Employer fees are anchored by Jobberman's free basic and ₦100,000 standard listing ([Jobberman brochure](https://static.jobberman.com/Jobberman_Nigeria_Product_Brochure_2025.pdf)). Course affiliates pay up to 45% on Coursera Specializations and Professional Certificates through Impact ([Coursera](https://coursera.org/about/affiliates)) and a 10% base on Udemy with a 7-day cookie and mobile-app sales excluded ([Udemy](https://partnersupport.udemy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049412813-What-is-the-Commission)); the per-user amounts are small but Coursera certificates at 45% are worth routing to. B2B2C and B2G contracts are the pattern that kept Jobberman alive through its Mastercard Foundation partnership and Zeraki alive by selling to schools ([AllBusiness Africa](https://allbusiness.africa/insights/african-edtech-adult-learning-pivot)); LSETF-style employability programmes that pay per trained or placed participant should be in the plan from month one, not as an afterthought. Pricing must be reviewed at least annually against inflation, and a dollar-denominated tier is defensible only for the remote-work and diaspora segment.

## Ship a PWA that produces a CV before asking for money, then phase in learning paths and partnerships

The MVP's job is to prove three things in order: that a Nigerian graduate will complete a CV and set alert preferences in one session, that a fit-scored digest gets opened and acted on, and that some fraction pays ₦1,999. Everything that does not test those three belongs in a later phase. Onboarding should be phone-number-first with OTP over SMS and automatic WhatsApp and voice fallback, because SMS OTPs to Nigerian numbers fail often enough that at least one app team found they only arrived via WhatsApp ([Onde community](https://community.onde.app/t/driver-app-not-sending-otp/13389)), with Google sign-in as the second path. The first session must stay under a few megabytes and end with a downloadable CV and a weakness score before any payment screen appears, mirroring how ALX and uLesson earned trust with free or cheap access. Trust cues belong in the MVP, not Phase 2: SoundTechWorks' CAC registration number in the footer with a visible "a SoundTechWorks solution" line, a named and photographed founding team, a published employer-verification policy, a corporate email domain, Paystack checkout, a 7-day refund and a prominent "we never charge you to apply, and no employer here may charge you" statement, because Nigerian caution "reflects accumulated experience of fraud" and trust must be "part of the foundation from day one" ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/interview-how-profiled-nigeria-emmanuel-ajao-is-building-trust-infrastructur)).

Two scope decisions deserve emphasis. First, do not build a native app before Phase 3: Android is ~82% of traffic, a PWA is installable and push-capable on Android Chrome, and app-store billing would cost 15% of every naira. Second, do not scrape LinkedIn, Indeed or Glassdoor even though open-source tools make it trivial; the hiQ case ended with hiQ paying $500,000 and accepting a permanent injunction after losing on contract grounds despite winning the CFAA point ([Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)), and a subscription product cannot carry that risk.

## Run matching on pgvector plus a cheap reranker; variable cost lands at $0.10–0.30 per user

No major free job API covers Nigeria. Adzuna's XML specification lists 19 countries ending at South Africa ([Adzuna](https://www.adzuna.co.uk/jobs/xml-specification.html)), Careerjet's official client lists 67 locales with no en_NG ([Careerjet on GitHub](https://github.com/careerjet/careerjet-api-client-python)), and Indeed's Publisher API is discontinued with remaining APIs employer-side only ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/indeed-publisher-api)). None of Jobberman, MyJobMag or Hot Nigerian Jobs publishes a developer feed. The practical route to Nigerian local roles is a Google-for-Jobs-derived aggregator that indexes the JobPosting structured data those boards embed for Google visibility ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/data-types/job-posting)): JSearch on RapidAPI at roughly **$30/month for 10,000 requests** ([CodeWords](https://www.codewords.ai/blog/jsearch-api)), SerpApi's Google Jobs engine from $50/month for 5,000 searches ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/compare-products/serpapi)), or DataForSEO at $0.0006 per page of ten results ([DataForSEO](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/serp/google-jobs-serp-api)). Querying 50–100 Nigerian role-and-city combinations daily costs about $30–130/month at launch. For remote roles, Remotive's public API is free but delayed 24 hours, limited to a few requests a day, requires a link back and credit, and prohibits displaying its jobs "in order to collect signups/email addresses" ([Remotive](https://github.com/remotive-com/remote-jobs-api)), so Remotive listings must stay attributed and outside any signup wall, with its paid private API as the upgrade. Bulk vendors such as TheirStack (from a free 200-credit tier to $1,500/month for a million credits, one credit per job) ([TheirStack](https://theirstack.com/en/pricing)) become worthwhile at scale, but the highest-value source is a direct feed from Jobberman or MyJobMag, so the ingestion layer must be source-agnostic around a normalised JobPosting-like schema from the start. De-duplication is mandatory because the same role appears with different wording on every board and true duplicates can share as little as 37% textual similarity ([Textkernel](https://www.textkernel.com/newsroom/online-job-postings-have-many-duplicates-but-how-can-you-detect-them-if-they-are-not-exact-copies-of-each-other/)); the design is MinHash/LSH on ~7-word shingles for recall, an embedding cosine threshold plus agreement on company, title, location and date for confirmation, and connected-component clustering that keeps the earliest, most complete record with all source URLs. The scam filter is a cheap rules layer first (fee mentions, webmail-only contact, WhatsApp-only application, missing company domain, "no experience needed" with high pay) drawn from Jobberman's own guidance that candidates "are never supposed to pay to be interviewed or given a job" ([Jobberman](https://www.jobberman.com/discover/identify-job-scams)) and Africa Check's observation that agencies never recruit through Gmail or Yahoo addresses ([Africa Check](https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/meta-programme-fact-checks/scam-alert-nigerias-customs-service-isnt-recruiting-ignore)), followed by an LLM classifier on borderline cases, a verified-employer domain whitelist and user reporting.

The CV layer should stand on open standards and permissive open source. JSON Resume is the de facto MIT-licensed schema for a résumé as structured JSON ([JSON Resume](https://github.com/jsonresume/resume-schema)); extend it with ESCO skill URIs, target roles, location preferences and salary expectation in naira. Reactive Resume (MIT) offers 18 forkable templates with ATS-safe filters and in-app PDF generation ([Reactive Resume](https://github.com/AmruthPillai/Reactive-Resume)); Resume Matcher (Apache-2.0) supplies a deterministic ATS score that makes no LLM call ([Resume Matcher](https://github.com/srbhr/Resume-Matcher)); OpenResume is AGPL-3.0 and must not be embedded server-side in a proprietary SaaS ([OpenResume](https://github.com/xitanggg/open-resume)). Parse uploaded CVs with text extraction plus an LLM structured-output call (a few thousand tokens, well under a cent) and move to a commercial parser such as Affinda at about $0.10/document ([Affinda](https://www.affinda.com/resume-parser/pricing/)) only if Nigerian CV formats defeat it. Render PDF through headless Chromium and DOCX through the MIT `docx` library ([docx](https://github.com/dolanmiu/docx)).

Matching follows the three-stage pattern practitioners and researchers converge on: hard filters, vector recall, then an LLM rerank on the shortlist, with résumés and postings normalised into structured summaries before embedding and embeddings keyed to content hashes so stale postings get re-embedded ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/ai-job-matching.md)); embedding-only ranking "lacks controllability and explainability", which is why ConFit v3 re-ranks the top-N with an LLM ([ConFit v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09760)). Concretely: embed every new job once with text-embedding-3-small at **$0.02 per million tokens** ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/text-embedding-3-small)) into pgvector, whose HNSW index and `halfvec` type handle 1,536-dimension vectors comfortably ([pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector)) and which is "almost always the cheapest production-grade choice" below about five million vectors ([spendark](https://spendark.com/blog/vector-database-pricing/)); per user, embed a profile summary and pull the top 300 filtered by location, remote flag and seniority; then rerank the top 30 in batches of ten with a rubric (must-have skills, seniority, location, salary) that emits a 0–100 score and a one-line "why" for the email. On a Haiku-class model priced at $0.10 input / $0.50 output per million tokens ([Anthropic](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing)) that rerank costs about **$0.005 per user per week**; on GPT-5 mini at $0.25 / $2.00 ([anotherwrapper](https://anotherwrapper.com/llm-pricing/gpt-5-mini-2025-08-07/grok-4.1-thinking-2025-11-17)) about $0.015; reranking all 300 jobs without the embedding stage costs ten times more, which is why the embedding stage exists. CV generation at roughly 3,000 input and 2,500 output tokens costs under a cent on small models and about three cents on a Sonnet-class model; use the stronger model only for CV writing and the cheap one for everything else, cache the user-profile prefix, and run nightly reranks through batch APIs at 50% off.

Delivery is email-first for cost reasons. Amazon SES is about **$0.10 per 1,000 emails** ([CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/email-api/amazon-ses/)) and Resend's Pro plan covers 50,000 emails for $20 ([Resend](https://www.resend.com/pricing.md)), so 17 digests a month per user is immaterial. Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require SPF, DKIM and aligned DMARC, RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe honoured within two days, and a spam rate under 0.3% (ideally under 0.1%) ([dmarcian](https://dmarcian.com/yahoo-and-google-dmarc-required/)); send from a dedicated subdomain, warm up gradually and watch Postmaster Tools. WhatsApp is the attention channel but not a cheap one: Meta moved to per-message billing on 1 July 2025 with marketing, utility and authentication templates charged per delivered message ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)), and Nigerian press puts utility messages at about $0.0101 (~₦14) and marketing at about **$0.062 (~₦84)** per message ([DUBAWA](https://dubawa.org/misleading-whatsapp-not-set-to-charge-nigerians-n14-per-message/)), figures the research could not verify against Meta's rate card. A weekly "jobs for you" message is a marketing template, so it costs roughly ₦340–370 per user per month, 25–60 times the AI compute behind it; it belongs in the Pro tier or as utility-only notifications (CV ready, new verified match) with free-form replies inside the 24-hour service window. SMS at ₦5.62–7.00 per page ([BulkSMS Nigeria](https://www.bulksmsnigeria.com/resources/sms-pricing-calculator)) via Termii ([Termii](https://termii.com/developers)) is for OTP only.

The lean stack is Next.js (App Router, PWA manifest today; service worker and Web Push arrive in Phase 1 via Serwist, Turbopack compatibility unverified) on Vercel Pro at $20/seat or a Hetzner VPS via Coolify once usage bills grow; Supabase Pro at $25/month with Postgres, pgvector, Storage and Auth covering up to 100,000 monthly active users ([JetAdmin](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/supabase-pricing-2026-guide-to-plans-limits-and-real-world-costs/)), whereas Clerk would cost about $1,825/month at that scale ([apicalculators](https://apicalculators.com/compare/clerk-vs-supabase-auth)); Inngest or Trigger.dev (or pg-boss on the VPS) for nightly ingestion, embedding, matching and digest fan-out; and Cloudflare in front, which has had a Lagos point of presence since 2018 ([btw.media](https://btw.media/en/cloudflare-enhancing-internet-infrastructure-in-africa)). AWS's only full African region is Cape Town, with a Lagos Local Zone and CloudFront edge ([Connecting Africa](https://connectingafrica.com/cloud-networking/lagos-launches-first-aws-local-zone-in-africa)), so place the database in London (eu-west-2) by default, moving to Frankfurt only if measured Lagos round-trip times say so (Cape Town is not a Supabase region, per an unverified snippet), and rely on the CDN edge for perceived speed. Keep initial JavaScript under 150 KB gzipped, enforced by a CI budget file, serve WebP/AVIF, avoid autoplay media and ship plain-text MIME alternatives for digests.

## Score CV weakness on six components and route each failure to a learning module

"Weak CV" has to be measurable or the learning-path trigger is arbitrary. Two industry rubrics supply the parts. Jobscan's match rate scores a CV against one job description, weighting hard skills above job title above soft skills, and recommends 75–80% ([Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/what-jobscan-match-rate-should-i-aim-for/)); Resume Worded's job-agnostic quality score checks impact and quantified achievements, growth and leadership signals, brevity, action verbs, buzzwords, section completeness and ATS compatibility, with 85+ "good" and 90+ passing all checks and thresholds that adapt to seniority ([Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/llm-info); [Resume Worded score guide](https://resumeworded.com/score-guide)). Harvard's career guides supply the editorial rules: results over duties, action verbs over passive language, skimmability ([Harvard OCS](https://cdn-careerservices.fas.harvard.edu:443/wp-content/uploads/sites/161/2023/08/College-resume-and-cover-letter-4.pdf)). Resume Matcher's open implementation shows a deterministic blend (keyword match 40%, skills coverage 20%, title match 15%, section completeness 15%, date consistency 10%) that needs no LLM call ([Resume Matcher](https://github.com/srbhr/Resume-Matcher)). Resume Worded's critique that soft skills should be evidenced through accomplishments rather than listed ([Resume Worded](https://resumeworded.com/compare)) is worth adopting.

The recommended score is a weighted sum of six components, each mapped to a remedy. First, target-role skill coverage: the share of the top-N demanded skills for the user's chosen role that appear in the CV, where demand weights come from the frequency of each ESCO skill in the platform's own last-90-days Nigerian postings for that title; coverage below 50% flags "skills gap" and routes to a learning path. Second, achievement quality: share of bullets containing a number and share opening with a strong verb, penalising "Assisted", "Helped" and "Responsible for"; failure routes to a guided rewrite with Nigerian examples. Third, completeness: contact, summary, skills, education, experience and certifications sections present, plus NYSC status for graduates, since many formal roles require the discharge certificate. Fourth, tenure and gap flags, handled by a short "explain the gap" module rather than penalty. Fifth, parse-ability: single column, standard headings, no tables or images, exported as PDF or DOCX. Sixth, role-specific missing credentials, such as no ICAN or ACCA for an accounting target where Big Four firms "explicitly prefer or require" them ([Profolio.ng](https://www.profolio.ng/accountant/best-certifications)), or no recognised certificate for a cybersecurity target. Thresholds must flex by seniority as Resume Worded's do, so a fresh NYSC graduate is not penalised for lacking leadership signals.

ESCO is the right skill ontology because it is free for commercial use: version 1.2.1 (December 2025) is downloadable as CSV, JSON-LD and RDF, and the API is licensed under EUPL 1.2 with components under Apache 2.0 ([ESCO](https://esco.ec.europa.eu/ar/use-esco/use-esco-services-api)). Lightcast's Open Skills library (33,000+ skills, 75,000+ titles) is richer but its free tier is for nonprofits and public-good initiatives, with "commercial or production use" requiring a licence ([Lightcast](https://docs.lightcast.io/lightcast-api/docs/api-access)). The MIT-licensed esco-skill-extractor embeds ESCO skills and input text with a sentence transformer and matches by cosine similarity at a 0.6 threshold ([esco-skill-extractor](https://github.com/KonstantinosPetrakis/esco-skill-extractor)); the alternative is an LLM structured-output call constrained to ESCO labels. Published career-path methods score candidate occupations by the share of linked ESCO skills already present in the résumé ([arXiv 2310.15636](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.15636)), which is the same calculation inverted. A Nigeria-specific layer (ICAN/ACCA, NYSC, local tools, Pidgin role synonyms) has to be added by hand.

Courses map to gaps by embedding course titles and descriptions into the same pgvector table tagged as courses, retrieving the nearest for each gap skill, and letting an LLM assemble a three-step path with an explicit time budget. Because catalogue APIs are gated (Coursera's reportedly for approved affiliates only, edX's in beta, Udemy's unconfirmed), start with a hand-curated CSV of about 500 courses weighted toward free content: 3MTT when a cohort is open, ALX (which self-reports 347,100 graduates and 63% employed within six months, with no published completion rate ([ALX Africa](https://www.alxafrica.com/news-press/africas-talent-was-never-the-problem-alx-expands-access-across-the-continent/))), Google Career Certificates via Coursera's financial-aid fee waiver ([Coursera](https://blog.coursera.org/courseras-financial-aid-what-it-is-and-who-is)), which have passed one million graduates with about 70% of US graduates reporting a positive career outcome ([Google](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/grow-with-google/google-career-certificates-impact-report-may-2025/)), and Alison. Coursera Plus is localised for Nigeria at **$24/month or $160/year** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=1019597)) for users who can pay. Outcome claims must be labelled "provider-reported" and never implied to be Nigerian placement rates; 3MTT's own minister conceded in December 2025 that placing "over 15,000" of nearly 390,000 fellows was "small" ([Channels TV](https://www.channelstv.com/2025/12/12/how-3mtt-will-translate-to-jobs-bosun-tijani/)), which is precisely the training-to-job gap the platform exists to close by converting training into CV evidence and matches.

Path design should borrow what demonstrably improves completion. Self-paced MOOCs complete at a median of 6.5% of enrollees ([Ruzuku](https://www.ruzuku.com/learn/articles/cohort-vs-self-paced-completion-study)); Udacity's project-plus-mentor Nanodegrees report 34% graduation and over 60% with personalised mentorship ([Udacity](https://www.udacity.com/blog/?p=5498)), with half of 61,000 project reviews returned within 2.5 hours and 88% within 24 hours ([Georgia Tech Lucy Labs](https://lucylabs.gatech.edu/expert-evaluation-of-300-projects-per-day)). Coursera Career Academy frames each role as 6–8 months at 3–10 hours a week ([UTPB](https://careers.utpb.edu/resources/career-academy/)). The pattern to copy is role-first entry, an explicit time budget ("4 months at 5 hours a week"), one portfolio project per milestone, fast feedback (which an LLM reviewer can provide same-day at negligible cost), and a re-match that visibly improves the user's fit score when a module is completed, so the loop closes. Expect single-digit completion for purely self-paced paths and treat cohort dates or sponsorship as the Phase 3 lever. For remote-work paths, pair any BPO, virtual-assistant or support track with a "getting paid" module, since PayPal is "effectively unusable" for Nigerian freelancers ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/best-platform-to-withdraw-from-upwork-in-nigeria-2026)) and the 2025 tax reform taxes residents on worldwide income ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/what-you-need-to-know-as-tech-freelancers-to-brace-for-2025-tax-reform/)). The demand-ranked shortlist for curated paths in Nigeria is cybersecurity, data analysis and AI/ML, software development, accounting with ICAN/ACCA, customer support and BPO (the federal government is channelling 3MTT fellows into BPO jobs ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/technology/fg-bpo-companies-partner-to-create-jobs-for-3mtt-fellows/))), and skilled trades where up to 80% of artisans are reportedly foreign and welders, electricians and plumbers are chronically scarce ([TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/how-nigeria-can-reverse-the-artisan-shortage)). The oft-quoted "85% of graduates lack digital skills" comes from a roughly 100-person survey of NYSC members ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=2199459)) and should not be repeated in marketing without that caveat.

## Launch through NYSC batch dates, campus scholars and X, with trust as the product

Nigerian consumer growth has a documented playbook. Kuda pays ₦1,000 to referrer and referee but only after the new user confirms BVN, adds an ID and buys ₦500 of airtime ([Kuda](https://help.kuda.com/en/articles/8954699-claim-your-referral-rewards)), a gate that exists because cash referrals get farmed; Cowrywise instead rewards both sides with a fee waiver tied to its core product ([Cowrywise](https://help.cowrywise.com/en/articles/5591470-how-referral-bonuses-work-on-cowrywise)). The career-platform equivalent is a month of Core or an extra tailoring for each referred user who completes a CV, not cash. Campus programmes work when framed as a credential: Cowrywise's Ambassador Scholars receive **₦200,000 a year** plus mentorship and must serve as ambassadors ([Cowrywise](https://ambassadors.cowrywise.com/scholarship)), reaching 80+ campuses ([Cowrywise](https://cowrywise.com/ambassadors)); final-year students and NYSC-bound graduates need CVs anyway, so a "Ladda Scholars" programme of twenty ₦100,000 awards buys both distribution and credibility for roughly ₦2 million a year. Paid reach is cheap: Meta CPM in Nigeria is about **$1.50 (≈₦2,100) with CPC of ₦30–300** ([Grey](https://grey.co/blog/facebook-ads-cost-nigeria)), TikTok has about 37.4 million Nigerian users, roughly level with Facebook ([Intelpoint](https://intelpoint.co/insights/tiktok-closely-rivals-facebook-in-nigeria-with-tiktoks-37-40m-users-representing-15-8-of-the-national-population/)), and X has only about 7.57 million ([Intelpoint](https://intelpoint.co/insights/nigeria-with-7-57m-active-twitter-users-is-a-significant-contributor-to-the-total-9-9m-share-of-west-africas-twitter-users/)) but is where Nigeria's tech, HR and journalist conversation lives. So X is the launch and credibility channel, TikTok and WhatsApp Status are the mass channels for 18–30-year-olds, and installs under ₦1,000 are plausible if creative is strong; the binding constraint is conversion to paid, not reach. Nigerian Gen Z are mobile-first, trust creators over celebrities, and read forced youth slang as fake ([Marketing Edge](https://marketingedge.com.ng/gen-zs-are-not-distracted-your-brand-is-just-irrelevant/)), so the brand account should speak standard Nigerian English and leave Pidgin to creators. SEO is a long game: MyJobMag draws about 130,000 monthly organic visits ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/websites/myjobmag.com)) and "jobs in abuja" alone has about 112,100 monthly searches ([Semrush](https://tr.semrush.com/website/myjobmag.com/overview)); expect 12+ months to rank, and publish structured city-tagged job pages and "how to write a CV for X" content from day one so it compounds.

Timing beats spend. NYSC batches put hundreds of thousands of graduates into simultaneous CV-writing mode on public dates, and the scheme's mobilisation partners are financial bodies rather than career startups ([VON](https://von.gov.ng/nysc-partners-with-stakeholders-to-strengthen-orientation-exercises/)), so there is no documented precedent for a camp activation and the realistic route is WhatsApp groups, alumni channels and ambassadors timed to registration and passing-out dates. Institutional partnerships have clearer precedents: LSETF's Get Hired programme with King's Trust requires applicants aged 18–35 to own a smartphone and runs a recruitment fair with 22 employer partners ([MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/call-for-applications-lsetf-x-kings-trust-international-get-hired-programme-2026/)); its UNDP project built a verified employer database placing trained candidates within 48 hours ([UNDP](https://undp.org/node/251061)); and its Makoko pilot with Jobberman reported 44% placement ([Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2018/05/28/lsetf-partners-jobberman-others-to-empower-unemployed-youths-in-communities/)). That 48-hour verified-match database is the B2G version of this product's engine, and pitching the platform as the digital layer for LSETF and its Kaduna and Edo equivalents is the credible wedge. Telco zero-rating has gone to school-facing edtech (MTN won NCC approval for a 500MB daily Edves bundle ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=77532)) and ALTON telcos endorsed a federal 100MB/day education programme starting 1 October 2026 ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/economy/1730940-mtn-airtel-telcos-endorse-fgs-free-daily-data-nigerian-students/))), so a zero-rated bundle is possible but slow and framed as "educational", which the learning path supports. Faith groups and cooperatives have no documented precedent as formal partners and should be treated as community channels reached through ambassadors.

Positioning should name the outcomes seekers already name: "get shortlisted", "fix my CV", "upskill" and, carefully, "japa-ready", since emigration remains the dominant career frame and tech is seen as a "ticket out" ([Ventures Africa](https://venturesafrica.substack.com/p/japa-syndrome-1262813)) even as UK-bound migration fell from 141,000 to about 52,000 ([ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/?p=251245)). "Build the value that gets you hired here or abroad" serves both camps without taking a side. Gender inclusion is a positioning opportunity as well as an ethic: only six of 93 surveyed Nigerian tech firms had a woman in top management ([ONE](https://www.one.org/africa/stories/nigerias-tech-sector-is-rising-but-its-leaving-women-behind/)) and the Naija Girls in Tech initiative launched in December 2025 targets 100,000 girls and young women by 2030 ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512230063.html)), so a visible women-in-tech track with female mentors differentiates from Jobberman's generic positioning. Target benchmarks for the first year, derived from global data and Nigerian anchors rather than any published Nigerian cohort: 60%+ of sign-ups complete a CV and set an alert, OTP drop-off under 15%, digest open rate 35–50% (vendor claims of 95% WhatsApp open rates are not planning numbers), free-to-paid 2–3% in months one to six rising to 4–5% by month twelve, monthly churn under 10% then under 7%, blended CAC of ₦700–1,500 per activated free user with payback inside three subscription months, and a published quarterly "shortlist rate" against the 44–50% placement bar that LSETF pilots set for funders.

## Register with NDPC before the 200th data subject: the compliance checklist

A CV platform is a data controller processing large volumes of personal data, so data protection is the gating item. The Nigeria Data Protection Commission issued the General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID) on 20 March 2025, replacing the NDPR 2019 ([Templars](https://templars-law.com/app/uploads/2025/04/NDPC-Issues-the-Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-General-Application-and-Implementation-Directive-2025.pdf)); GAID Article 9 requires controllers "of major importance" to register, with Ordinary High Level entities renewing annually and higher tiers filing an annual Compliance Audit Return ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/2025/05/Review%20of%20the%20NDPA%20General%20Application%20and%20Implementation%20Directive%20GAID%202025.pdf)). Registration fees are ₦250,000 (UHL: banks, telcos, payment gateways), ₦100,000 (EHL) and **₦10,000 (OHL: SMEs)** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/news/legal-business/article/key-highlights-of-the-guidance-notice-on-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance/)); sources disagree on whether the base threshold is more than 200 data subjects in six months with tier placement by additional factors, or 1,000 and 5,000 subjects for the higher tiers ([Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1456402/ndpc-guidance-notice-registration-of-data-controllers-and-processors-in-nigeria-explained)), so register as OHL as soon as qualifying processing begins and re-tier with counsel as volume grows. GAID Article 18(1)(a) requires consent for direct marketing ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/privacy-protection/1700306/gaid-2025-unpacked-series-ii-data-protection-officers-mandatory-consents-and-emerging-technologies)), and the Federal High Court in Araka v. Jumia and Eat 'N' Go held that processing a phone number for contract performance did not extend to unsolicited marketing ([Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/consent-vs-legitimate-interest-nigeria/)). The product therefore needs three separate, unticked checkboxes (CV processing and AI matching; job-alert emails as the contracted service; promotional and partner emails as direct marketing), one-click unsubscribe honoured immediately, and a privacy notice that discloses automated profiling and offers human review of match decisions.

## The biggest risk is that Nigerians will not pay; measure shortlist rate, not downloads

The risk register starts with demand. No representative willingness-to-pay data exists; the one Nigerian-founded team with venture backing chose the US; and the 2024–26 record of consumer edtech is a string of shutdowns and price cuts. The mitigation is the Phase 0 paid pilot and a pricing structure that leads with a one-off outcome purchase rather than a subscription. The second risk is competitive response: Jobberman already has an AI tool, 2.65 million users, a biweekly jobseeker newsletter and a Telegram channel, and could bundle a digest tomorrow; the defence is the loop (learning path plus re-match) and the trust layer, neither of which incumbents have shipped, and a partnership posture that makes Jobberman a data supplier rather than only a rival. Third, data-source fragility: aggregator APIs can change price or terms, Remotive's rules forbid gating, and any scraping shortcut invites the hiQ outcome; the mitigation is a source-agnostic schema and an early push for a direct board feed. Fourth, channel cost: WhatsApp marketing at roughly ₦84 per message would erase the margin of a ₦1,999 plan if bundled, so it stays in Pro and utility-only. Fifth, payment failure: 8–14% involuntary churn per cycle on cards and worse on wallets means dunning, transfer fallbacks and prepaid passes are revenue infrastructure, not polish. Sixth, episodic retention: job search ends when the user is hired, so retention must come from weekly value (digest, learning progress) and re-activation at NYSC and exam cycles, and "re-subscription within 90 days" is a better metric than monthly churn alone. Seventh, macro: inflation near 16% and a naira near ₦1,380/$ require annual price reviews, while slowing japa redirects ambition to remote and better local work, which the product serves. Eighth, reputational contagion: the whole "pay for job help" category is tainted by scams, so one fake listing in a paid digest could be fatal; employer verification and a public scam watch are brand insurance. Ninth, a free quasi-competitor in 3MTT, which is also the best partner, since its 390,000 selected fellows and weak placement record are exactly the users this product converts into matches.

The metrics to track map one-to-one to the funnel and the risks. Activation: share of sign-ups completing a CV and an alert preference in session one, and OTP delivery rate by carrier. Engagement: digest open and click rates by channel, weekly active share, and 30-day return rate. Quality: digest precision (share of recommended jobs the user marks relevant or applies to), listing freshness (share expired at send time), scam reports per 1,000 listings, and Gmail Postmaster spam rate under 0.1%. Monetisation: free-to-paid by tier, pass-to-subscription upgrade rate, ARPU in naira, involuntary versus voluntary churn, dunning recovery rate, and employer and affiliate revenue share. Cost: variable cost per active user split into job-data API, LLM, email and WhatsApp, with the per-user figure held under ₦400. Outcome: the published quarterly shortlist rate (share of active users reporting at least one interview), learning-path completion by module, and fit-score lift after course completion. Growth: blended CAC per activated user, referral share of sign-ups, and campus and NYSC cohort conversion.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the product from "AI builds your CV" to "a trusted layer between Nigerian job seekers and a fragmented, scam-ridden market", and that reframing changes what gets built first, what gets charged for, and who else pays. The document is the hook; the durable value is fit-scored, verified, fresh matches with an explanation attached, and a learning path that measurably moves the fit score. Pricing follows payment mechanics as much as affordability: ₦1,999 is not a round number but the largest charge that keeps Paystack at 1.5%, sits in the band Spotify and YouTube have proven Nigerians will pay monthly, and undercuts Jobberman's weekly pass. Because conversion will be low, the business is three revenue lines from the start, and the institutional line (state employability programmes, 3MTT, employers) is what has kept every surviving Nigerian career or edtech company alive.

The sharpest implication is sequencing. Everything downstream depends on two facts the research could not establish: whether unemployed Nigerians will pay anything for job help, and whether a weekly email actually gets opened by a cohort that lives on WhatsApp. Both can be tested in eight weeks with a spreadsheet, an LLM and Paystack before a line of platform code is written, and the naming, registration and compliance work can run in parallel. If the pilot clears a 10% pre-pay rate and a 40% open rate, the architecture here is cheap enough to serve 100,000 users for a few thousand dollars a month; if it does not, the founder should pivot to the B2G and employer-paid version of the same engine rather than spend on consumer acquisition.
