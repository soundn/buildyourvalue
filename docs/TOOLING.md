A two-person team can ship a 2026-grade AI career platform for Nigeria on a deliberately small stack: three Claude tiers (Haiku 5.5 for the weekly digest, scam triage and verification passes at **$0.10/$0.50 per million tokens**; Sonnet 5.5 for CV parsing, tailoring and interview grading at **$2/$10**; Opus 5.5 reserved for the coach agent and eval judging) ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), fetched), one Supabase Postgres in London carrying pgvector `halfvec` embeddings, full-text search and ESCO skills, and the Next.js 16 + Hono workspace the repo already has, with Drizzle (ADR-0004). The AI bill is not the constraint: a batched Haiku digest costs on the order of **$0.002 per user per week** and a Sonnet CV tailoring about **three cents**, so even 50,000 weekly users would spend roughly $90 a week on matching (author's estimates from the fetched price list). The feature bar is set by a converged global bundle (JD tailoring with an honest keyword score, a stage-based tracker, explainable "why you match" that LinkedIn now gives away free, JD-generated mock interviews) that must be delivered WhatsApp-adjacent, under 150 KB of JavaScript and inside a trust layer, because fee-demanding "recruiters" are Nigeria's dominant 2026 scam and no incumbent publishes verified outcomes. On the tooling side the team should enable only the official Supabase, Sentry, PostHog and Playwright plugins from its own catalog plus `typescript-lsp`, `security-guidance` and Context7 from Anthropic's marketplace, keep the five project skills now written (`/add-endpoint`, `/new-migration`, `/email-template`, `/adr`, `/release`), commit a `.claude/settings.json` with `.env` deny rules and a typecheck hook, and skip the six unverifiable community plugins in the catalog until their hooks and MCP configs have been read. The run-rate is about **$200/month** for two Claude Max 5x seats this week, roughly **$270–330/month** through Phase 0, and **$450–700/month** once the Phase 1 MVP is live with 1,000 users (author's estimate; every non-Anthropic price below is from a search snippet because the research session could not open any vendor pricing page directly). Throughout, "fetched" marks facts read from an official page, "snippet" marks facts taken from search-result extracts, and "estimate" marks the author's own arithmetic or judgement.

## The 2026 feature bar is a converged bundle; Nigeria rewards trust, not auto-apply

Every leading CV tool now ships the same six things, and reviewers have learned which ones they value. Teal's stage-based tracker (Bookmarked, Applying, Interviewing, Accepted) is "the tracker that wins me each time" in user reviews and sits on the category's most generous free tier, with unlimited resume versions and tracked applications without a card ([Resumly, Teal review](https://resumly.ai/answers/teal-review), snippet; [Jobscan vs Teal](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/jobscan-vs-teal/), snippet). Jobscan's ATS score is the most thorough keyword matcher but its own documentation describes the match rate as a visualisation of keyword overlap, and no one has shown a higher score produces more interviews ([LandThisJob comparison](https://landthisjob.com/blog/jobscan-vs-teal-vs-resumeworded-comparison/), snippet). LinkedIn made explainable matching free in January 2025: Job Match compares a profile against a posting's required and preferred qualifications and shows the summary only to the candidate, so users now expect "why you match / what's missing" at no charge ([The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/linkedins-new-ai-job-match-tool/), snippet). On the interview side the benchmark is Big Interview's 16-dimension rubric with bronze/silver/gold progress markers and Final Round AI's JD-generated question sets with STAR feedback ([Final Round AI, Big Interview review](https://www.finalroundai.com/blog/big-interview-review-pros-cons), snippet; [Final Round AI blog](https://www.finalroundai.com/blog/best-ai-mock-interview), snippet), while Yoodli's delivery-only feedback is criticised for not evaluating "the quality or strategy of what you are saying" ([Interview Drills](https://interviewdrills.com/blog/best-ai-mock-interview-tools), snippet).

The loudest complaints tell the team what not to copy. Billing friction is the single most common one-star driver: **11 of 93** Teal Trustpilot reviews were one-star as of March 2026, mostly users charged after cancelling ([Resumly](https://resumly.ai/answers/teal-review), snippet), and LazyApply's full auto-apply sits in the low 2s on Trustpilot with **25% of 105 reviews** citing refund or cancellation failures ([LoopCV](https://blog.loopcv.pro/lazyapply-review/), snippet). Alert spam is the other: LinkedIn is "among the most aggressive email senders", some users report **10–20 emails a day**, and there is no single off switch ([Mailstrom](https://mailstrom.co/articles/how-to-unsubscribe-from-linkedin-emails/), snippet). For a WhatsApp-heavy market where Meta bills per message and offers no per-category unsubscribe, a self-imposed once-daily digest with dedupe, expiry and honoured STOP replies is a design constraint, not a nicety; PLAN.md's email-first digest and WhatsApp-only-in-Pro decision follows from the same arithmetic (PLAN.md).

Nigeria shifts the bar in three ways. First, trust: Jobberman verifies employers by Tax ID or Certificate of Incorporation and tags only directly received jobs as "recommended" while the rest is sourced from the internet ([Jobberman help centre](https://help-center.jobberman.com/portal/en/kb/articles/all-you-need-to-know-about-the-job-listings-on-jobberman), snippet); LinkedIn's 2026 Safety Pulse finds scammers twice as likely to ask to move off-platform and has required recruiter employer verification since September 2025 ([LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/LinkedIn-Job-Search-Safety-Pulse), snippet; [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/linkedin-outlines-measures-to-combat-scam-job-listings/811042/), snippet). Second, the delivery channel: about six in ten Nigerians remained offline in 2024, rural smartphone ownership is **39% versus 73% urban**, and Nigerians spend over 9% of monthly income on 1 GB ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/?p=167676), snippet; [Technology Times](https://technologytimes.ng/gsma-flags-need-to-close-mobile-internet-gap/), snippet), which is why WhatsApp-first job services (Aremu, WorqNow, My Job Concierge's daily channels for 24+ countries) are appearing ([My Job Concierge](https://gn.myjobconcierge.com/channels/), snippet). Third, the employer anchor: Jobberman's 2025 brochure sells a **₦25,000 Job Boost** and a **₦100,000** "shortlist of 3 fit-for-hire candidates by an HR expert within 10 working days" ([Jobberman brochure 2025](https://static.jobberman.com/Jobberman_Nigeria_Product_Brochure_2025.pdf), snippet), which sets the ceiling for an SME shortlist product and matches PLAN.md's ₦15,000–30,000 employer tier (PLAN.md).

Two cautions on this table. Instrument the tracker so that number is computed automatically from day one. And the researchers found no evidence that gamification or streaks move job-seeker outcomes, so treat those as experiments rather than roadmap items.

## Three Claude tiers plus pgvector cover every AI capability without custom ML

The AI layer should be Claude-native and Postgres-resident. Claude's PDF path converts each page to an image and extracts the text alongside it, accepts up to 32 MB and 100 pages per request within a sub-1M context, and bills each page at roughly 1,500–3,000 tokens with no PDF surcharge ([Anthropic PDF support](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/pdf-support), fetched). That means scanned and photographed CVs, the common Nigerian case, need no separate OCR vendor. DOCX is not accepted as a document block ("convert them to text or PDF first"), so a `mammoth` or LibreOffice step sits in front ([same](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/pdf-support), fetched). Structured outputs through `client.messages.parse()` with `zodOutputFormat(schema)` validate the response against a Zod schema, with the constraints that schemas cannot be recursive, cannot carry `minimum`/`maxLength` style bounds, must set `additionalProperties: false`, and that changing the output format invalidates the prompt cache for that thread ([Anthropic structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs), fetched). Prefill and forced `tool_choice` are gone on the 5.5 models; structured outputs are the replacement ([Anthropic migration guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/migration-guide), from the bundled `claude-api` skill). A 2-page CV at roughly 5–8k input tokens therefore costs well under a cent on Haiku 5.5 and about **$0.01–0.02 on Sonnet 5.5** (estimate from the fetched price list), against commercial parsers whose floors third parties put at **$800 per month** for Affinda and $99 per month for Textkernel ([FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/affinda-resume-parser), snippet; [JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/textkernel-review), snippet). JSON Resume, MIT-licensed with validation tooling, is the right canonical schema, extended with ESCO URIs and Nigerian fields (NYSC status, HND/OND, ICAN/COREN) as PLAN.md already proposes ([jsonresume.org](https://jsonresume.org/schema), snippet; PLAN.md).

Generation should produce structured JSON that the team renders itself. Anthropic's `docx` and `pdf` Agent Skills work through the Messages API with a code-execution container, but containers retain data for 30 days, are not ZDR-eligible, and execution time is billed once the free 1,550 hours are exhausted at $0.05 per container-hour ([Agent Skills overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/overview), fetched; [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), fetched). A single HTML template rendered to PDF by Playwright's headless Chromium, which is how the 28.8k-star Apache-2.0 Resume-Matcher does it, plus the MIT `docx` library for Word output, is deterministic, ATS-controllable and free ([Resume-Matcher](https://github.com/srbhr/Resume-Matcher), fetched; [docx](https://github.com/dolanmiu/docx), fetched). The guardrail that matters most is provenance: a 2026 study of automated hiring pipelines found the fully automated baseline **invented candidate names in 6 of 60 outputs** and frames prompt instructions as the cheapest but weakest mitigation ([arXiv 2608.26171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26171), snippet). The layered answer is a schema-level `evidence[]` field on every bullet pointing at master-profile item IDs, a `[ADD METRIC]` placeholder rule, a Haiku 5.5 support-check pass, and a UI diff the user confirms before export; fabrication rate becomes the headline safety metric with a target of zero (estimate, synthesising the fetched structured-outputs constraints with the cited research).

Matching is a three-stage pipeline the field has converged on: hard SQL filters, vector recall, LLM rerank. pgvector 0.8.x stores `halfvec` at 2 bytes per dimension, indexes HNSW up to 4,000 dimensions, defaults to `m=16, ef_construction=64`, fixes filtered-query under-fill with `hnsw.iterative_scan = relaxed_order`, and documents hybrid search with Postgres full-text fused by Reciprocal Rank Fusion ([pgvector](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector), fetched). Neon's benchmark found `halfvec` halves storage with equivalent recall ([Neon](https://neon.com/blog/dont-use-vector-use-halvec-instead-and-save-50-of-your-storage-cost), snippet). Embed Claude-produced structured summaries, not raw text, and keep the `tsvector` leg so exact Nigerian tokens ("NYSC", "ICAN", "Ikeja") are not blurred by the embedding. For the embedding model itself the research recommends Voyage `voyage-4-lite` at **$0.02 per million tokens** with 200M free tokens, or OpenAI `text-embedding-3-large` at $0.13 ([usagepricing](https://usagepricing.com/blueprint/voyage-ai), snippet; [OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/inconsistent-pricing-for-text-embedding-3-large-between-model-card-and-pricing-page/1338277), snippet); PLAN.md's `text-embedding-3-small` at $0.02 is an equally cheap choice and the embedding column is rebuildable, so this is a low-stakes decision (PLAN.md). The rerank should be one Claude structured call scoring 30 finalists with a reason and gap list each, because that single call doubles as the product's explanation; Cohere Rerank at roughly $2 per thousand 100-document searches is an optional middle stage only if recall widens to hundreds ([MemberJunction docs](https://docs.memberjunction.org/api/classes/_memberjunction_search-engine.CohereReRanker.html), snippet). Evaluate with a 300–500 pair human-labelled set and a Sonnet/Opus judge validated by Cohen's κ, since LLM judges agree with humans **85–89% of the time on nDCG@10** in product search and never flipped a significant ranker comparison ([arXiv 2406.00247](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00247), snippet).

The weekly digest is where cost compounds, and the arithmetic is reassuring. Haiku 5.5 bills $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens on prompts under 100k, the Batch API halves both input and output, and caching discounts stack with batch ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), fetched; [Batch processing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/batch-processing), fetched). Assuming 30 reranked jobs at ~600 tokens each plus profile and rubric (≈21k input, ≈3k output), a per-user digest costs about **$0.0036 on Haiku 5.5 standard, $0.0018 batched, $0.036 batched Sonnet, $0.072 batched Opus**; at 50,000 weekly users on batched Haiku the bill is roughly $90 a week (estimate; token counts are assumptions to verify with `countTokens`). Keep Haiku prompts under 100k tokens to avoid the 5x long-context rate, submit the batch Sunday night and consume Monday, and never use Fable 5.1 for product traffic because it is a Covered Model requiring 30-day retention at $10/$50 ([API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention), fetched).

Per-user AI cost rolls up as follows (author's estimate from the figures above): a free user on the throttled path (four batched Haiku digests plus one parse) costs about **$0.02 a month**; a Core subscriber with five Sonnet tailorings, a parse and four digests about **$0.17**; a Pro subscriber adding two interview sessions about **$0.25–0.30**. Those figures are AI only (they exclude the job-data API, email and background jobs); they sit inside PLAN.md's $0.10–0.30 per registered user all-in and confirm its conclusion that WhatsApp marketing templates, not AI, are the cost line that can break the ₦1,999 margin (PLAN.md). ## The application stack stays as planned, with London Postgres, Lagos edge and Nigerian rails

The repo's existing choices survive scrutiny. Next.js 16 shipped on 21 October 2025 with Turbopack as the stable default bundler, Cache Components built on Partial Pre-Rendering, and `middleware.ts` renamed to `proxy.ts` ([Next.js 16 blog](https://nextjs.org/blog/next-16), snippet of vendor page). Hono measures within 1% of Fastify on Node 24, offers a typed `hc` client with zero codegen and is the only mainstream framework with native edge runtime support ([techplained](https://www.techplained.com/hono-vs-express-vs-fastify), snippet), and `@hono/zod-openapi` generates OpenAPI 3.1 from the same Zod schemas that live in `core` ([@hono/zod-openapi README](https://github.com/honojs/middleware/blob/main/packages/zod-openapi/README.md), fetched). Drizzle adds almost no overhead over the raw driver and fits SQL-first pgvector and RLS work, though its 1.0 is still in beta so versions must be pinned ([makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/md/tutorials/drizzle-vs-prisma), snippet). The one component that needs testing before commitment is the service worker: a Next.js 16 PWA template reports that Serwist is disabled in development because of Turbopack compatibility, other guides say it works natively in production or needs `@serwist/turbopack`, and the sources conflict ([startwiseph/next-pwa](https://github.com/startwiseph/next-pwa), snippet). Plan for a production-only service worker and test offline mode with `next dev --webpack`.

Nigeria's network reality turns bundle size into a product feature. Statcounter puts Android at about **82%** of Nigerian mobile OS share with Android 11–13 the largest versions ([Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/nigeria), snippet), browser-measured real-world medians are around **5 Mbps** despite Ookla's 44 Mbps national figure ([speedof.me](https://speedof.me/internet-speed/nigeria), snippet), and the January 2025 tariff approval pushed the average 1 GB bundle from ₦287.5 to **₦475** ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/?p=949775), snippet). Enforce web.dev's sample budget of 125 KB total JS and 300 KB page weight through `lighthouse --budget-path` in CI ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/use-lighthouse-for-performance-budgets?hl=pl), snippet), ship shadcn/ui on Radix rather than Base UI until the reported Vercel AI Elements type conflicts settle ([Vercel plugin shadcn skill](https://mcpservers.org/tr/agent-skills/vercel/vercel-plugin/shadcn-2), snippet), and implement Pidgin as `next-intl` message variants under an `en-NG` locale, since no platform offers Pidgin as a locale (estimate).

Supabase's documented regions include London (eu-west-2) but no Lagos or Cape Town region ([Supabase regions](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions), snippet of vendor page), while Cloudflare has served from Lagos since 2018 and AWS opened a Lagos Local Zone in 2023 ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/lagos), snippet; [AWS](https://go.aws/3XZcGZn), snippet). The sensible topology is therefore Cloudflare free in front for the Lagos PoP, Vercel functions co-located with the London database rather than in Cape Town because most requests are DB-bound, and aggressive client caching to hide the ~100 ms round trip (estimate; no Lagos→London latency measurement was found). Phone OTP cannot use Supabase's native providers (Twilio, MessageBird, Vonage, TextLocal) cost-effectively; the Send SMS Hook exists precisely for "a regional SMS provider" ([Send SMS Hook](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/send-sms-hook.md), snippet of vendor page), and Termii's DND route is required to reach DND-registered numbers while the generic route is blocked 8 pm–8 am ([Termii DND blog](https://blog.termii.com/the-dnd-service-in-nigeria-everything-you-need-to-know), snippet). Register the sender ID early: Africa's Talking describes telco whitelisting of two weeks or more ([Africa's Talking](https://help.africastalking.com/en/articles/2241664-does-africa-s-talking-support-sending-of-sms-to-dnd-numbers-in-nigeria), snippet). Keep WhatsApp and email OTP as fallbacks, as PLAN.md's onboarding already specifies (PLAN.md).

Payments confirm PLAN.md's pricing logic. Paystack's local card fee is 1.5% + ₦100 capped at ₦2,000 with the ₦100 waived under ₦2,500 ([kolonell](https://kolonell.com/en/blog/paystack-transaction-fees-breakdown-nigeria-2026), snippet), subscriptions support card and Nigeria Direct Debit only ([Paystack subscriptions](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/), snippet of vendor page), webhooks are signed with HMAC-SHA512 in `x-paystack-signature` ([Paystack webhooks](https://paystack.com/docs/payments/webhooks/), snippet of vendor page), and 99% of Pay-with-Transfer payments confirm within 8 seconds ([Paystack-Titan](https://paystack.com/blog/product-engineering/paystack-titan), snippet of vendor page). None of the community Node SDKs is official, so wrap the REST API in a thin Zod-validated client in `core` and own dunning yourself with retries on days 1, 3 and 7 (estimate). Messaging follows the same pattern of avoiding fixed fees: Resend's free tier is 3,000 emails a month and Pro is $20 for 50,000 ([Resend pricing](https://resend.com/pricing.md), snippet of vendor page), which makes it the lower-friction choice over SES for a team without an AWS footprint; Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender rules require SPF, DKIM, DMARC, RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe and a spam rate under 0.3% ([Bird](https://bird.com/explained/deliverability/what-are-the-gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sender-requirements), snippet). WhatsApp went per-message on 1 July 2025 ([setsmart](https://setsmart.io/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing), snippet), Nigerian utility rates are quoted between **$0.0067 and $0.0101** and marketing between **$0.03 and $0.06** by vendors who disagree by up to 2x ([ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/nigeria/), snippet; [Daily Trust](https://dailytrust.com/?p=1946166), snippet), and a single vendor reports a new charge on service replies from 1 October 2026 after 1,000 free per number ([MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/whatsapp-to-charge-businesses-for-customer-replies-from-october-2026/), snippet). Use the Meta Cloud API directly rather than paying 360dialog's $49 per number ([360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/360dialog/prices-plans-and-payment-options), snippet of vendor page), and keep all job alerts in strictly non-promotional utility templates, since mixed content is reclassified as marketing ([YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/blog/whatsapp-api-message-template-category-guidelines-update), snippet).

The project configuration should be committed; the repo now has `.claude/settings.json`, hooks, five skills and a root `CLAUDE.md`, and the path-scoped rules below are still to add. The docs recommend a root `CLAUDE.md` under 200 lines holding build commands, layout and "always do X" rules, with multi-step procedures moved into skills and path-scoped `.claude/rules/*.md` files that load only when matching files are touched ([Claude Code memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory), fetched). For this workspace that means a root `CLAUDE.md` (~100 lines) plus `.claude/rules/api.md` (`paths: ["api/**"]`), `.claude/rules/web.md` (`frontend/**`, `admin/**`), `.claude/rules/core.md` (`core/**`) and `.claude/rules/db.md` for migrations (author's mapping of the documented pattern onto the actual `core`/`api`/`frontend`/`admin` layout). Hooks give enforcement that CLAUDE.md cannot: the docs' canonical example is a `PostToolUse` hook on `Write|Edit` that runs lint and `tsc --noEmit` and exits 2 so "stderr is the reason fed back to Claude", noting that "Exit 1 is not a block" and hooks are fail-open unless `onFailure: "block"` is set ([Hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks), fetched). Add the documented `PreToolUse` Bash hook that filters test output to failures, which cut output "from tens of thousands of tokens to hundreds" ([Manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs), fetched), and a `SessionStart` hook that runs `npm ci` so cloud sessions can test (the `session-start-hook` skill in the session exists for exactly this). Permissions evaluate "deny, then ask, then allow"; `Bash(npm run *)` covers `npm run build` but not `npm install`, `npx` is not stripped so must be listed explicitly, and the documented secret-protection pattern is `Read(./.env)`, `Read(./.env.*)` and `Read(./secrets/**)` deny rules ([Configure permissions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions), fetched). The committed `.claude/settings.json` should allow `Bash(npm run *)`, `Bash(npx vitest *)`, `Bash(npx tsc *)`, `Bash(npx biome *)`, `Bash(git diff *)`, `Bash(gh pr *)` and `Bash(supabase db diff *)`, deny `Read(./.env)`, `Read(./.env.*)`, `Bash(curl *)`, `Bash(wget *)` and `Bash(git push --force *)`, and route documentation fetches through `WebFetch(domain:...)`, because argument-constrained Bash patterns "are fragile" ([same](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions), fetched).

The security rules for AI-assisted development follow from one principle: everything Claude reads is untrusted input. The security docs state that WebFetch "runs a separate model call over the page, and Claude receives that call's answer instead of the raw page", that `curl` and `wget` are not auto-approved, and that "no system is completely immune" ([Security](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/security), fetched). The realistic vectors here are third-party PR bodies feeding the GitHub Action, dependency READMEs, the job-listing pages the verification agent fetches, and user-generated rows returned by the Supabase MCP. The Action's own security guide says config files are restored from the base branch but `package.json` and lockfiles are not, so hooks that run package scripts "can therefore execute PR-supplied code" and tools must be invoked directly with pinned versions; it also forbids PATs because they "could be recovered over time through prompt injection" ([claude-code-action security](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code-action/blob/main/docs/security.md), fetched). In headless `claude -p` runs and Agent SDK sessions, `.mcp.json` servers load without a prompt, so keep write-capable MCPs out of that file or list them under `disabledMcpjsonServers` ([MCP docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/mcp), fetched). In cloud sessions use network secrets, which the proxy adds "after each request leaves the session's VM, so the key itself stays outside the VM", rather than environment variables readable by anyone using the environment ([Configure cloud environments](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments), fetched). And because AI-written SQL is the highest-impact failure mode for a platform holding CVs and payment records, every migration goes through a PR with `supabase db diff` output attached and a Vitest project that exercises RLS as anon, authenticated and service role against a local Supabase (author's rule, built on the fetched skill scope and permission docs). The listing-verification agent, if built on the Agent SDK, runs with `maxBudgetUsd`, `maxTurns`, `disallowedTools` and domain allowlists and emits a verdict a human or deterministic check gates ([Agent SDK TypeScript reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/typescript), fetched).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which of fifty tools should we adopt" to "how few can we get away with", and the answer is smaller than the planned stack already is. Three Claude tiers behind one Zod-typed interface in `core`, one Postgres that doubles as vector store, queue, cron and skills taxonomy, and seven plugins replace what would have been a dozen vendors and custom ML; the AI line costs less than the two developers' Claude subscriptions until the platform passes several thousand users. What the state of the art adds in 2026 is not a new component but a discipline: structured outputs with provenance instead of free text, batch-plus-cache economics designed in from the first digest, evals with human-labelled Nigerian gold sets gating every prompt change, and a Claude Code configuration where `.env` denies, exit-2 hooks and read-only MCP refs are committed to the repo rather than remembered.

The implication for sequencing is that the tooling work is a one-week task that should precede any plugin enabling, and the eval-labelling work is a Phase 0 task that should run alongside the paid pilot PLAN.md already prescribes, because without Nigerian labels the team cannot tell a better reranker from a worse one or a Nigerian-accent STT failure from a product failure. The differentiators the feature benchmark surfaced, guaranteed feedback on every application, job-linked micro-lessons, a published interview-conversion rate, a verified WhatsApp identity with a public scam counter, all depend on the same instrumentation and the same trust posture, and none requires technology the team does not already have access to. The constraint is not capability; it is the willingness to publish the number.
